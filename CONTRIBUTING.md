# Contributing to The Garden Project Curricula

Thank you for contributing to The Garden Project Curricula.

This repository contains Bible studies, leader guides, workshops, formation practices, teaching frameworks, and supporting resources. Contributions should help the curricula become more biblically faithful, Christ-centered, formation-oriented, clear, and usable.

## Curriculum Design Principles

Every contribution should respect three foundational principles:

### 1. Biblically Outlined

The structure and claims of a curriculum should arise from the biblical text, its literary context, and the broader storyline of Scripture.

### 2. Christ-Centered

The curricula should show how Scripture finds its unity and fulfillment in Jesus Christ without forcing artificial connections or ignoring the original context.

### 3. Formation-Oriented

The purpose is not merely to transfer information. The curricula should help participants learn to see, love, trust, follow, and embody the way of Jesus.

## Helpful Contributions

Contributions may include:

* Spelling, grammar, and formatting corrections.
* Clearer teaching explanations.
* Stronger discussion questions.
* Improved leader instructions.
* Biblical and theological feedback.
* Formation exercises and practices.
* Accessibility improvements.
* Reading recommendations.
* Visual or structural improvements.
* Feedback from pilot groups.
* Translation proposals.
* Corrections to citations, references, or links.

## Changes Requiring Prior Discussion

Open an issue or contact the maintainer before making substantial changes to:

* The theological framework of a curriculum.
* The overall curriculum sequence.
* Session structure or learning outcomes.
* Major biblical interpretations.
* Christological conclusions.
* Formation philosophy.
* Curriculum names, branding, or audience.
* Publication-ready editions.
* Translation projects.
* Licensing or copyright notices.

Editorial corrections and small clarity improvements generally do not require prior discussion.

## Working with Biblical and Academic Sources

When contributing theological or research content:

* Cite the biblical passages being discussed.
* Distinguish the text’s direct claims from theological inference.
* Identify significant interpretive alternatives when relevant.
* Cite academic or published sources rather than copying them.
* Use quotations sparingly.
* Verify quotations, page numbers, and bibliographic information.
* Do not upload copyrighted books, articles, or scans without permission.

## Writing Standards

Aim for writing that is:

* Clear without being shallow.
* Theologically substantive without unnecessary jargon.
* Pastoral rather than merely academic.
* Appropriate for the intended learning level.
* Consistent with the voice of the surrounding curriculum.
* Respectful of participants from different stages of faith.

Preserve recurring terminology and frameworks unless a change has been approved.

## File and Version Standards

Use:

* Markdown for text-based curriculum files whenever practical.
* Clear file and folder names.
* Relative links within the repository.
* ISO dates: `YYYY-MM-DD`.
* Explicit version or status labels.

Recommended status labels:

```text
Draft
Working Draft
Pilot
Under Review
Approved
Published
Archived
```

Do not overwrite a published edition with an experimental draft. Create a new version or development branch.

## Privacy and Copyright

Never commit:

* Participant names or contact information.
* Private group feedback that identifies individuals.
* Pastoral or counseling information.
* Unlicensed images or illustrations.
* Complete copyrighted articles or book chapters.
* Passwords, tokens, API keys, or credentials.

Pilot feedback should be anonymized before inclusion.

## Suggested Git Workflow

1. Create a branch for the contribution.
2. Make one focused set of changes.
3. Review the files and links.
4. Commit with a descriptive message.
5. Open a pull request.

Example branch names:

```text
edit/planted-session-2
feat/god-with-us-leader-guide
fix/scripture-reference
research/prophets-reading-list
```

Example commit messages:

```text
docs(planted): clarify gospel storyline section
feat(temple): add leader discussion prompts
fix(prophets): correct Jeremiah reference
research(covenant): expand recommended reading
```

## Pull Request Description

Explain:

* What you changed.
* Why the change is helpful.
* Which curriculum and session are affected.
* Whether the change alters theology, structure, or learning outcomes.
* Whether the contribution has been tested in a group.
* Which sources support significant theological claims.

## Review Criteria

Contributions will be evaluated for:

* Biblical faithfulness.
* Responsible interpretation.
* Christ-centered integration.
* Formation value.
* Theological coherence.
* Clarity and accessibility.
* Consistency with the curriculum’s intended level.
* Practical usefulness for leaders and participants.
* Copyright and privacy compliance.

Submission does not guarantee acceptance.

## Contributor Agreement

By submitting a contribution, you confirm that:

* The work is your own or you have permission to submit it.
* It does not knowingly violate copyright, confidentiality, or privacy.
* You have clearly identified third-party material.
* Paul Canaan and The Garden Project may edit, publish, translate, reproduce, and distribute the accepted contribution as part of this repository and related curriculum editions.
* Your contribution may remain publicly visible in the repository history.

Thank you for helping create resources that are rooted in Scripture, centered on Christ, and cultivated for formation.
