# Workshops MOC

## Orientation

A Garden Project workshop is a bounded formation experience that helps people practice or inhabit one dimension of the biblical Story. Workshops integrate Scripture, teaching, reflection, programs, prayer, community, and response around a focused governing question.

They are larger than a single program and more focused than a sustained curriculum.

## Workshop roles

| Role | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance | Gives participants an interpretive doorway | A future Five Echoes workshop; often served by a program such as [[Building A House]] |
| Vision | Enlarges the horizon of a curriculum | [[Eternity Planted]] |
| Focused practice | Rehearses one biblical movement | [[The Exodus Road Workshop]] |
| Capstone | Integrates and embodies a curriculum's theological destination | [[The Church Workshop]] |
| Commissioning | Sends participants toward vocation and mission | [[From Planted to Plant]] |

Standalone workshops remain connected to [[The Garden Project Ministry MOC|the ministry's mission]] and may later contribute to curricula without losing their independent purpose.

## Shared design DNA

Every workshop is:

1. **Biblically outlined** — its arc emerges from Scripture and the Story.
2. **Christ-centered** — interpretation and response converge on Jesus.
3. **Formation-oriented** — participants practice a new way of seeing, loving, belonging, acting, or witnessing.

Each also needs a governing question, no more than three primary outcomes, a timed arc, accessible participation, pastoral and safety boundaries, prayer and integration, provenance, and a clear relationship to any source curriculum.

## Relationship to other artifacts

- [[Programs MOC|Programs]] contribute one memorable experience; they do not carry the whole workshop.
- [[Frameworks MOC|Frameworks]] organize insight; they do not replace Scripture or practice.
- [[Curricula MOC|Curricula]] provide sustained journeys; featured workshops enlarge or culminate them.
- [[00 CoJourner Program|CoJourners]] help participants connect a workshop to relationship, church, practice, and ongoing accompaniment.
