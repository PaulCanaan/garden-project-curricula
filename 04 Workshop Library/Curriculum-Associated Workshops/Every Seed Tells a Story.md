# Every Seed Tells a Story

A featured workshop accompanying [[00 Planted|Planted.]] that teaches participants to trace biblical patterns toward Christ using [[Seed-Form Typology]].

> Imported from Notion on 2026-10-05 without editorial changes. Review notes:
> - The "Planted. Storyline Review" below lists a **12-session** sequence (The Seed and the Soil → Every Seed Tells a Story). [[00 Planted]] in this repository uses a five-chapter structure. Reconcile them in the Canonical Decision Log.
> - Its six-step method (Seed → Pattern → Growth → Fulfillment → Participation → Harvest) should be checked against the canonical [[Seed-Form Typology]] framework note.

## Workshop Purpose

To help participants recognize recurring biblical patterns and trace how earlier people, events, places, and images develop through Scripture, find fulfillment in Jesus, shape the life of God's people, and point toward new creation.

By the end of the workshop, participants should be able to:

- read a passage within the larger biblical story;
- recognize a recurring biblical pattern;
- distinguish typology from arbitrary symbolism;
- trace a pattern toward Christ;
- apply the method to a new biblical theme.

## Workshop Introduction

*A seed is small, but it contains a future form within it. The mature plant is not visible yet, but its identity and direction are already present.*

Biblical patterns often work the same way. God plants an earlier reality in history, allows it to recur and develop, and then reveals its fuller meaning in Christ.

> **Every seed tells a story, and the biblical Story grows toward Jesus.**

### Simple definitions

**Biblical theology** reads the Bible as one unfolding story from creation to new creation.

**Typology** recognizes earlier historical realities that establish patterns later developed and fulfilled more fully.

**Seed-form typology** describes how a biblical reality may appear first in a smaller, incomplete form and later grow toward greater fulfillment.

---

## Workshop Framework: Seed-Form Typology

*Seed → Pattern → Growth → Fulfillment → Participation → Harvest*

### 1. Seed — What was planted?

Identify the earlier person, event, promise, place, object, or institution.

Examples:

- Adam;
- garden;
- exodus;
- temple;
- Davidic king;
- seed;
- vine;
- exile.

### 2. Pattern — What shape takes root?

Ask what theological pattern the earlier reality establishes.

Examples:

- creation and vocation;
- rebellion and exile;
- deliverance through judgment;
- God dwelling with humanity;
- suffering followed by glory;
- death producing life.

### 3. Growth — How does the pattern develop?

Look for repetition, expansion, failure, prophetic reuse, and growing expectation.

Ask:

- Where does it appear again?
- What remains unresolved?
- How does it become larger or clearer?

### 4. Fulfillment — How does Jesus fulfill it?

Ask what Jesus:

- embodies;
- completes;
- reverses;
- transforms;
- brings to its climax.

### 5. Participation — How do God's people share in it?

Ask how union with Christ shapes:

- the church;
- discipleship;
- worship;
- mission;
- hope.

### 6. Harvest — Where is the pattern going?

Trace the pattern toward its final completion in new creation.

Prepare cards with the following images:

- seed;
- soil;
- garden;
- tree;
- thorns;
- vine;
- vineyard;
- fruit;
- wilderness;
- harvest;

- Psalm 80;
- Isaiah 5;
- Isaiah 11;
- Matthew 13;
- John 12:24;
- John 15;
- 1 Corinthians 3;
- 1 Corinthians 15;
- Revelation 22.

### Instructions

1. Match the passage cards with related images.
2. Arrange related passages in biblical-story order.
3. Identify what remains continuous.
4. Notice how the image changes or expands.
5. Identify where Jesus becomes the center of the pattern.
6. Trace the pattern toward the church and new creation.

### Discussion

- Which images recur most often?
- Which patterns begin in Genesis?
- Where do the patterns appear to fail?
- Which expectations remain unresolved until Jesus?
- How does the New Testament reinterpret earlier images?
- Where does the pattern finally lead?

## Workshop Demonstration — The Seed Pattern

### Seed of the woman

**Genesis 3:15**

A promised human offspring will suffer conflict with the serpent and ultimately defeat him.

### Seed of Abraham

**Genesis 12:1–3; 15:1–6**

The promise narrows into a family through whom blessing will reach the nations.

### Israel as planted people

Israel is brought into a garden-like land and called to bear covenant fruit, yet repeatedly becomes unfaithful and enters exile.

### Jesus as the Seed

Jesus gathers the pattern into himself:

- offspring of the woman;
- offspring of Abraham;
- faithful Israelite;
- Davidic Messiah;
- grain of wheat that dies.

### Resurrection as harvest

Jesus rises as the firstfruits. His resurrection is the beginning of the coming harvest, not an isolated miracle.

### Church as multiplied seed

Those who belong to Christ participate in his life and mission. The singular seed produces many.

### New creation as final harvest

The story ends with life, fruitfulness, healing, and God dwelling with humanity.

## Workshop Demonstration — The Garden Pattern

### Eden

The garden is the place of:

- divine presence;
- human vocation;
- life;
- trust;
- cultivation.

Human rebellion leads to exile east of Eden.

### Gethsemane

Another representative human stands in a garden and faces a decisive act of obedience.

Adam's posture:

> My will rather than God's.

Jesus' posture:

> Not my will, but yours.

### New Jerusalem

The final garden is greater than Eden:

- garden becomes garden-city;
- one family becomes redeemed nations;
- the river of life flows;
- the tree of life bears continual fruit;
- the nations are healed;
- God dwells openly with humanity.

The story does not simply return to Eden. It grows toward fulfillment.

---

## Workshop Demonstration — The Vine and Vineyard Pattern

### Israel as God's vine

God plants and cares for Israel as his covenant vineyard.

### The unfruitful vineyard

The prophets describe Israel's failure to produce justice, righteousness, and covenant faithfulness.

### Jesus as the true vine

Jesus embodies the faithful and fruitful covenant people.

### Disciples as branches

Fruitfulness comes through abiding in Jesus, not independent effort.

### Church as fruitful community

The people of God bear fruit together through love, obedience, witness, and dependence on Christ.

## Guardrails

### Typology is not arbitrary symbolism

Do not assign secret meanings to every incidental detail.

Look for patterns supported by:

- repeated biblical language;
- similar narrative structures;
- prophetic development;
- covenantal continuity;
- New Testament interpretation;
- Christological fulfillment.

### The earlier text still matters

A type has a real historical meaning before it points forward.

The exodus matters as Israel's deliverance from Egypt before it becomes a pattern for greater salvation.

### Fulfillment includes continuity and escalation

The fulfillment resembles the earlier pattern but exceeds it.

```text
Adam → Christ, the faithful human
Exodus → liberation from sin, death, and exile
Temple → Christ and his Spirit-filled people
David → Jesus, the everlasting King
Garden → garden-city new creation
```

## Workshop Activity 2 — Trace a New Pattern

Divide participants into groups. Assign each group one biblical theme:

- water;
- wilderness;
- mountain;
- shepherd;
- bread;
- temple;
- exodus;
- exile;
- king;
- bride.

Ask each group to trace the theme using six questions:

1. What was planted?
2. What pattern took root?
3. How did it grow?
4. How does Jesus fulfill it?
5. How do God's people participate?
6. What final harvest does it anticipate?

Each group presents its pattern in five minutes.

## Planted. Storyline Review

Use the session titles to reveal the pattern participants have already followed:

1. **The Seed and the Soil** — the kingdom is received like seed.
2. **In the Beginning, a Garden** — God creates a world of life and presence.
3. **Made to Flourish** — humanity is planted with identity and vocation.
4. **East of Eden** — rebellion produces exile, thorns, and death.
5. **The Serpent-Crusher** — the faithful Son confronts humanity's enemy.
6. **A Seed Must Die** — Jesus enters suffering and death.
7. **The Firstfruits Rise** — resurrection begins the final harvest.
8. **The Gospel of the Kingdom** — God's reign grows like seed.
9. **Eternity Planted** — God's future enters the present.
10. **Don't Miss the Forest** — disciples grow together as God's people.
11. **Plant Again** — the fruitful community participates in mission.
12. **Every Seed Tells a Story** — the whole journey reveals one Story centered on Jesus.

### Central synthesis

> The garden, seed, tree, vine, fruit, forest, and harvest are not isolated illustrations. Together they trace God's work from creation, through Christ, into the church, and toward new creation.
