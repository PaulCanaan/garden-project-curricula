# Rooted Through the Seasons

*Grace, Truth, and the Growth God Gives over Time*

A featured workshop accompanying [[00 Planted|Planted.]] that follows the growth of one seed into a mature tree, then lets the picture widen into a forest. It comes after [[Eternity Planted]] and prepares for *Don't Miss the Forest*.

A seed may carry life within it, but it does not become a tree in a moment.

Roots deepen before branches spread. Growth often remains hidden beneath the soil. A young tree passes through rain and drought, sunlight and shadow, pruning and rest. It does not grow by escaping the seasons, but by remaining rooted through them.

After **Eternity Planted**, we have learned to see our lives within God's larger future. Eternity has been planted in the human heart, and eternal life begins now through knowing God in Christ. Yet this eternal life is lived within ordinary time—in days, habits, relationships, trials, and seasons.

This workshop turns from **eternal perspective** toward **spiritual growth**. It follows the development of one seed into a mature tree. Only after seeing that growth clearly will the picture widen: one tree becomes many, and the journey of individual discipleship opens into life together.

## The Growth God Gives

Read **1 Corinthians 3:5–9**.

Paul describes discipleship and ministry through the language of cultivation:

> "I planted the seed, Apollos watered it, but God has been making it grow."

Some plant.
Others water.
Each participates faithfully.
But growth belongs to God.

We cannot force a seed to open, pull a sapling upward, or manufacture spiritual maturity. Growth is gift before it is achievement. At the same time, God invites us to participate—to abide, receive, practice, cultivate, and remain.

Spiritual formation is neither passive waiting nor anxious self-improvement.

> We participate in the conditions of growth, while trusting God to give the growth.

### Key passages

- **1 Corinthians 3:5–9** — God gives the growth
- **John 15:1–8** — fruit comes through abiding in Christ
- **Colossians 2:6–7** — rooted and built up in Christ
- **Psalm 1:1–3** — a tree planted by streams of water
- **Romans 12:1–2** — transformed rather than conformed

## The Grace–Truth Matrix, Part 2

The Grace–Truth Matrix first helped us recognize four spiritual and relational environments.

Truth without grace can become pressure and condemnation.

Grace without truth can become comfort without transformation.

Without grace or truth, people often withdraw into isolation.

Grace and truth together create an environment where we can be honestly known, deeply loved, and faithfully formed.

But the first version of the matrix captures only a moment or a particular relationship. Growth unfolds through another dimension:

### Time

A seed must remain in the soil.
A branch must remain in the vine.
A disciple must continue walking with Jesus.

The deeper growth model is:

### (Grace + Truth) × Time → Growth

This is not a formula that guarantees predictable results. Time alone does not produce maturity, and spiritual growth cannot be reduced to a technique.

It is a picture of the environment in which God ordinarily forms people.

#### Grace over time

Grace gives us room to:

- be honest without hiding;
- fail without being abandoned;
- repent without despair;
- receive forgiveness;
- heal without being rushed;
- begin again.

#### Truth over time

Truth gives growth:

- direction;
- structure;
- boundaries;
- correction;
- wisdom;
- a vision of life in Christ.

#### Time reveals what is taking root

One moment of pressure may be painful. Repeated pressure can form a life of performance.

One moment of indulgence may provide relief. Repeated avoidance can form stagnation.

One season of isolation may seem protective. Prolonged isolation can deepen disconnection.

But when grace and truth remain together through many seasons, God can form:

- rootedness;
- honesty;
- resilience;
- freedom;
- faithfulness;
- Christlike fruit.

The growth model should therefore be spoken in two complementary sentences:

> **Grace and truth cultivate growth over time.**
> **God alone gives the growth.**

## Rooted Against the Current

Growth does not happen in a neutral environment.

The present world carries currents that continually shape our desires and imagination:

- hurry;
- comparison;
- consumption;
- individualism;
- performance;
- fear;
- comfort;
- self-definition;
- the pursuit of power.

The kingdom of Jesus often moves in another direction.

Its citizens live in the world, but they are not formed entirely by the world's currents. They refuse to accept an upside-down world as normal. Through renewed minds, cruciform love, truth, grace, and hope, they learn to embody the right-side-up kingdom wherever they go.

Romans 12:2 describes this kind of formation:

> "Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

A rooted tree does not leave the landscape. It remains within it without being carried away by it.

Its roots reach toward another source.

## The Painting

Throughout *Planted.*, an image has been quietly developing:

*Seed → Cloud → Sprout → Sapling → Tree*

Now the group completes that growth together.

The purpose is not to produce polished artwork. The painting allows participants to see how the story of *Planted.* has unfolded through time.

### The Seed

The seed recalls the beginning:

- created in God's image;
- known and loved;
- planted with purpose;
- carrying life not yet fully visible.

Near the seed, participants might add words such as:

> loved · known · called · image-bearer · possibility · purpose

### The Cloud

The cloud gathers over the seed.

It represents:

- sin;
- alienation;
- shame;
- fear;
- exile;
- suffering;
- spiritual conflict;
- the broken currents of the world.

The seed appears buried in darkness.

And yet the story asks:

> Were you buried—or were you planted?

The difference may not be immediately visible. It is revealed through resurrection and time.

### The Sprout

A small shoot breaks through the soil.

The sprout represents the death and resurrection of Jesus—the moment when apparent defeat becomes the beginning of new creation.

Near the sprout:

> hope · resurrection · breakthrough · new creation · firstfruits

The sprout is small, but it announces that death does not have the final word.

### The Sapling

The sprout becomes a young tree.

This is the stage of real but unfinished discipleship. A sapling is alive and growing, yet still vulnerable. It bends under pressure, needs nourishment, and may require support.

Around the sapling, participants can add:

- rain for grace;
- sunlight for truth;
- seasons for time;
- roots for abiding in Christ;
- pruning for correction and transformation;
- a supporting stake for relationships and encouragement.

The sapling reminds us:

> New life begins in resurrection, but maturity develops through formation.

### The Tree

The sapling becomes a large tree with visible roots, branches, leaves, and fruit.

The roots might contain:

- Christ;
- Scripture;
- prayer;
- identity;
- grace;
- truth.

The trunk may represent:

- character;
- perseverance;
- obedience;
- integrity.

The branches and fruit may represent:

- love;
- joy;
- peace;
- hospitality;
- mercy;
- service;
- witness;
- vocation.

Each participant can draw or paint a tree that represents abundant life to them.

The trees should not all look alike. Some may be tall or small, fruitful or newly budding, straight or weathered. Some may show scars, pruning, winter, or recovery.

## In Time, a Forest

When everyone has finished, place the trees together.

Step back before explaining anything.

What appeared to be many separate pictures becomes one landscape.

A forest.

The forest should be introduced here, but not fully explored yet. This workshop primarily concerns **individual discipleship through time**. The community-level practice of spiritual formation belongs to **[[The Bond]]** and **Don't Miss the Forest**.

For now, let the image create anticipation.

Ask:

- Where is time visible in this painting?
- What growth happened beneath the surface?
- Which stage most resembles your present season?
- What has helped you remain rooted?
- Where have grace and truth shaped you over time?
- What does it mean that God gives the growth?
- What can we cultivate, and what must we entrust to God?
- What changes when the separate trees are placed together?

The final question opens the next movement.

Individual growth is real, but the Christian life is not merely the multiplication of isolated disciples.

Trees share soil.
Roots intertwine.
Young growth receives protection.
Mature trees offer shelter.
A forest becomes an ecosystem.

The older material already summarizes this movement well: individual growth cannot be separated from the whole, and the growth of the whole also depends on its members. The forest is connected by the Spirit's work, mutual love, and a shared purpose and mission.

---

> Grace and truth have nurtured the seed through time. God has given growth. What began as a seed has become a tree—rooted, resilient, and increasingly fruitful.
>
> But when we place our trees together, we discover that spiritual growth was never meant to end with individual maturity. A forest is more than many trees standing near one another. Its life depends on relationships beneath and above the surface.
>
> What binds those lives together? What makes a collection of trees become a living forest?

During the next session, **Don't Miss the Forest**, we can develop the fuller claim:

> Spiritual formation is personal, but never private. God forms disciples through the life, practices, gifts, correction, love, worship, and mission of a people.

## Closing Refrain

> We are planted by grace.
> We are rooted in truth.
> We grow through the seasons.
> God gives the growth.
> And when our trees stand together,
> a forest begins to appear.
