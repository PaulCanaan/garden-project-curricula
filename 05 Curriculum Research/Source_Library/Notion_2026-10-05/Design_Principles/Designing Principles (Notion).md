# Designing Principles

## Example: the design commitments of Planted.

**Biblically outlined.** The curriculum follows the broad storyline of Scripture—creation, rebellion, redemption, new creation, and mission—while introducing participants to recurring biblical-theological themes such as the kingdom of God, God's presence, resurrection, identity, and the people of God.

**Christ-centered.** The biblical story finds its center and fulfillment in Jesus. Participants are not merely introduced to Christian principles, but invited to behold the beauty of Christ, understand his life, death, and resurrection, and respond to his call to follow him.

**Formation-oriented.** The goal is not simply to learn the story, but to inhabit it. Participants are invited to locate their own lives within God's story, practice the way of Jesus, grow in grace and truth, and become people who embody the hope they have received.

### Belonging · Beholding · Becoming

*Example: Planted.*

- **Behold** — See the beauty of Christ and the grand story of Scripture.
- **Become** — Be transformed into a mature disciple who lives and shares the gospel.

## Inhabit the Story

*Example: Planted.*

*Planted.* uses a biblical-theological approach: participants encounter individual passages within the coherent story of Scripture. Yet the goal is more than recognizing patterns or mastering a storyline. The goal is **inhabiting the story**.

To inhabit the biblical story is to allow it to reshape our imagination—how we understand God, ourselves, other people, suffering, hope, work, community, and the future. Participants are invited to discover not only what happened in the story, but where they stand within it and how they may faithfully respond.

To inhabit the biblical story means more than knowing what happened. It means learning to see God, ourselves, other people, and the world through the story Scripture tells. We begin to ask:

- Where am I in this story?
- What is God doing?
- Who am I becoming?
- What faithful response is being invited from me?

Recurring questions for group leaders / facilitators / CoJourners:

1. **What is happening in God's story?**
2. **What does this reveal about God and humanity?**
3. **Where do we recognize ourselves within this story?**
4. **How are we invited to live differently because of Jesus?**

## Modular Bible Study Introduction

### One Story. Multiple Journeys.

> *The same biblical story can be walked at different rhythms.*

Garden Project Curricula are designed to be **modular**.

Each curriculum follows one coherent biblical movement, but it does not have to be experienced in only one way. The same material can be used for personal study, small-group formation, or a longer church-wide journey.

What changes is not the story itself, but the pace, depth, and setting in which people enter it.

The structure stays intact.
The rhythm adapts to the people.

### Why Modular?

Discipleship does not happen on a single timetable.

A person studying on their own may need something simple and focused. A small group needs room for conversation and shared practice. A church may want to slow the journey down and make space for meals, workshops, and community life.

Modular design allows the same curriculum to serve all three settings without being rewritten from the ground up.

The biblical movement remains whole.
The experience can become shorter, deeper, broader, or more communal depending on the context.

### Three Ways to Journey

| Format | Ideal Context | Duration | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal Journey** | Individual study | **5 weeks** | Scripture, reflection, personal devotion |
| **Community Journey** | Small groups | **12 weeks** | Conversation, programs, shared formation |
| **Church Journey** | Church-wide ministry | **5 months** | Deep formation, shared meals, rhythms of life |

### 1. Personal Journey — Five Weeks

For some people, the best place to begin is simply to read and reflect.

In the five-week format, each chapter becomes one week of study.

This rhythm works well for:

- personal devotions
- one-on-one discipleship
- leader preparation
- reading before joining a group

The focus stays on the essential biblical movement of each chapter without requiring the fuller group experience.

Simple. Focused. Personal.

### 2. Community Journey — Twelve Weeks

The twelve-week format is designed for small groups in churches, ministries, campus communities, and other Christian settings.

It includes:

- introduction
- ten core sessions
- epilogue

With roughly two sessions per chapter, groups have more room for Scripture study, discussion, prayer, programs, and shared reflection.

The material is meant to be used as a **resource, not a script**. Leaders can shape the timing, activities, and discussion around the needs of their own group while staying faithful to the movement and formation goal of each chapter.

This rhythm gives people time not only to learn the Story, but to process it together.

### 3. Church Journey — Five Months

Some stories need more room to unfold.

In a church setting, each chapter can become a month-long movement, giving biblical teaching, embodied practice, and community life time to work together.

A typical month includes **two core study sessions**, **one gathering in another format**—such as a program, activity, workshop, or practice—and **one Family Dinner**.

| Monthly Rhythm | Possible Format | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Program / Activity | Session A |
| **Week 2** | Session A | Session B |
| **Week 3** | Session B | Workshop / Practice |
| **Week 4** | Family Dinner | Family Dinner |

These different gatherings are not interruptions to the study. They are part of the formation process.

The month moves between learning, practicing, reflecting, celebrating, and simply being together.

### Why Family Dinners?

Not all formation happens in a teaching environment.

Some of it happens around a table.

Throughout Scripture, meals are places of fellowship, remembrance, hospitality, celebration, and encounter. Family Dinners make room for those dimensions of discipleship.

They give people time to slow down, share life, tell stories, and deepen relationships.

Instead of adding another lesson, the community practices what it means to belong to one another.

### Build Your Own Journey

Every Garden Project curriculum is built around **five chapters**, each carrying a major movement in the biblical story.

That structure provides a clear path, but the pace can change.

You might:

- walk through one chapter each week on your own,
- use the twelve-week format with a small group,
- spend one month on each chapter as a church,
- or combine several curricula into a longer discipleship pathway.

The goal of modular design is not maximum flexibility for its own sake.

It is to make the curriculum usable in real communities without losing the shape of the Story.

Adapt the rhythm.
Keep the movement.
And give the story enough time to take root.
