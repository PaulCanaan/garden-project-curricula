# Program: Storytelling (Testimony Sharing)

**Session:** (Testimony night / community formation week)
**Estimated Time:** 60–90 min total (depends on group size)
**Group Size:** 4–16 (works best 6–12)

---

## Purpose

This program helps participants experience:

> The power of naming Jesus' work in our real lives—and the way stories build courage, faith, and belonging in community.

The program is designed to prepare participants to explore:

- Testimony as spiritual warfare: resisting shame and silence (Revelation 12:11)
- The inexhaustible story of Jesus' works (John 21:24–25)
- Authenticity and courage as practices of discipleship
- Listening as love: honoring one another's stories without fixing or performing

The activity should not attempt to teach the whole session. Its purpose is to create a shared practice of witness—speaking and listening—so the group can later interpret the experience through Scripture.

---

## Desired Discovery

Participants may begin to notice that:

> Sharing testimony is not a performance—it is witness. When a community listens with honor, shame loosens its grip, faith strengthens, and belonging deepens.

Do not over-explain this before the program. Let the stories do their work.

---

## Materials

- A timer (phone or small stopwatch)
- A way to give a gentle 4-minute signal (hand signal, card, or quiet chime)

### Optional

- Printed testimony outline (the 3-part structure)
- Tissues + water
- A list of "safe follow-up questions" for listeners

---

## Setup

Before participants arrive:

1. Arrange chairs in a circle (or semicircle) so everyone can see each other.
2. Decide where the timekeeper will sit (visible to the speaker).
3. Choose the 4-minute reminder method (non-interruptive).
4. Prepare the room for a tender space: minimize distractions, create warmth.

Arrange the room so that:

- the speaker can share without feeling "on stage";
- participants who feel overwhelmed can step out quietly.

---

## Facilitator Preparation

The Program Facilitator should understand:

- this program can surface grief, shame, trauma, and complicated family stories;
- the goal is not impressive stories, but honest witness;
- vulnerability must be invitational, not coerced;
- how to protect confidentiality and emotional safety;
- how to keep time without shaming people.

Before beginning, decide:

- whether everyone will share tonight, or whether passing is honored (recommended: honor passing, especially for newer participants);
- how you will handle oversharing or unsafe content (gently redirect; offer follow-up after);
- who will be available for pastoral care / 1:1 follow-up.

---

## Prior Week Preparation (Send Ahead)

Ask participants in the previous week:

- "Prepare to share your testimony next time."
- "Since many of us will share, please aim for **under 5 minutes**."
- "Write it out, read it aloud, record yourself, and check the time."

Give this simple 3-part structure:

1. **Before Jesus:** What was your life like before you accepted Jesus as Lord and Savior?
2. **Meeting Jesus:** The moment you experienced Jesus in your life—what was it like?
3. **After Jesus:** What is your life now? How has Jesus been working and changing you?

Encourage prayer:

- "Pray for authenticity and courage."
- "Be as vulnerable as you can *without* feeling forced."
- "God often reveals details as you remember and write."

---

## Introduction to Participants

Begin with an opening prayer.

Then give only the information needed to participate:

> "Tonight we're practicing storytelling—sharing testimony. We're not trying to perform. We're practicing witness: telling the truth about what Jesus has done, and listening with honor."

Explain:

- Time limit: **5 minutes** each.
- Timekeeper: a gentle reminder at 4 minutes.
- Freedom: sharing is invitational; passing is honored.
- Confidentiality: "What's shared here stays here," unless someone asks for help.
- Response: we will not debate, correct, or fix someone's story.

---

## Instructions

### Preparation

1. Confirm the sharing order (volunteer order is often best).
2. Assign a timekeeper.
3. Remind the group of the 4-minute signal and the 5-minute target.
4. Invite everyone to listen actively: phones away, eyes up, hearts open.

### Sharing Round — Testimony (5 minutes each)

For each person:

1. Speaker shares their testimony (aim for the 3-part structure).
2. Timekeeper gives a gentle 4-minute reminder.
3. At ~5 minutes, the facilitator may say kindly: "I'm going to pause you there so we can make space for everyone—thank you."
4. The group responds with brief honor (choose one):
   - "Thank you for sharing."
   - a short prayer of gratitude,
   - or a moment of silence.

### Optional: 2-Minute Pair Share (If group is large)

If you have more than ~10 people, consider:

- having half the group share in pairs first (2 minutes each),
- then inviting 4–6 people to share with the full group.

### Completion

End the program when:

- everyone who wants to share has shared,
- and there is still time for debrief + Scripture connection.

---

## Accessibility and Safety

- No forced vulnerability.
- No pressure to share traumatic details.
- No cross-talk while someone is sharing.
- Do not interrogate someone's past.
- Follow up privately if needed.

---

## Discussion

Take time here. The discussion is part of the program, not an optional explanation afterward.

### 1. Experience

1. What did it feel like to share? What did it feel like to listen?
2. What was harder than you expected? What was easier?
3. Did you feel any fear, resistance, or shame as you spoke?

### 2. Observation

1. What patterns did you hear across stories?
2. What did you learn about God from listening to others?
3. What helped the space feel safe? What threatened safety?

### 3. Interpretation

1. Why do you think Jesus calls us to witness—not just privately believe?
2. What do testimonies do to shame and isolation?
3. How might storytelling be a way God builds a people (not just individuals)?

### 4. Scripture Connection

**Key Passages:**

- John 21:24–25
- Revelation 12:11
