# Devil Not Today (EN) — Chapter 2: Say No to the Enemy

*(Planted 2023 — Chinese source translation)*

## Activity: Dartboard

**Materials:** magnetic dartboard + darts; a blindfold cloth for each person
**Set up:** hang the dartboard on a tree or wall

### Flow

1. **Round 1:** normal distance (~3 meters) throwing darts
2. **Round 2:** increase distance (~5 meters)
3. **Round 3:** increase difficulty by throwing while blindfolded

Each person gets 3 darts per round. (Optional: keep score + small prize.)

### Discussion

- Ask people how they felt (difficulty, any method they discovered).

### What the activity represents

**Observation:** the bullseye is small, and there's a lot of area where you can miss.

**Theme:** *What is sin?*

- The **bullseye** represents **God's standard**.
- **Sin** is anything that falls short of God's standard.
- No one can claim to hit the bullseye every time; no one is perfect or sinless.
- **Distance** represents how close our relationship with God is: the closer we are to God and know God, the easier it is to follow God's way.
- **Blindfold** represents that before knowing God we walk in darkness; relying on ourselves (self-righteousness) it's almost impossible to "hit the mark."

### Scripture connection

- **Romans 3:23** — "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."
- **1 John 1:8** — "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us."
- **John 3:17** — God sent the Son not to condemn the world, but to save it.
- **Romans 8:1** — No condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.

> Purpose note: this activity is not meant to make participants feel condemned. If Jesus does not condemn us, we have no right to condemn others. The point is to help us understand why sin is part of human nature.

## Transition into Bible study: Genesis 3

Last week we studied Genesis 1–2: in a garden named "Delight," humans enjoyed intimate relationship with God.

So why do we no longer feel like we're in that garden? Why do humans carry a sinful nature and fall short of God's standard?

Today we explore what happened in **Genesis 3**.

### Observations from Genesis 3

- The **serpent** is described as crafty; its craftiness shows up in its words: "Did God really say…?" "You will not surely die…"
- The serpent **distorts** what God originally said (cf. Genesis 2:16–17).
- Eve adds words God didn't say ("and you must not touch it"), stepping into the trap.
- Before eating, Eve "saw" the fruit as good for food, pleasing to the eye, desirable for wisdom (Genesis 3:6).
- After eating: "their eyes were opened," and beneath the surface **shame**, **guilt**, and **fear** enter:
  - shame: realizing nakedness
  - guilt: hiding from God
  - fear: "I was afraid…" (Gen 3:10)

Humanity's "view" did not expand so much as **God became more distant in our sight**.

### Is Eve the source of sin?

Eve is deceived and speaks like a victim ("the serpent deceived me, and I ate"). The story suggests something deeper is happening.

So where does sin come from?

## Another angle: Jesus and temptation

Jesus is called the sinless, spotless Lamb (cf. **1 Peter 1:19**). He faced real temptation yet did not sin.

In **Matthew 4:1–11**, the tempter speaks in a familiar way: "If you are the Son of God…"—attacking identity and twisting truth.

Jesus names the enemy: "Away from me, *Satan!*"

## Spiritual warfare

Our struggle is not merely against flesh and blood.

- **Ephesians 6:12**

### The enemy's strategies

1. **Isolation** (Eve alone; Jesus in the wilderness)
2. **Whispering lies** (twisted truth, partial truth)

- **John 8:44** — the devil is a liar and the father of lies.
- **John 10:10** — the thief comes to steal, kill, and destroy.

### The raw materials (1 John 2:16)

**1 John 2:16** summarizes the connection between Eve's and Jesus' encounters.

|  | Lust of the flesh | Lust of the eyes | Pride of life |
|---|---|---|---|
| Devil → Eve | good for food (Gen 3:6) | pleasing to the eye (Gen 3:6) | desirable for wisdom (Gen 3:6) |
| Devil → Jesus | "turn stones to bread" (Matt 4:3) | kingdoms + splendor (Matt 4:8) | "I will give you all this" (Matt 4:9) |

### The enemy as manipulator + accuser

When someone says "yes" to disordered desire or false pride, the enemy turns into an accuser and pours out shame, guilt, and fear.

## Jesus' victory

Jesus was not truly alone: he remained in the Father's presence.

He countered lies with Scripture:

- **Deuteronomy 8:3**
- **Deuteronomy 6:16**
- **Deuteronomy 6:13**

"Not today, Satan."

## Gospel invitation

Jesus is the physician who came for the sick, not the righteous.

- **Matthew 9:12–13**

We don't need to clean ourselves up before returning to God; healing is available in Jesus.

## Closing

We are in spiritual warfare, but we don't have to be victims.

Proclaim truth over lies, fight together in God's presence, and **give the devil no opportunity** (Ephesians 4:27).

You are a seed, buried in darkness. Because of sin, the world is full of brokenness—but that's not the end of the story.
