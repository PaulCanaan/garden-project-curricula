# Planted Vol. 2 — Sin: Devil Not Today

Let's do a dart board activity before starting our Bible study today.

In the first round, just throw darts regularly; after everyone tries several throws, increase the distance for the second round; then level up, let the throwers close or cover their eyes, make sure they follow the instructions and not hit anybody, then try to throw the darts.

Hard, isn't it? The bullseye is so small, and there's so much area that you can miss. In this area we are learning about a fact - what sin and brokenness is. The bullseye represents God's standard, and sin is anywhere away from the bullseye; nobody can say that they can hit the bullseye every time. The distance represents how close our relationship with God is. It's easier to follow the Way of God if we build close relationships with Him. What's more, it's almost impossible to hit the bullseye when our eyes are closed. We were walking in the darkness before we met God, therefore, there's no way that we were able to hit the bullseye of righteousness by ourselves.

Romans 3:23 stated the truth: *For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.* 1 John 1:8 was even more straightforward: *If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.*

The fact is clear: we are all broken in this broken world, and everyone is broken in different ways. We've known sin is falling short on God's standards. The following question is: where is sin from? You may know Genesis 3 is usually titled *The Fall*. Maybe we can find some clues there. Let's dig in, shall we?

First let's make some observations. Who was there? The serpent, who "was more crafty than any other animals"; Eve, "the mother of all living" Adam named her later; her husband Adam, who showed up after Eve's encounter with the serpent; and God of course, He is omnipresent, but notice that He was at a distance at first, because later He had to look for Adam and Eve. We can tell how cunning the serpent was from the way he spoke: "Did God really say…" "You will not certainly die…" etc. His words were so twisted from the original Word of God.

How did Eve fall for the trick of the serpent? She went with the flow - "God did say 'You must not eat the fruit…'", and she added "you must not touch it", which was not mentioned by God when He was commanding in Genesis 2:17. She was one step in the trap.

What did Eve do before she made the decision of eating the fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil? The Scripture drew the scene: When the woman saw that the fruit of the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eye, and also desirable for gaining wisdom (verse 6). She set her eyes on the fruit first, and started thinking about what the fruit could do to her body and mind. Pay attention to this detail, and we will get back to this part.

What happened when Eve ate the fruit and shared it with her husband? "Their eyes were opened". The name of the tree was true, they gained knowledge and self-awareness and were enlightened. What's below the surface was that *shame* and *guilt* creeped in. They realized that they were naked (shame), tried to cover themselves and hid from God (that's guilt, they knew they did something wrong), and *fear* became the motives of their actions: "I was afraid…" said Adam in verse 10.

Maybe originally the awareness of humans was like the dart board with the bullseye only; once our Father and Mother ate the fruit from the tree, the dart board was no longer bullseye only, but as what we can see today, with so much area to mess around. It's not because our world view was extended, but our view on God became more distant. Maybe God's original thoughts of humans were enjoying intimate relationships with them forever in the Garden of Delight, now there are age limits on human flesh. Adam and Eve didn't die right away physically, but their relationships with God were choked by shame, guilt, and fear. They were dead *spiritually*.

There are more consequences of sin in Genesis 3 for later studies. Until now, we still have little clue how everything just happened. Was Eve intentionally disobedient and tried to rebel against God? No, she was deceived, she was a victim, she was not the source of sin. The case may be more complicated than we thought. Was sin from the tree or the fruit? Well, it has "good" in its name, so I don't think it's all evil. The serpent seems most likely to be the Suspect. Did an animal cause the whole human being to stumble, or is there anything behind it?

We already started the case, now we need to have the detective mindset and think from other perspectives. Do you know the Dude Jesus, who was called "the sinless, spotless Lamb of God" (1 Peter 1:19 NLT)? He came to the world to experience everything people could suffer from, and face every temptation people could face on this planet earth, but he *did not sin*. Sin doesn't work on him. How come?

Matthew 4:1–11 took place when Jesus was fasting 40 days in the wilderness. The tempter showed up with a tone that sounded very familiar: "If you are Son of God…" Crafty, cunning, tricky. Just like the serpent! The same way he twisted the truth spoken from God, the same way he attacked the identities that were found in God. Now we know that the serpent showed up again, he's not just the serpent, not just the tempter, but also the name that was pointed out by Jesus - "Away from me, *Satan*!" (verse 10)

"Satan", aka the devil, means "Opposer". There are invisible enemies. There are spiritual forces. Whether you are a believer or not, we feel the world is off and so broken. Why? Can I tell that we are in the middle of spiritual warfare. I don't want us to be like soldiers in WWII, not knowing where the enemies would attack from, and being slaughtered in ignorance. Knowing who our enemies are is the first step of attacking them. Ephesians 6:12 (NLT) says: For we are not fighting against flesh-and-blood enemies, but against evil rulers and authorities in this dark world, and against evil spirits in the heavenly places.

The devil actually has some common strategies. First is isolation. When God was walking in the Garden, he had to look for Adam and Eve, which indicates Eve was alone and distant from the presence of God when she encountered the serpent. Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted (Matthew 4:1). The tempter showed up after he fasted alone for 40 days and 40 nights. When you are in isolation, you are an easy prey, you are more likely to be a target of the devil. Then he starts to whisper lies. Lies are not 100% untruth, but twisted truth. Partial truth mixed with lies is the most difficult to distinguish, and the most destructive. The devil knew what God said and who Jesus was (Matthew 3:17 NKJV: "This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."). He took part of the truth and twisted, and added question marks: "Did God really say that?" "Jesus, are you really the Son of God?" When he lies, he speaks his native language, for he is a liar and the father of lies (John 8:44). The devil does not have the ability to create anything bad, he doesn't have anything original, he just takes what God created for the good, and makes it meant for evil. There are many hijacked ideologies in this world, to name a few: liberty, happiness, love, prosperity gospel… which are far different from what they were supposed to be. The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy (John 10:10). Exactly how the devil works.

What are some raw materials the devil uses to form weapons? There's a verse in John's messages that can be used to summarize the *correlations* between Eve's and Jesus's encounter with the devil:

> *1 John 2:16 — For everything in the world - the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life - comes not from the Father but from the world.*

|  | The lust of the flesh | The lust of the eyes | The pride of life |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Devil to Eve** | the woman saw the fruit of the tree was good for food (Gen 3:6) | (the fruit of the tree was) pleasing to the eye (Gen 3:6) | and also desirable for gaining wisdom (Gen 3:6) |
| **Devil to Jesus** | "Tell these stones to become bread." (Matt 4:3) | (the devil) showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their splendor (Matt 4:8) | "All this I will give you…" (Matt 4:9) |

The devil is not just a liar, but a manipulator. He knows how the desires of the flesh and the way of the world works, and he knows how to manipulate them. Steal. Kill. And destroy. Once you say a "yes" to the desire of the flesh that's disordered by the devil or the pride of life falsely promised by him, he will become the accuser, snapping his fingers: "ha, got ya!", and dump shame, guilt, and fear over you.

Jesus wasn't a victim like Eve. He won that spiritual battle. It seemed like Jesus was isolated in the wilderness, but he was not. After 40 days of fasting and praying, his physical body was weak, but his Spirit was stronger than ever. Jesus had been in the presence with his Father the whole time, he was not fighting alone. Jesus knew and held firm on his identities and callings found in God, and he countered every lie with the truth from the Scripture. Deuteronomy 8:3: "*Man shall not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God.*" Deuteronomy 6:16: "*Do not put the Lord your God to the test.*" Deuteronomy 6:13: "*Worship the Lord your God, and serve Him only.*"

Not today, Satan! Jesus was saying "*no*" to the liar, with the sword of truth in his hand, standing firm in God's presence, till his glorious victory: then the devil left him, and angels came and attended him (Matthew 4:11). The triumph of Jesus over the devil was not simply casting out the existence of the devil, but disarming him. One day there will be no more death or sorrow, but for now, the time of fulfilling God's salvation plan has not arrived yet. Jesus redeems what has been stolen by the devil, resurrects who has been killed, and restores the destroyed. He speaks truths to the lies. He brings identities over the shame. Forgiveness to the guilt. Home and community for the isolated and the outcast.

"*It's not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick… For I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners.*" (Matthew 9:12–13). Saint Augustine once said, the church is not a museum of saints, but a hospital for the sinners. Christians are all forgiven sinners. We all need to be healed. We all have broken hearts that need to be restored. You don't clean yourself before you turn back to God, just like you don't wait to see the doctor till you are healed. Healing is always available in the name of Jesus. Even for the healthy, no one can promise they won't be sick in their lifetime, just like no one can say they won't sin anymore. Sin is a contagious disease, but the Good News is: Jesus died for our sins from the past, present, and in the future. It doesn't mean we can keep messing around, Jesus loves us too much to let us stay the same. Who would like to be sick and broken when they could choose to be healthy and whole?

Take a deep breath. It's been a long day. Now we know that we are in the middle of spiritual warfare. We know *who* our enemies are (if you need a fresh reminder, go ahead and read Ephesians 6:12 again this week). And we know that we don't have to be victims. We don't have to be shaken by the lies the enemy whispers to us. We don't have to fight alone. What we can do is to proclaim the truth over the lies, fight together as a community in the presence of God. Give no opportunity to the devil (Ephesians 4:27 ESV)!

You are a seed, and you are buried in the darkness. Because of sin, the world is full of brokenness. However, that's not the end of the story…
