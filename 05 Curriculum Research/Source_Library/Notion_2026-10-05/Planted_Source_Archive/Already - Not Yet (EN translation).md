# "Already / Not Yet" (EN)

*(Chinese source translation)*

"Already / not yet" is a major theme in the Bible.

In salvation history, Jesus and the Holy Spirit have **already** accomplished God's promises, and yet the Lord has **not yet** returned to bring final judgment and complete restoration.

### Key Scriptures

- **2 Corinthians 5:17** — If anyone is in Christ, they are a new creation.
- **Philippians 1:6** — God will complete the good work in you at the day of Christ.
- **Acts 3:21** — Christ remains in heaven until the time for the restoration of all things.
- **Philippians 3:20–21** — Our citizenship is in heaven; we await the Savior who will transform our bodies.

> (Quote paraphrase from Burk Parsons): We are pilgrims on the way home; our identity as citizens of heaven is secured in Christ, who reigns and will return to judge, conquer, and consummate all things.

### The "already / not yet" of the Kingdom

Holding both Christ's finished work and the promised future renewal is how Paul can say, "forgetting what lies behind… I press on."

But how do we "set our minds on things above"? It's hard to long for a home we have never been to.

We need a reshaped imagination through **God's revelation** (Scripture) and through the **eyes of the Holy Spirit**, who enables us to see God's wisdom, eternal life, and the realities of the Kingdom.

## Heaven and earth

### A common "worldly" view of Christianity

1. Humans are bad.
2. God is good.
3. Believers go to heaven after death.
4. Non-believers go to hell after death.

### The Bible's storyline

1. The world and humans began as God's good creation.
2. Sin damaged and separated our relationship with God.
3. Jesus' death and resurrection rebuilt the bridge of reconciliation.
4. When Jesus returns, the present world will pass away and there will be a new heaven and new earth.

God's longing is to **dwell with** God's people: tabernacle, temple, and the Spirit's dwelling within believers—Emmanuel, "God with us."

- **John 1:14** — The Word became flesh and *dwelt* among us.
- **Revelation 21:1–4** — New heaven/new earth; God's dwelling with people; no more death, mourning, crying, pain.

Humans are conditioned by time (Augustine).

- **Psalm 90:12** — Teach us to number our days.

Ecclesiastes' "vanity" (*hebel*) can be understood as vapor/breath—life's fleeting nature.

## Eternal past + eternal future

Eternity is one of God's mysteries. God's time is higher than ours, so past/present/future are "present" to God.

Where heaven and earth overlap is not only the Kingdom's "already / not yet," but also the intersection of eternity with human history.

From the incarnation, eternity and history begin to interweave.

Plants can teach us about eternal life: cuttings can root and become new life—continuity.

In a similar way, God plants eternal life in our hearts.

- **Romans 8:24–25** — We hope for what we do not yet see and wait with patience.

So what is eternal life?

Not merely infinite time, but **life with God**—life that transcends the limits of time.

Eternal life does not begin only after death. When someone receives Jesus as Savior, the destination of life is changed, and eternal life begins now.

Looking back we remember God's faithfulness; looking forward we hold God's promises with hope.
