# Framework Design Test

Before something is classified as a *framework* in the Garden Project curriculum library, it should pass these tests:

### 1. Is there a recurring need?

Does it address a problem participants will encounter more than once?

### 2. Is it biblically governed?

Does Scripture shape the framework rather than merely provide a supporting verse?

### 3. Is it portable?

Can participants remember and use it outside the original session?

### 4. Is its form meaningful?

Does the acronym, matrix, sequence, equation, or image clarify the idea rather than merely decorate it?

### 5. Does it lead to practice?

Can participants do something with it?

### 6. Does it remain Christ-centered?

Does it direct participants toward Christ rather than toward a self-contained technique?

### 7. Does it have clear limits?

Can leaders explain where the framework is useful and where it is insufficient?

### 8. Can it mature?

Can it be revisited, practiced, integrated, or deepened later?

A concise definition for your curriculum architecture could be:

> **Frameworks are memorable, biblically grounded lenses and practices that help participants interpret Scripture, understand their lives, and respond faithfully beyond a single session.**
