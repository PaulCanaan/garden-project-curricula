## Facilitation Notes

During the activity:

- encourage participation without forcing it;
- allow enough time for the experience to change or deepen;
- avoid interpreting participants' behavior publicly;
- notice who is excluded, overlooked, uncomfortable, or dominating;
- preserve the intended contrast between stages;
- adapt when the activity becomes unsafe or loses its purpose.

### Watch For

- initial reactions versus reactions after several minutes;
- changes in energy or emotion;
- unexpected strategies;
- patterns of leadership or withdrawal;
- who depends on whom;
- what happens when the rules change;
- what participants say spontaneously.

Do not treat every reaction as spiritually significant. The activity creates a conversation; it does not diagnose participants.

### Emotional and Relational Safety

## Discussion

Take time here. The discussion is part of the program, not an optional explanation afterward.

### 1. Experience

Begin with what happened.

1. How did you feel when the activity began?
2. Did your experience change over time?
3. What was easy, difficult, surprising, or frustrating?
4. What moment stood out most?

### 2. Observation

Move from feeling to what participants noticed.

1. What patterns emerged?
2. What changed between the rounds or stages?
3. Who or what held the activity together?
4. What helped or hindered success?
5. What did the activity reveal that words alone might not have shown?

### 3. Interpretation

Invite participants to suggest meaning before the facilitator gives key points.

1. What might the objects, spaces, rules, or roles represent?
2. Where do you see a similar pattern in everyday life?
3. What human or relational tendency did the activity expose?
4. What do you think this program is inviting us to consider?

### 4. Scripture Connection

Introduce the relevant passage only after participants have begun interpreting the experience.

**Key Passage:** [Passage]

Ask:

1. Where do you see a connection between the activity and this text?
2. How does Scripture clarify or correct our interpretation?
3. What does the passage reveal about God?
4. What does it reveal about us?
5. Where do grace and truth appear?

### 5. Response

1. Where do you recognize this pattern in your own life?
2. What might faithful response look like?
3. What would need to change?
4. Where might you need grace, truth, courage, or support?

## After the Program

The facilitator should note:

- What produced the clearest discovery?
- Which instructions were confusing?
- Did the activity run too long or too quickly?
- Did every participant have a meaningful way to engage?
- Did the discussion reach the intended insight?
- Did any participant need follow-up?
- What should be changed before the program is used again?
