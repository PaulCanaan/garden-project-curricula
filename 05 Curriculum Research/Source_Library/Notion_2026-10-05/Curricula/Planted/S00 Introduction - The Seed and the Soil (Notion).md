# Introduction: The Seed and the Soil

> Then he told them many things in parables, saying, "Consider the sower who went out to sow. As he sowed, some seed fell along the path, and the birds came and devoured them. Other seed fell on rocky ground where it didn't have much soil, and it grew up quickly since the soil wasn't deep. But when the sun came up, it was scorched, and since it had no root, it withered away. Other seed fell among thorns, and the thorns came up and choked it. Still other seed fell on good ground and produced fruit: some a hundred, some sixty, and some thirty times what was sown. Let anyone who has ears listen."
> (Matthew 13:3–9)

A garden is where nature and culture meet, where nature can be cultivated, and culture can grow naturally. Children play, seasons change, and living beings grow towards their full potential. Life is marked by beauty and faithful stewardship. A garden grows by planting one seed at a time, and every seed that is sown dreams about a garden. Jesus once told a story about a farmer who went out to sow.

In Jesus' parable, the seed is not the variable, the condition of the soil is. What we call the parable of the sower is more about the parable of the soils. As Jesus tells the story, the same seed falls in four different places. Some lands on a hardened path. Some falls on shallow, rocky ground. Some begins to grow among thorns. Some falls into good soil and produces a harvest. What is the farmer like in the story? Is he a careless farmer who allows the seeds to leak through a hole on the seed bag? Does he lack experience, not knowing what soil is best for the seed to grow? Neither. Jesus included a detail that "The sower *went out* to sow." The farmer is intentional. He has a plan, and he is determined to spread the seed on all the fields. The way the farmer's wide scatter reveals that he is a generous sower and he does not take partiality. The seed is good wherever it falls. Yet the life hidden within the seed is received differently by each soil.
