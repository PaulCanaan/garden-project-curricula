# Session 1 Leader Notes — In the Beginning, a Garden

## 1. Session Aim

**Big Idea:** We were created to know God and flourish in His presence. God brings order, beauty, and life into a world of chaos—and invites humanity to receive dignity and vocation as His image-bearers.

**Central Question:** If "In the beginning, God…," what does that re-center about reality, about who we are, and about what a good human life is for?

**Formation Goal:** Participants begin to receive their lives as known, loved, and purposefully planted by God—moving from self-starting to God-starting, from anxiety to wonder, and from achievement-based worth to gifted dignity.

## 2. Preparation

**Read**

- Genesis 1:1–2:3 (slowly; note repetition and structure)
- Psalm 90:4 and 2 Peter 3:8 (for posture in the Day-Age debate; optional)
- Psalm 139:1–18 (for next week's bridge / homework)

**Pray**

- For humility to pursue truth without defensiveness.
- For participants carrying shame/rejection to feel God's delight as believable.

**Materials**

- Printed Bible passages or Bible access for everyone
- Whiteboard / paper for capturing repeated phrases and the "days" pattern (forming/filling)
- Optional: a simple 6-day parallel chart (Days 1–3 forming; Days 4–6 filling; Day 7 climax)

**Pastoral Awareness**

- Some participants may have spiritual wounds connected to parental approval, rejection, or performance; "delight" language can feel unsafe or unbelievable at first.
- Some participants may come in with strong opinions about creation chronology; redirect from debate to discipleship posture (humility, wonder, worship).

## 3. Program Guidance

**Purpose**

- Establish a "safe and curious" culture for the whole program: questions are welcomed; the goal is worshipful understanding, not winning.
- Set the program's baseline worldview: God is first; reality is Creator/creature; creation is good and purposeful.

**Instructions (flow)**

1. Start with a brief check-in + last-session review.
2. Read Genesis 1:1–2:3 aloud (consider two or more readers).
3. Observation first: capture repetitions/structure before interpretation.
4. Move to meaning: Creator–creature distinction, chaos/order motif, Sabbath climax, image/vocation.
5. Close with reflection + prayer: wonder, gratitude, surrender of "created ultimates."

**Watch For**

- "Science vs. Bible" spiral. If it arises, affirm legitimacy of questions, then reframe: Genesis is giving a worldview and a God-centered story of reality.
- Overly abstract discussion. Keep grounding in the text's repeated phrases and structure.
- Shame narratives: "I'm not enough / I don't matter." Gently connect *imago Dei* to gifted worth prior to achievement.

**Debrief (after)**

- What moments felt like worship? What moments felt defensive?
- Did anyone seem stuck in shame or anxiety? Follow up personally.

**Transition**

- Bridge forward: Genesis 1 zooms out; Genesis 2 zooms in—from the cosmos to a garden, from structure to story, from "Who is God?" to "What are humans made to do?"

## 4. Framework Guidance

**Core Idea**

- **S.O.A.P.** keeps the session text-first and formation-oriented:
  - **S**cripture: Genesis 1:1–2:3
  - **O**bservation: repetition, structure, climax
  - **A**pplication: where to re-center life on God; stewardship; Sabbath posture
  - **P**rayer: praise, confession (idolatry), trust amid chaos, renewed identity

**Guardrails**

- Don't let "application" become mere productivity. Application here is *worshipful alignment*: receiving identity, practicing trust, and stewarding created life as gift.
- Keep the group from treating Genesis like a technical manual. Teach that the text is theological, literary, and doxological—designed to reshape imagination toward God.

**Practice**

- End with one concrete "image-bearing stewardship" practice for the week (home, work, relationships, body, time), plus a brief Sabbath-oriented micro-practice (rest as trust).

## 5. Passage Guidance

**Context**

- Genesis 1 functions as the Bible's opening worldview statement and the introduction to the Pentateuch's story of God and God's people.
- The passage is richly patterned (rhythm/repetition/symmetry) and aims to form worshipful perception of reality.

**Key Details to Highlight**

- "In the beginning, God…": God precedes and grounds everything.
- Repetition ("Then God said…," "and it was so," "God saw that it was good").
- Structure: Days 1–3 forming/separating; Days 4–6 filling; Day 7 as climax.
- Seventh day: no evening/morning refrain; God blesses and sanctifies the day.
- Humanity: created in God's image; identity and vocation are given together.

**Interpretive Cautions**

- Be careful about turning "days" into a litmus test for orthodoxy within the group; keep humility and charity.
- "Waters" can be discussed as both material reality and a symbolic picture of chaos/disorder; avoid over-allegorizing, but don't flatten the text's imagery.

**Likely Questions**

- "Are the days literal?" → "Different faithful Christians answer differently; our aim today is the text's God-centered worldview and what it forms in us."
- "Why no 'Let there be water'?" → "Genesis 1:1 is the umbrella claim: God created everything; the narrative then highlights God ordering/overcoming chaos."
- "Why does God 'rest'?" → "Not fatigue; enthronement/delight—creation is for God's presence."
- "What is the image of God?" → "Gifted dignity and a royal-priestly calling to steward."

## 6. Teaching Emphasis

**Main Insight**

- Genesis 1 is not merely about *what* exists but *who* reality belongs to: the Creator who brings order from chaos and invites human beings into dignified stewardship within His presence.

**Christological Center**

- Read creation as the first movement of the story that culminates in Christ:
  - God's Word brings creation into being; in the New Testament, Christ is revealed as the divine Word through whom all things were made (John 1:1–3).
  - The Sabbath-climax anticipates God's dwelling with humanity; Jesus is the one who brings the presence of God to us and begins new creation.

**Biblical-Story Connections**

- Creation → Fall: the good world becomes disordered through sin.
- New creation: God again brings order and life out of chaos, ultimately through resurrection.

## 7. Discussion and Formation

**Essential Questions**

- Why does it matter that Scripture begins with God rather than our needs?
- Where do you see the Creator–creature line blurred in culture, and in your own life?
- What "chaos" are you facing, and what would trust look like without denial?
- How does Sabbath challenge achievement-based worth?
- What changes if your dignity is *given* before anything you accomplish?

**Weekly Practice**

- Choose one small stewardship practice (15–30 minutes) as worship: care for body, home, work, relationships, or time as God's gift.
- Try a simple Sabbath micro-practice: one intentional "stop" moment where you resist productivity to remember God is God.

**Pastoral Follow-Up**

- For participants carrying shame: reinforce that being "image" is gift, not reward; invite gentle prayer and check-in midweek.
- For participants prone to debate: affirm their desire for truth; invite them into curiosity and worship, and offer resources for later conversation if needed.

## 8. Worship and Prayer

**Response**

- Wonder, gratitude, humility, and renewed trust: "In the beginning, God… therefore I am not the beginning."

**Prayer Focus**

- Praise: God as Creator and King.
- Confession: ways we've made created things ultimate (self, success, relationships, control).
- Lament/trust: present chaos brought honestly before God.
- Petition: renewed imagination for what it means to be human; grace to steward well.

**Blessing**

- "May the Creator who spoke light into darkness speak peace into your chaos, and may you learn to live as God's beloved image-bearer—receiving and reflecting His presence."

## 9. After the Session

**Debrief**

- What did participants notice in the text's structure and repetition?
- Where did the room move toward worship? Where did it drift toward argument or anxiety?
- Who needs personal follow-up (shame, grief, strong reactions)?

**Follow-Up**

- Send/remind homework: Genesis 2 + Psalm 139:1–18.
- Encourage one sentence of testimony next week: "Because God created the world, I can…"

**Curriculum Notes**

- This session sets tone: safety + curiosity + worshipful learning.
- If time is tight, prioritize: (1) observation of structure, (2) Sabbath climax, (3) image/vocation + one concrete practice.
