# Planted. — Leader Resources

## Session Flow & Purpose

### Welcome — Become present

- Hospitality and relational check-in
- Recap of the previous gathering
- Opening question, story, image, or experiential program
- Introduction to the session's central tension

### Explore — Enter the story

- Reading the biblical text
- Observation and discussion
- Biblical-theological connections
- Christ-centered discovery
- Framework introduction or practice
- Connection to participants' lives

### Debrief — Respond and carry it forward

- Personal reflection
- Practical next step
- Prayer and worship
- Preparation for the next gathering
- Continuation into everyday life

| Movements | Typical elements |
|---|---|
| Welcome | Food, conversation, check-in |
| Experience | Program, image, story, or question |
| Scripture | Reading, observation, interpretation |
| Discussion | Discovery, reflection, shared meaning |
| Response | Practice, prayer, worship |
| Sending | Next step and preparation |

## How to Facilitate Discussion

### Ask open questions

Prefer:

> What do you notice?
> What surprises or troubles you?
> What does this reveal about God?
> Where do you see yourself in this passage?

Avoid questions that merely ask participants to guess the leader's predetermined answer.

### Create space

Silence is not failure. Give people time to think.

### Invite without forcing

Participation should be encouraged, but vulnerability should never be coerced.

### Clarify before correcting

When someone says something uncertain, ask:

> Tell us more about what you mean.

### Return to the text

When opinions multiply, gently redirect:

> What do we see in the passage itself?

### Protect the group

Interrupt shaming, dominating, fixing, or overly personal interrogation.

## Leading a Seeker- and New-Believer-Friendly Group

### Explain Christian language

Terms such as *gospel, sin, grace, covenant, kingdom, resurrection,* and *discipleship* should not be assumed.

### Welcome honest questions

Do not treat doubt as rebellion or curiosity as disrespect.

### Distinguish essential truth from secondary debate

Leaders should avoid allowing complex doctrinal controversies to derail the central movement of a session.

### Do not pressure conversion

Clearly invite response to Jesus while honoring each participant's agency and pace.

### Avoid insider assumptions

Do not assume everyone knows how to find Bible passages, pray aloud, participate in communion, or understand church practices.

### Make next steps concrete

Possible next steps may include:

- reading a Gospel;
- beginning to pray;
- attending church;
- meeting with a mature Christian;
- joining Christian community;
- choosing to follow Jesus;
- baptism;
- serving;
- sharing one's story.

The leader helps participants discern a faithful next step rather than prescribing the same step for everyone.

## Working with the Five Frameworks

> The five frameworks are companions, not content to be mastered in a single meeting. Introduce each framework simply, practice it concretely, and return to it when later sessions deepen its meaning.

Then give one leader note per framework:

- **The Grace–Truth Matrix:** Begin as a diagnostic and relational lens; later reveal its formation dimension through time.
- **S.O.A.P.:** Model it repeatedly until participants can use it independently.
- **The Gospel Flow:** Keep the movement from God's character and action to identity and response; do not begin with human performance.
- **Know · Grow · Go:** Use it to help participants identify balanced next steps in discipleship.
- **PLANT:** Present evangelism as prayerful participation in God's work, not pressure to manufacture results.

The evolving Grace–Truth Matrix can be explained explicitly:

> Do not reveal every dimension of the matrix at its first introduction. Its development is part of the learning journey. After participants encounter resurrection, kingdom, and eternity, the addition of time transforms the matrix from a map of possible postures into a model of patient growth:
>
> **Growth = (Grace + Truth) × Time**
>
> The framework itself evolves because the participant's vision is evolving.

## Pastoral and Group-Care Notes

Because the topics of the curriculum include sin, shame, isolation, spiritual warfare, suffering, testimony, and identity, leaders need basic boundaries.

> Leaders should be prepared for participants to disclose painful experiences. Receive such disclosures with compassion, avoid offering simplistic answers, and do not attempt to function as a licensed counselor when needs exceed the group's competence.

Include guidelines such as:

- Protect confidentiality, while explaining its safety limits.
- Never promise secrecy when someone may be in danger.
- Do not diagnose mental-health or spiritual conditions.
- Avoid attributing every struggle directly to demonic activity.
- Follow the church or ministry's safeguarding policies.
- Know whom to contact for pastoral care, counseling, abuse reports, or crisis support.
- Follow up privately when appropriate, but maintain healthy relational boundaries.

This can remain concise in the main guide and link to organizational policies.

## Preparing Each Session

A repeatable preparation checklist:

### Pray

Pray for each participant, the group environment, and the work of the Spirit.

### Inhabit the passage

Read the text personally before preparing to explain it.

### Identify the movement

Determine:

- the session's central biblical movement;
- how it points to Christ;
- the intended formation response;
- the role of the featured framework.

### Prepare the environment

Confirm:

- room and seating;
- food and hospitality;
- materials for activities;
- audiovisual needs;
- Scripture copies;
- worship and prayer plans;
- accessibility and safety considerations.

### Prepare questions

Choose fewer, stronger questions rather than trying to cover every possibility.

### Prepare to adapt

Know the essential goal of the session so less-important content can be shortened when conversation needs more time.

## After Each Session

Standard leader debrief.

### Notice

- Where did participants engage?
- What caused confusion or resistance?
- What seemed spiritually significant?
- Who may need follow-up?

### Reflect

- Did the session remain biblical, Christ-centered, and formation-oriented?
- Did participants have space to discover and respond?
- Did the leaders speak too much?
- Was the environment welcoming to seekers?

### Respond

- Pray for participants.
- Follow up where appropriate.
- Record improvements.
- Prepare the transition into the next session.

This makes curriculum improvement part of the ministry rhythm.
