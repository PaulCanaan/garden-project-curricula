# Covenant, Kingdom, and People of God — Course Frame (Notion)

## Overview

This lesson is a 5‑week Bible study on how God forms a people and establishes God's Kingdom through a series of covenants that move the story from creation to Christ.

- **Week 1 (Universal / Creation–Noah):** Read Genesis 1–11 as a movement from order to disorder and back again. Explore whether covenant language can apply to Adam/creation, then study the **Noahic covenant** (Gen 6–9) as God's unconditional commitment to preserve and restore creation.
- **Week 2 (Ancestral / Abraham):** Follow the patriarchs as "sojourners" (Abraham, Isaac, Jacob). Study the **Abrahamic covenant** (Gen 12, 15, 17)—descendants, land, and blessing—highlighting God's unilateral faithfulness (Gen 15) and the covenant sign moving from **circumcision** to a deeper "circumcision of the heart."
- **Week 3 (National / Moses–Sinai):** Re-tell the Exodus as **way out, way through, way in**. See the **Mosaic covenant** (Exo 19–24) as God restoring nearness/presence (tabernacle), forming Israel into a **kingdom of priests and a holy nation**, and setting a pattern that anticipates a greater "new exodus."
- **Week 4 (Messianic / David + Jeremiah 31):** Trace Israel's kings (Saul, David, Solomon) and the rise/fall that leads to exile. Study the **Davidic covenant** as the Abrahamic promises taking a kingdom/royal form (house, throne, everlasting kingdom), and introduce **typology** through Jeremiah 31's promise of a **New Covenant**.
- **Week 5 (New Covenant / Jesus):** Present Jesus as the fulfillment: **New King + New Exodus + New Creation**. Connect Jesus' baptism, cross, burial, resurrection, and the Lord's Supper to covenant renewal and fulfillment, showing how all previous covenants culminate in the **blood-sealed New Covenant** and a discipleship life of "The Way" (already/new creation now, not yet/full restoration later).

Overall theme: God's covenants show both **continuity and development**—God's faithful pledge creates the covenant reality, and through each stage God shapes a covenant people until everything finds its fulfillment in Christ.

## Learning Objectives

### Week 1 — Universal Covenant (Creation → Fall → Flood → Babel)

- Retell Genesis 1–11 using the chaos → cosmos (order) → chaos pattern.
- Identify and articulate core covenant elements (parties, context, commands/conditions, sign, blessings/curses, fulfillment/breaking, uniqueness) in the Noahic covenant (Gen 6–9).
- Compare Noahic covenant elements with Genesis 1–3 and evaluate whether a pre-Noah/Adamic "covenant" framework is warranted (e.g., Hos 6:7).
- Distinguish Covenant of Works vs Covenant of Grace (and the alternative labels Covenant of Creation/Redemption) and explain what each emphasizes.
- Apply covenant themes to personal reflection on God's design, God's character, and covenant faithfulness.

### Week 2 — Ancestral Covenant (Abraham/Isaac/Jacob)

- Recount the patriarchal narratives (Gen 12–35) as "sojourner" stories tied to covenant establishment.
- Explain the meaning of the Gen 15 covenant-cutting ritual and God's unilateral passage through the pieces (unconditional divine commitment).
- Identify common covenantal elements across the covenant's renewal with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (Gen 12, 15, 17; 26; 28; 35).
- Summarize the Abrahamic covenant's key promises (descendants/land/blessing) and how obedience relates to enjoying covenant blessings.
- Connect circumcision as covenant sign to "circumcision of the heart" (Deut 30:6; Rom 2; Col 2) and articulate the movement from outward sign to inward renewal.
- Apply "sojourner mindset" and "being a blessing to the nations" to present-day discipleship.

### Week 3 — National Covenant (Mosaic/Sinaitic; Exodus Road)

- Retell the Exodus as a three-part pattern: way out (deliverance), way through (wilderness formation), way in (inheritance).
- Define exile as removal from God's presence and explain exodus as reversal/restoration of nearness to God.
- Identify covenant themes across Exodus: fulfillment of prior promises, covenant establishment at Sinai, and renewal.
- Describe the Mosaic covenant's identity goal: "kingdom of priests" and "holy nation" (Exo 19–24; 34).
- Explain the Exodus purpose as worship/service near God (Exo 3:12) and trace restoration of Yahweh's name/knowledge, presence (tabernacle), and promises (land).
- Apply wilderness-formation themes to spiritual growth, obedience, and struggles in "in-between" seasons.

### Week 4 — Messianic Covenant (Davidic) + Jeremiah's New Covenant (Typology preview)

- Recount the transition to kingship and the rise/fall context of Israel (Saul → David → Solomon) in relation to covenant story.
- Explain how Abrahamic promises are advanced/specified in the Davidic covenant (royal line, kingdom, land rest, mediated blessing).
- Recognize covenant reality even where the word "covenant" isn't in the origin narrative (2 Sam 7 / 1 Ch 17) and use later interpretive texts (Ps 89; Jer 33; Isa 55) to support the covenant reading.
- Identify covenantal elements of the Davidic covenant (house/temple, throne, dynasty, everlasting kingdom).
- Define typology (type/antitype) and begin mapping how Jeremiah 31 echoes earlier covenants (Noahic/Abrahamic/Mosaic/Davidic).
- Reflect on "dwelling in the house of God" and learn from the chaos–order–chaos pattern in Israel's history and exile.

### Week 5 — New Covenant (Jesus: New King, New Exodus, New Creation)

- Explain Luke 9's "exodus" language and summarize Jesus' mission as New King inaugurating New Creation through the New Exodus.
- Retell the gospel using the Exodus Road framework: way out (cross/Passover), way through (burial), way in (resurrection/new creation).
- Interpret Jesus' baptism as covenantally significant (Jordan crossing / Red Sea echoes) and as foreshadowing the "greater baptism" of the cross (Mark 10; Luke 12).
- Connect Passover, the Lord's Supper, and the Messianic banquet/kingdom feast imagery (Luke 14; Matt 22; Matt 26:29).
- Identify New Covenant elements (parties, sign, blessings, etc.) as a "blood-sealed covenant" (Luke 22; 1 Cor 11).
- Trace how prior covenants find fulfillment in Christ across key texts (Luke 1; Acts 2; Heb 8–10; 2 Cor 3; Rom 4–5; Gal 3–4; 1 Pet 3; 1 Cor 15).
- Articulate "already/not yet" salvation and apply covenant discipleship as "the Way" (Acts) to daily identity, purpose, and community practice.

### Appendix (Covenant Theology table) — integrative objectives

- Compare covenants across a consistent set of elements (context, mediator, parties, scope, sign, blessings, responsibilities, conditions, curses, fulfillment in Christ).
- Understand continuity and diversity across covenants, with emphasis on God's pledge and the formation of God's covenant people.

## 🏁 Final Project Assignment (optional but highly encouraged)

> 🚩 **Goal:** Integrate the course themes (Covenant, Kingdom, and the People of God) into a coherent, well-supported synthesis that shows both biblical-theological continuity and key developments across redemptive history.

### Prompt

You will produce a concise, structured synthesis that answers:

1. **How does "Kingdom" relate to each major biblical covenant (Adamic, Noahic, Abrahamic, Mosaic, Davidic, New Covenant)?**
2. **How do these covenants together form one unified story of God and his people?**
3. **What changes (and what remains consistent) as the story moves from covenant to covenant?**

### Deliverables

Choose **one** format:

- **Essay** (recommended): 1,500–2,500 words
- **Teaching outline**: 35–60 minutes of content + handout (1–2 pages)
- **Slide deck**: 12–18 slides (divider lines `---` can be used as slide breaks in Notion Presentation Mode)

### Required sections (include headings)

#### 1) Thesis (100–200 words)

State your core claim about how kingdom relates to covenant across the whole storyline.

#### 2) Covenant-by-covenant analysis (core of the project)

For **each** of the five covenants, include:

- **Context & scope** (Where does it occur? Who's involved? What problem is being addressed?)
- **Kingdom dimension** (rule/reign, realm, people, presence, mission)
- **People of God** (Who belongs? What marks them? What is their vocation?)
- **Sign / mediator / promises** (as applicable)
- **Forward-looking trajectory** (How does this covenant anticipate later fulfillment?)

Use a consistent mini-template so your analysis is easy to compare.

#### 3) Integrative synthesis

- Identify **3–5 major continuities** across the covenants (e.g., God's initiative, blessing-to-the-nations, presence, obedience/faith, mission).
- Identify **2–4 key developments** (e.g., expansion from family to nation to multiethnic people; law to Spirit; land to new creation; shadow to substance).

#### 4) Exegetical spotlight (choose 1 text)

Pick **one** passage that you think is pivotal for tying covenant and kingdom together. Examples:

- Genesis 12:1–3
- Exodus 19:4–6
- 2 Samuel 7
- Jeremiah 31:31–34
- Luke 22:14–20
- Acts 2
- Hebrews 8–10
- Revelation 21–22

Do a focused 600–900 word (or equivalent) analysis:

- What does the text say in context?
- How does it connect covenant themes to kingdom themes?
- How does it illuminate the identity/mission of God's people?

#### 5) Application / reflection (200–400 words)

Answer both:

- What is one implication for how you understand the church (or the people of God) today?
- What is one implication for your personal discipleship, mission, or spiritual formation?

### Sources & citation expectations

- Use the Bible as primary text.
- Interact with **at least 3 secondary sources** (class readings, lectures, or credible books/articles). Include a brief bibliography.

### Grading rubric (100 points)

- **Thesis clarity & coherence (15)**
- **Covenant-by-covenant accuracy (25)**
- **Kingdom integration (20)**
- **Synthesis: continuity & development (15)**
- **Exegetical spotlight quality (15)**
- **Organization, style, and documentation (10)**
