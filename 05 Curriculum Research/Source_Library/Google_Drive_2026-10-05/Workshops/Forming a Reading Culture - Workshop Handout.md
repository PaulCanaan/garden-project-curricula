# Forming a Reading Culture

A Workshop Handout

## Key Scripture

2 Timothy 3:14–17; Luke 24:25–27, 44–49

## Why a Reading Culture?

We read Scripture not just to gain information, but to be formed. A reading culture shapes how a community listens, hopes, and lives together in light of God’s story.

> “Serious and sustained Bible reading, at once reflective and practical, is the soul of theology and the beating heart of the body of Christ.” — Kevin Vanhoozer

## Philology — Our Posture Before the Text

Philology means paying close attention to the biblical text. It is a posture of humility and patience—listening carefully before speaking, allowing the text to say what it says before asking what it means for us.

## Christology — The Firm Foundation of the Word

Christology means that Jesus Christ is the center of the overarching storyline of the Bible. All of Scripture ultimately points to him, finds coherence in him, and is fulfilled through him.

## Inhabit the Story

The Bible is not merely a record of the past—it is a story we are invited to live inside. To inhabit the story is to see our lives shaped by the same God who acts within its pages.

### Biblical Theology — The Whole Story

Biblical theology reads the Bible as one unified story: creation, fall, redemption, and new creation. Rather than isolated verses, we trace the movement of God’s salvation purposes across time.

### Typology — A Unified Story

Typology recognizes patterns and echoes within Scripture. Earlier events, people, and institutions point forward to later fulfillments—ultimately culminating in Jesus Christ.

### Telos — Where the Story Is Going

Telos means “goal” or “destination”. The Bible tells a story that is going somewhere. History is moving toward the renewal of all things in Christ.

## A Frame of Reference

Our reading is shaped more by eschatology than sacramentality. This means we read Scripture in light of God’s promised future, not merely by locating divine presence in present symbols. Meaning flows from where God is taking the world into how we live now.

### Already / Not Yet

God’s kingdom has already begun through Jesus’ resurrection, but it is not yet complete. We live in hope, patience, and faithfulness, formed by the future God has promised.

### Pauline > Platonic

The Christian hope is not escape from the world, but the renewal of the world. Bodies, creation, and daily life matter because God intends to make all things new.

## What Kind of Reading Culture?

A reading culture forms a community into Christlikeness. We read together to grow in grace, wisdom, love, and hope—becoming a people shaped by God’s future.

> “But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To him be glory both now and forever! Amen.” — 2 Peter 3:18
