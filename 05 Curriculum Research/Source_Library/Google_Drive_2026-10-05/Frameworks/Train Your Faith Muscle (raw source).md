# Train Your Faith Muscle

People start physical training for different reasons. Some want to look bigger so they can feel valued. Some are driven by peer pressure. Others simply want to make a change. But is there a better way? What about faith?

Some people try to do “faith +”—faith plus influence, faith plus followers, and so on. But no—you need to be clear about what you are following. You are following Jesus, and you should seek to know what God has for you. Don’t just follow because others do. Experiencing Jesus is personal.

Physical training and training in godliness are both difficult, but rewarding. It takes persistence to lift weights and to run the race. However, His yoke is easy and His burden is light. Jesus shares our burdens.

There are many ways to work out, just as there are many denominations of Christianity, each with different focuses. Different approaches work for different people.

Not everyone realizes that physical training is also training of the mind, and not all believers understand what a relationship with God truly means. Lifters who focus only on outward appearance are like religious people who only want others to see what they have done.

Fitness is one of the few things where effort almost always leads to results. Often, you may not see changes in the short term, but every drop of sweat will reward you in some way in the future. “All things work together for good for those who love God.” Becoming a Christian is never easy, but sometimes the path of carrying the cross feels less difficult—not because the burden is lighter, but because we have grown stronger.

The fruits of the Spirit are the outcomes of faith. They are part of our testimony and may cause others to think, “I want to experience that.” Many people step into the gym for that reason—they want to be like someone else. That’s a great start. It takes faith to walk into a gym for the first time. But the truth is, you don’t have to become someone else. Eventually, you will become who God created you to be.

Church is also a judgment-free zone—not just Planet Fitness. Judging is God’s job, not ours. We tend to judge what we don’t understand, but we need to lay down that burden. Give it to God, trust Him, and rest.

God is strong in our weakness. I’m not afraid to share my weaknesses with Him because I know He is for me, not against me. He will never leave you nor forsake you, and He is more patient than any personal trainer. Even more, God doesn’t charge you—His truth and grace are free. His Word, which gives guidance for every aspect of life, is also free. Free indeed.

Theology alone doesn’t set you free—you have to start walking in faith. Simply holding on to knowledge about lifting doesn’t make you strong—you have to actually lift the weight. When knowledge is practiced, it becomes wisdom. Wisdom is also knowing how to apply knowledge appropriately. There is so much truth and wisdom in the Bible. Take advantage of it. Equip yourself with His Word—that is the key to becoming stronger.

In fitness, you may sometimes hit a plateau, where no matter what you try, you can’t seem to improve. For Christians, plateaus often come when facing fear and doubt. Breaking through often requires trying new methods, and God will also make a way for us.

Willpower works—until it doesn’t. Just like muscles reach failure after too many repetitions. That’s why a spotter is helpful for heavy lifting, especially when attempting new personal records. An ideal spotter is stronger and more experienced than you—someone who can catch you when you fail and encourage you when you break through. The Holy Spirit is the ultimate spotter, and we can truly rely on Him.

True transformation happens from the inside out. The world focuses on outward change, but God works inwardly first—renewing the heart and mind, which then reshapes our actions. Real, lasting growth is not behavior modification, but identity transformation.

“Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind” (Romans 12:2). And “Be strong and courageous… for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go” (Joshua 1:9). These aren’t just verses to memorize—they are truths to live by. Renewal requires intentionality, and courage is required to step into that renewal daily.

Discipline is the bridge between intention and transformation. Motivation may get you started, but discipline keeps you going when feelings fade. Just like training your body, spiritual growth requires showing up consistently—even when you don’t feel like it.

Growth also requires both stretching and resting. Muscles don’t grow during the workout—they grow during recovery. In the same way, faith is strengthened not only through challenges, but also through seasons of rest in God. Knowing when to push and when to be still is part of maturity.

Pride can quietly sabotage both physical and spiritual growth. It makes us compare, perform, and pretend. It keeps us from admitting weakness and receiving help. But humility opens the door for real growth, because God gives grace to the humble.

In the race of faith, everyone is at a different stage. As the saying goes, “闻道有先后” (there is an order in hearing and learning the truth). Some are just starting, some are pressing through, and others are mentoring along the way. The key is not comparison, but faithfulness in your current position.

Peace of mind is one of the greatest outcomes of a life aligned with God. It’s not the absence of challenges, but the presence of assurance. When your mind is anchored in truth, external circumstances lose their power to shake you.

There is always a better way—the Way of God. The world offers shortcuts and quick fixes, but God’s way produces lasting fruit. It may not always be the easiest path, but it is always the most meaningful and life-giving.

In physical training, muscle growth involves breakdown and rebuilding—almost like the cells are being “born again” stronger. Spiritually, we also need to be renewed and reborn. But growth requires good soil. A heart that is open, receptive, and rooted in truth allows God to cultivate lasting change.

Every journey starts small. You don’t begin by lifting the heaviest weights—you start where you are. The same is true in faith. Small steps of obedience, repeated daily, lead to significant transformation over time.

Consistency matters more than intensity. One great workout won’t change your body, just like one spiritual high won’t transform your life. But steady, faithful practice—day after day—builds strength that lasts.

Exercising your “faith muscle” means putting your trust into action. Faith isn’t passive—it’s lived out through decisions, perseverance, and obedience. The more you use it, the stronger it becomes.  
We also need to be careful about what we take in—both physically and mentally. Just as nutrition affects your body, what you consume mentally shapes your thoughts and beliefs. Filtering what you watch, listen to, and dwell on is essential for healthy growth.

Endorphins last longer than dopamine. Dopamine chases quick rewards and instant gratification, but endorphins are built through sustained effort and bring deeper satisfaction. In the same way, a life built on fleeting pleasures will leave you empty, but a life rooted in discipline, purpose, and faith produces lasting joy.
