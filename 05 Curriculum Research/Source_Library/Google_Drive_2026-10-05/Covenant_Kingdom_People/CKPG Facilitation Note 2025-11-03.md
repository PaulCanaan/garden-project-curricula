**Facilitation Note 11/03/25**

1. What is a covenant?  
- A chosen relationship/partnership in which two parties make binding promises/pledges to each other for specific purposes. (Binding agreement, conditions, signs)

2. The narratives/storyline:  
- who  
- what  
- when  
- where  
- why

3. Blessings & Curses - life-giving or life-taking  
- How many times does the word "blessing"  show up in Genesis 12:1-3?  
- The fivefold repetition of the word blessing is deliberately set in opposition to the fivefold occurrence of the word curse in Genesis 1-11 (Gen 3:14, 17; 4:11; 5:29; 9:25).

4. Adamic Covenant - the Covenant of Creation  
- The creation order: God, humanity, and the rest of the creation.  
- In the Fall, autonomy leads to alienation, between humans and God, creation, and each other.  
- Doubt-desire-disobedience  
- Guilt/shame/fear  
- Autonomy leads to alienation.  
- There's hope for redemption in the blessings and curses.  
(The Lord God said to the serpent) "...I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel."(Genesis 3:15)

- "The Tree of Life" is the last phrase in Genesis 3, which is highly connected to the Adamic Covenant (obedience, blessing, life).

5. Noahic Covenant - the Covenant of Preservation  
- Creation and fall served as the contexts for redemption and restoration. The gospel in a nutshell.  
- The ark landed on Mountain Ararat. Ararat = "the curse reversed: precipitation of curse"  
- [Genesis 8:21] The Lord smelled the pleasing aroma and said in his heart: “Never again will I curse the ground because of humans, even though every inclination of the human heart is evil from childhood. And never again will I destroy all living creatures, as I have done."  
- The foundational nature of human beings hadn’t changed since the flood. The intention of the heart was still defiled by evil. Starting over again wouldn’t lead to Eden.

6. Abrahamic Covenant - the Covenant of Promise  
- Abraham is asked to give up all the symbols of security and autonomy with which the builders of Babel sought to store up their own identity.  
- At Babel, people had sought to make a great name for themselves, but God promises that he will make a great name for Abraham and his descendants through their involvement with him and dependence on him.

7. What do Adam, Noah, and Abraham have in common?  
- They walk with God  
- They are blessed (with "be fruitful and multiply") and commanded/called by God  
- Given a new world: Garden & Promised Land  
- A new covenant, new start, but the same purpose of God

8. Why should not Abraham himself possess the land of promise immediately?  
(Only God's grace to sinful men provides inadequate [sic] response to this question)  
-The possession of this “inheritance” was not dependent on death, but on covenantal faithfulness. (Blessing > curse)

9. What did God do in the covenant-inauguration procedure/covenant ratification ceremony (Genesis 15)? What's the significance of His action?  
- Only God himself passes between the pieces. By this action, God promises. The Lord assumes to himself the full responsibility for seeing that every promise of the covenant shall be realized.  
- By the inherent pledge to death of the covenant-inauguration procedure, a “bond in blood” is established.

10. From a "covenant" to a "testament": pledge of life and death.  
- Testament: the document/record or expression (a will) of that covenantal relationship.  
- In legal terms, “Covenant” = two living parties agree.  
“Testament” = one person’s declaration, effective at death.

11. The conversation between God and Abraham:  
- Abraham was called to leave his fatherland (Genesis 12:1)  
- God's blessing and promise (Genesis 12:2-3)  
- The pleading voice of the patriarch: "How can I know? How can I be sure?"  
- The solemn ceremony of covenantal self-malediction provides the Lord’s reply: “I promise. I solemnly commit myself as Almighty God. Death may be necessary. But the promises of the covenant shall be fulfilled.”  
- God gives a sign that seals the covenant and Abraham's faithful response (Genesis 17:1, 14).

12. The covenantal sign of the Abrahamic Covenant.  
- The outward sign of cleansing symbolized the inner purification necessary for a life of obedience and love to God.  
- A begining of something greater. The covenant ritual of sealing finds its fulfillment in the new covenant reality of sealing.

13. Abraham, Isaac, Jacob: stories of family reconciliations  
- Abraham and his descendants are all too human (far from being perfect), and as they journey with God they're gradually shaped into people fit to bear the promise.  
- God will work through such people to bring blessing to the world, but first he has to work in them and with them, to bring reconciliation and maturity.  
- The people of God have to be formed to be like God, to become like the promise they carry.
