# Introduction Session - The Seed and the Soil

*Entering the Story of Planted.*

*Planted.* begins with a seed.

The seed of Jesus.

The seed of the kingdom.

The seed of faith.

A seed may look small and ordinary, but it carries life within it. Once it enters the soil, something hidden begins. Roots reach downward before anything appears above the ground. Growth comes slowly, quietly, and often invisibly.

Jesus once told a story about a farmer who went out to sow.

## The Parable of the Sower

Read **Matthew 13:1–9** aloud.

As Jesus tells the story, the same seed falls in four different places. Some lands on a hardened path. Some falls on shallow, rocky ground. Some begins to grow among thorns. Some falls into good soil and produces a harvest.

The sower is generous. He scatters widely. The seed is good wherever it falls. Yet the life hidden within the seed is received differently by each soil.

Pause before reading Jesus’ explanation.

What did you notice?

* What remained the same throughout the story?  
* What changed?  
* What prevented the seed from growing?  
* What allowed it to become fruitful?  
* Which soil draws your attention most?

Leave the questions open for a moment. The meaning of the soils will become clearer after stepping into another kind of landscape.

## The Grid

A large grid has been marked on the floor. Its quadrants are unlabeled.

In one space, participants are asked to perform—to keep moving, maintain good form, and appear cheerful while doing it. In another, they turn away, close themselves off, and repeat words of withdrawal. In a third, snacks and comforts are plentiful, but there is no direction except to keep consuming.

Participants may move between the spaces, but wherever they go, they remain within the experience of that quadrant.

After some time, everyone is invited into the remaining space.

There is no performance to maintain. No need to disappear. No pressure to keep consuming. There is room to breathe, to be present, and to notice what the other spaces have done within us.

Talk together:

* How did each space feel when you first entered it?  
* Did the feeling change after staying there?  
* Which space felt most familiar?  
* Where do these patterns appear in relationships, churches, families, or workplaces?  
* What might the final space represent?

The Grid gives shape to several ways people learn to live.

Some live under constant pressure to prove themselves.

Some protect themselves by withdrawing.

Some seek comfort without direction or transformation.

But there is another way—a place where people can be fully seen, genuinely loved, and faithfully invited to grow.

## Grace and Truth

Read **John 1:14**:

> “The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us. We have seen his glory, the glory of the one and only Son, who came from the Father, full of grace and truth.”

Grace and truth are not merely two values that need to be balanced. They meet in the person of Jesus.

**Truth** names reality honestly. It reveals who God is, who we are, what is good, what has gone wrong, and where change is needed.

**Grace** meets us without demanding that we first repair ourselves. It receives, forgives, restores, and gives what we could never earn.

Truth without grace can become pressure, performance, and condemnation.

Grace without truth can become comfort without transformation.

Without either, people often retreat into distance, indifference, or isolation.

But grace and truth together create a different kind of environment:

> You are fully seen and deeply loved.

> You do not need to hide.

> You are welcome here—and you are invited to grow.

This is the atmosphere *Planted.* seeks to cultivate.

The Grace–Truth Matrix is not a way to label people. It is a lens for recognizing the environments we inhabit and create. It helps us ask:

* Are we being honest about reality?  
* Are we responding with the grace of Jesus?  
* Are people being loved without being left unchanged?  
* Are they being challenged without being condemned?

## Return to the Soil

Now read **Matthew 13:18–23**.

Jesus explains that the seed is the message of the kingdom.

Some hear it, but it never enters deeply. Some receive it quickly, but without roots they cannot endure difficulty. Some begin to grow, but worries, desires, and competing loves crowd the life from them. Others hear, understand, remain, and eventually bear fruit.

The soils should not become fixed labels for four kinds of people. We may find all four conditions within ourselves.

One part of the heart may be open while another remains hard.

We may receive something joyfully but resist the slow work of rootedness.

Faith may be alive yet crowded by anxiety, hurry, or the desire for control.

Soil can be cultivated.

Hard ground can be broken open.

Shallow soil can deepen.

Thorns can be cleared.

Roots can grow.

Grace welcomes us into that work. Truth shows us where the work is needed.

And growth takes time.

A seed does not become a tree in a day. Faith is nurtured through grace and truth, Scripture and prayer, suffering and hope, solitude and community. Much of its deepest work happens beneath the surface before it becomes visible.

## Entering the Story

The story of *Planted.* begins in a garden.

Humanity is created for life, relationship, and good purpose. Yet sin brings darkness, alienation, and exile. Jesus enters the broken world, passes through suffering and death, and rises as the firstfruits of new creation.

The seed that seemed buried was planted.

Through the life of Jesus, faith takes root. Through grace and truth over time, it grows. A tree does not remain alone; trees become a forest. The forest bears fruit, and the fruit carries seeds.

This is the journey ahead:

* God planted a garden out of delight;  
* humanity made to flourish;  
* exile east of Eden;  
* the faithful Son who crushed the serpent;  
* the Seed who must die;  
* the Firstfruits who rise;  
* the Gospel of the Kingdom;  
* eternity planted in the present;  
* a forest growing together;  
* and a people sent to plant again.

No one enters this story with perfect soil.

We enter with questions, wounds, hopes, resistance, faith, doubt, and unfinished stories. The invitation is not to pretend otherwise. It is to come honestly and allow the Gardener to work.

Take a moment to reflect:

> What do I hope God will grow in me during this journey?

> What may be hard, shallow, or crowded within me?

> Where do I need truth?

> Where do I need grace?

Participants may write privately, share with someone they trust, or simply hold the questions before God.

## A Prayer for the Soil

God our Gardener,

thank you for sowing your word generously.

Soften what has become hard.

Deepen what has remained shallow.

Clear what has become crowded.

Teach us to receive your truth without fear

and your grace without taking it for granted.

Root us in Jesus.

Form us through your Spirit.

Give us patience with hidden growth

and grace for one another along the way.

Let the seed of your kingdom take root,

grow strong,

and bear fruit in our lives.

Amen.

> The seed has been sown.

> Let us enter the story with open hearts.
