# The Story of Planted.

## Chapter 1 There Is a Garden Named Delight

The story of Planted. begins with a farmer went out to sow his seed…. 

“A farmer went out to sow his seed. As he was scattering the seed, some fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up. Some fell on rocky places, where it did not have much soil. It sprang up quickly, because the soil was shallow. But when the sun came up, the plants were scorched, and they withered because they had no root. Other seed fell among thorns, which grew up and choked the plants. Still other seed fell on good soil, where it produced a crop—a hundred, sixty or thirty times what was sown. Whoever has ears, let them hear.” (Matthew 13:3-9) 

Truth is like light, which is the food of plants. Come into the light, you will no longer walk in darkness.   
Grace is like water, which is refreshing to the soul. But too much water sometimes can cause root rot. 

Truth without grace is mean; grace without truth is meaningless; but truth and grace together, is medicine. 

With the lens of grace and truth, we dived into the opening chapters of the Bible, where we saw a beautiful garden. 

God revealed Himself to us as Elohim in Genesis 1—the all powerful God; and Yahweh in General 2 [sic]—the relational God, or the all loving Father. 

We discovered that the pre-creation state was “tohu wa-bohu”, and God brought the chaos into order. 

We saw Trinity in Genesis 1—God, Spirit of God, Word of God. 

We saw that there’s a *forward symmetry* structure in the 6-day creation encounter—day 1-3 separating the realms and day 4-6 filling those realms—and with day 7 as the climax of the creation 

There was a bonus creation on day 3—plants; and on day 6 God also created two things. The bonus creation on day 6 is humans—whom God created in His own image. 

When the whole picture of creation was completed, God called it “very good”. God takes delight in His good and beautiful creation. 

In chapter 1 of *Planted*, we learned both the Grace-Truth Matrix and S.O.A.P. Bible study framework (Scripture/Observation/Application/Prayer)

Genesis 2 retells the creation account and zooms into a garden. The garden was planted in the east of Eden, and the name of the land means “pleasure” or “delight” in Hebrew. 

“A river watering the garden flows from Eden”. The land of Eden is a high place, a mountain-top, which is related to the temple image. The garden is also a temple-garden, Its location correlates to the Holy of Holies (God’s dwelling place) in the east of the temple. 

God formed the very first man from the dust, and his name Adam simply means “human”, “mankind”. 

God saw it was not good for man to be alone, so He formed a helper (“ezer” in Hebrew) for Adam. Later we know the name of the woman is Eve, which means “life” or “to live”. 

Keep in mind, both of their names are abstract concepts in Hebrew language context. But we get the picture here, in the Garden of Delight, humans enjoying life in God’s presence. 

What does it mean to be human? We had a devotional Bible study on Psalm 139 and how we are fearfully and wonderfully made, and fully known and truly loved by God. 

God gives humans identity and dignity, and He also gives us the purpose of life. In the Temple-Garden, humans served as priest-kings. The calling of Adam was to work and keep the garden. The blessing/command they received was “be fruitful and multiply, fill the earth and subdue it.” 

Eden was unspoiled and also unfinished, and the ongoing process is the Kingdom project of God. He invites humans to partner with Him to advance His Kingdom so that the whole earth can be His dwelling place and enjoy His presence. God could make a tree, but He chooses to plant a seed. 

## Chapter 2 Devil Not Today

Jesus told them another parable: “The kingdom of heaven is like a man who sowed good seed in his field. But while everyone was sleeping, his enemy came and sowed weeds among the wheat, and went away. When the wheat sprouted and formed heads, then the weeds also appeared…. ” (Matthew 13:24-26)

There is another sower later in the story but he only sows weeds…. It explained why the world we live in today feels like anything but the Garden of Delight. 

Genesis 1 to 3 lay the foundation of Christian worldview. In this chapter of *Planted*, we were like detectives discovering what was really going on in the story of the Fall. 

Salvation has a context: what we are saved *from* & what we are saved *for*. If sin doesn’t make sense, salvation won’t make sense. 

In the program of Dartboard, we learned that sin (Greek: harmatia) is “missing the mark” (harmatanein). Sin is a human condition—we are all broken and we are broken in different ways. 

We saw guilt, shame, and fear come out of Adam and Eve’s act of disobedience, and how autonomy leads to alienation. Human effort to cover their sin, and how God made sacrifice and clothed them. 

Relating to the two trees: Knowing good vs. knowing good and evil (touch—experiential knowledge). God setting boundary before the Tree of Life as an act of grace (not letting humans’ spiritual death become a permanent state). 

This is the chapter that we were enduring till the good part. After discovering what sin is, where sin is from, and the consequences of sin, we turned our gaze to Jesus—the sinless and spotless Lamb of God. He is the only way out. 

Jesus was tempted in all ways but he never sinned (Hebrews 4:15). Temptation is not sin, only when you say “yes” to it, and continue to say “yes” to it, it becomes sin. 

In the account of Jesus being tempted in the wilderness, we heard the similar crafty voice which twisted the truth and sowed the seed of doubt. 

Comparing Jesus' and Eve's responses to the temptation, we discovered some common raw materials that the enemy uses to attack: the desires of the eyes, the desires of the flesh, the pride of life (1 John 2:16). 

Jesus won the first battle in the spiritual war, which leaves the example of how we fight our battles in spiritual warfare. 

What I have to point out here is that we can’t think ourselves out of sins. Not sinning is not just saying “no” to the temptations, but also putting our hearts under the Holy Spirit’s training. 

Our faith is not a religion about sin management. There is so much more in what we are “saved for” than what we are “saved from”. Christianity does not provide us ways to save ourselves, but points us to a Savior.
