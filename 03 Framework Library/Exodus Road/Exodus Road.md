# Exodus Road

## Purpose

The Exodus Road is a framework for seeing salvation as a journey of liberation, formation, and communion rather than only a moment of rescue.

## The three movements

### 1. The way out — deliverance

God breaks the power of bondage and brings a people out of slavery, sin, death, and exile.

- Original exodus: Egypt and the Red Sea
- Gospel fulfillment: the death of Jesus, the Passover Lamb
- Personal and communal question: What are we being freed from?

### 2. The way through — formation

God teaches a delivered people to trust, worship, obey, and live together in the wilderness between bondage and inheritance.

- Original exodus: wilderness, Sinai, Torah, and tabernacle
- Gospel fulfillment: the burial of Jesus and passage through death
- Christian life: sanctification, testing, dependence, and practices of the Way
- Personal and communal question: Who are we becoming?

### 3. The way in — communion and vocation

God brings a formed people into promised life, presence, inheritance, and participation in his purposes.

- Original exodus: entry into the promised land
- Gospel fulfillment: resurrection and new-creation life
- Final horizon: resurrection, glorification, and the renewal of all things
- Personal and communal question: What are we being saved for?

## Theological logic

| Movement | Salvation | Formation | Presence | Mission |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Way out | Deliverance | Separation from old mastery | God comes near to rescue | Witness to God's saving power |
| Way through | Sanctification | Trust, obedience, worship | God dwells with a pilgrim people | Priestly identity is learned |
| Way in | Inheritance | Mature participation | Communion in promised life | Cultivation and blessing |

## Already and not yet

Christ has completed the decisive exodus, yet his people still journey toward final resurrection. The church lives between deliverance accomplished and restoration consummated.

See [[Already Not Yet]].

## Use cases

- Explaining the full scope of salvation
- Structuring discipleship and formation
- Interpreting wilderness experiences
- Connecting baptism, communion, and Christian hope
- Designing courses, retreats, or spiritual practices
- Evaluating ministries that emphasize rescue but neglect formation or vocation

## Guardrails

- The framework is a biblical pattern, not a rigid timeline for every experience.
- Wilderness should not be romanticized; suffering and testing require pastoral care.
- “Way in” does not promise present ease or territorial entitlement.
- Grace grounds every movement; formation is not self-salvation.
