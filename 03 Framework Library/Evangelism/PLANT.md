# PLANT

## Purpose

PLANT is a repeatable discernment and action loop for discipleship, evangelism, ministry design, and personal growth. It prevents both action without attention and reflection without response.

## Components

### P — Pray

Begin in dependence. Offer the situation to God, ask for wisdom and love, and release the need to control the outcome.

### L — Listen

Attend to Scripture, the Spirit, people, context, pain, opportunity, and wise counsel. Ask before assuming.

### A — Articulate

Put the discernment into clear words: What seems true? What is God inviting? What problem or opportunity is actually present?

### N — Next steps

Choose the smallest faithful action that can be taken, owned, and reviewed. Name who, what, when, and what support is needed.

### T — Try again

Act, observe the fruit, receive feedback, repent or revise where needed, and return to prayer. Faithfulness includes learning.

## Sequence or logic

**Pray → Listen → Articulate → Next step → Try and return**

The final movement makes the framework a loop rather than a one-time plan.

## Biblical and theological grounding

- Luke 10:1–12 — prayer, attentive presence, contextual engagement, and mission
- Acts 13:1–3 — worship, listening, communal discernment, and sending
- Acts 16:6–10 — guidance, interpretation, action, and revision
- James 1:5, 19–25 — ask for wisdom, listen, and do the word
- 1 Corinthians 3:5–9 — planting and watering while God gives growth

## Worked example

A group senses concern for isolated neighbors. They **pray**, **listen** through conversations, **articulate** the need as lack of low-pressure connection, name a **next step** of one shared meal, then **try again** by reviewing who came, who was missed, and what love requires next.

## Use cases

- Evangelism and spiritual conversations
- Personal discernment
- Small ministry experiments
- Team planning and retrospective review
- Turning study application into action

## Limits and failure modes

- Listening must be tested; not every impression is divine guidance.
- “Next steps” should not rush grief, trauma, or complex decisions.
- Trying again is not permission to persist in an unsafe or unethical action.
- The model needs clear authority and escalation paths in organizational settings.
