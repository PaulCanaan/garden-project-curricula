# Know Grow Go

## Purpose

Know Grow Go is a simple discipleship model that integrates relationship with God, personal and communal formation, and outward participation in God's mission.

## Components

### Know God

- **Question:** Who is God, and how are we learning to live in relationship with him?
- **Emphasis:** revelation, Scripture, prayer, worship, intimacy, and trust
- **Correction:** knowledge is relational and obedient, not information alone

### Grow in Christ and live on purpose

- **Question:** Who are we becoming, and what fruit is taking form?
- **Emphasis:** identity, character, habits, gifts, healing, community, and vocation
- **Correction:** growth is grace-rooted and Spirit-enabled, not self-manufactured

### Go in love and mission

- **Question:** Whom are we being sent to serve, bless, disciple, and invite?
- **Emphasis:** witness, justice, hospitality, evangelism, culture-making, and multiplication
- **Correction:** going flows from presence with Christ and love of neighbor, not pressure to perform

## Sequence or logic

The words form a memorable movement, but not a one-way pipeline. Going often deepens knowing; service exposes areas for growth; growing in community reveals God more fully.

## Biblical and theological grounding

- John 17:3, 18–23 — knowing God, shared life, and being sent
- Matthew 28:18–20 — disciples who are baptized, taught, and sent
- Ephesians 4:1–16 — communal growth into Christ for shared ministry
- Philippians 3:8–16 — knowing Christ, pressing toward maturity, living from what has been received
- 2 Peter 1:3–8 — knowledge of God expressed in fruitful character

## Worked example

A six-week group devotes two weeks to knowing God through a Gospel, two to practicing a Christlike virtue in community, and two to serving and sharing faith locally. Every gathering still includes all three dimensions: encounter, formation, and mission.

## Use cases

- Discipleship pathway design
- Group or course learning outcomes
- Personal rule-of-life review
- Ministry health assessment

## Limits and failure modes

- “Know” must not be reduced to cognition.
- “Grow” must not become endless inward focus.
- “Go” must not instrumentalize people or reward visible activity over love.
- The model needs richer frameworks for trauma care, doctrine, ecclesiology, and organizational design.
