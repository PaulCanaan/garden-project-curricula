# Garden Framework

## Purpose

The Garden Framework integrates creation, formation, vocation, church, and new creation through the biblical image of a garden. It helps people ask not only what they should accomplish, but what God has planted, what conditions foster faithful growth, and whom the fruit is meant to serve.

## Core insight

God gives life and growth; human beings receive, tend, cultivate, and share. Christian agency is participatory stewardship rather than control.

## Movements

### Receive the garden

Creation begins as gift. Identity, place, relationships, capacities, limits, and time are received before they are managed.

### Attend to the soil

Growth depends on conditions: truth, grace, attention, community, habits, suffering, rest, and the work of the Spirit. Diagnose the environment, not only the visible plant.

### Cultivate faithfully

To cultivate is to draw out God-given potential through patient work, order, protection, pruning, repair, and creativity.

### Bear fruit

Fruit is mature life offered for God and neighbor: character, beauty, justice, provision, healing, hospitality, witness, and good work.

### Multiply and replant

Healthy fruit carries seed. Mature disciples, practices, ideas, institutions, and communities reproduce life without demanding uniformity.

### Anticipate the city-garden

Cultivation points toward new creation, where the garden becomes a city-garden and the tree's leaves serve the healing of the nations.

## Sequence or logic

**Gift → attention → cultivation → fruit → multiplication → renewal**

The sequence is seasonal rather than linear. Some seasons emphasize rooting, pruning, dormancy, harvest, or replanting.

## Biblical and theological grounding

- Genesis 1–2 — image-bearing, garden vocation, and Sabbath
- John 15:1–17 — abiding, pruning, and fruit
- 1 Corinthians 3:5–9 — people plant and water; God gives growth
- Galatians 5:22–25 — the Spirit's fruit
- Revelation 22:1–5 — tree, fruit, healing, and renewed vocation

## Worked example

For a new ministry, first name what has already been given (**receive**), study the people and conditions (**soil**), choose a few faithful practices (**cultivate**), define fruit in terms of love and maturity rather than attendance alone (**fruit**), apprentice others (**multiply**), and connect the work to God's renewing purpose (**anticipate**).

## Limits and failure modes

- Organic growth does not eliminate planning, accountability, or decisive action.
- “Fruit” must not be reduced to visibility, scale, or success.
- Pruning language can be spiritually abusive when used to rationalize avoidable harm.
- Not every delay is dormancy, and not every failure is a lack of faith.
