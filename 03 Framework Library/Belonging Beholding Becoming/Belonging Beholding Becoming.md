# Belonging Beholding Becoming

## Purpose

This framework keeps Christian formation from collapsing into information transfer, isolated self-improvement, or behavior management. People are formed as they receive a covenant identity, direct their attention toward Christ, and participate in Spirit-enabled transformation within a community.

## Core insight

We become like the one we behold, but beholding is sustained by belonging. Belonging, beholding, and becoming are mutually reinforcing dimensions, not a rigid three-step sequence.

## Components

### Belonging — whose are we, and with whom do we live?

- **Meaning:** receiving identity in Christ and life among God's people
- **Function:** creates safety for truth, repentance, practice, and perseverance
- **Practices:** baptismal identity, table fellowship, hospitality, confession, mutual care
- **Distortion:** belonging without truth becomes sentimentality, conformity, or avoidance of growth

### Beholding — what holds our attention and affection?

- **Meaning:** sustained attention to God as revealed in Christ through Scripture and the Spirit
- **Function:** reshapes imagination and desire by giving the heart a truer and more beautiful vision
- **Practices:** Scripture, worship, contemplation, prayer, testimony, communion
- **Distortion:** beholding without belonging can become private spirituality or disembodied admiration

### Becoming — what kind of people are we being formed to be?

- **Meaning:** growing into Christlikeness in character, practice, vocation, and witness
- **Function:** gives embodied expression to identity and attention
- **Practices:** obedience, habits, service, reconciliation, stewardship, mission
- **Distortion:** becoming without grace becomes performance or self-optimization

## Logic

Belonging gives a place from which to behold. Beholding gives a vision of whom to become. Becoming deepens shared life and trains attention, renewing the cycle.

## Biblical and theological grounding

- John 15:1–17 — abiding, love, fruit, and friendship
- 2 Corinthians 3:18 — beholding the Lord and being transformed
- Ephesians 2:19–22 — belonging to God's household and being built together
- Colossians 3:1–17 — identity, renewed attention, new character, and communal practice
- 1 John 3:1–3 — beloved identity, future vision, and present purification

## Worked example

A group studying forgiveness does more than explain the doctrine. It names participants as forgiven people in Christ (**belonging**), attends to Jesus' costly mercy (**beholding**), and practices confession, release of resentment, boundary-setting, and reconciliation where wise (**becoming**).

## Use cases

- Designing groups, classes, retreats, and discipleship pathways
- Evaluating whether a ministry integrates community, Scripture, and practice
- Diagnosing stalled formation
- Structuring teaching applications

## Limits and failure modes

- Do not treat the terms as chronological stages or personality types.
- Belonging must not excuse unsafe behavior or erase pastoral boundaries.
- Beholding is not mere visualization; Christ is encountered through revelation, worship, and obedient attention.
- Becoming is the fruit of grace and the Spirit, not a project of earning acceptance.
