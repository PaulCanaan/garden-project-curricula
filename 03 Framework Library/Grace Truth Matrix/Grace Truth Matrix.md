# Grace Truth Matrix

## Purpose

The Grace Truth Matrix is a diagnostic lens for Scripture, relationships, leadership, and pastoral decisions. It asks whether a response is both truthful and loving, taking Jesus—full of grace and truth—as the center rather than treating the two as opposing values.

## Axes

- **Truth:** honest contact with reality under God's revelation—what is good, broken, responsible, possible, and needed
- **Grace:** unearned favor expressed as welcome, patience, mercy, generosity, and power for change

## Quadrants

### High truth, low grace — performance and condemnation

Standards are clear, but people are reduced to success, failure, or compliance. Likely fruit: fear, hiding, pride, exhaustion, or harshness.

### Low truth, low grace — isolation and disengagement

Neither reality nor relationship is faced. Likely fruit: avoidance, numbness, fragmentation, or abandonment.

### Low truth, high grace — indulgence and sentimentality

Welcome is offered without honest naming, boundaries, or direction. Likely fruit: stagnation, enabling, confusion, or superficial peace.

### High truth, high grace — transforming love

Reality is named within steadfast love. People can be fully known without being discarded and deeply loved without being left unchanged.

## Dynamic dimension — time

Formation usually occurs as grace and truth are received and practiced over time. A helpful summary from *Planted* is: **growth = (grace + truth) × time**. This is a teaching metaphor, not a measurable formula.

## Discernment questions

1. What is true about God, the person, the situation, the harm, and the hope?
2. What would gracious love require here?
3. Where am I tempted toward condemnation, avoidance, or indulgence?
4. What truthful and gracious boundary, invitation, confession, or next step is needed?
5. What must be allowed to unfold over time?

## Biblical and theological grounding

- John 1:14–18 — Jesus full of grace and truth
- John 8:1–11, 31–32 — mercy, freedom, and a truthful call to change
- Ephesians 2:8–10 — saved by grace for a formed life of good works
- Ephesians 4:15–16 — speaking truth in love toward maturity
- 1 Corinthians 13:1–6 — knowledge and power are nothing without love; love rejoices with truth

## Worked example

A leader addressing repeated unreliability names the missed commitments and their impact (**truth**), listens for circumstances and affirms the person's worth (**grace**), establishes a realistic boundary (**truth and grace**), and agrees on support and a review point (**time**).

## Limits and failure modes

- Grace and truth are not quantities that can be objectively scored.
- “Truth” must not become a label for personal bluntness or untested interpretation.
- “Grace” does not require continued exposure to abuse or the removal of consequences.
- The matrix supports judgment; it does not replace Scripture, pastoral wisdom, safeguarding, or professional care.
