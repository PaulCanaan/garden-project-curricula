# The Grid

## Outcome

Participants identify how performance, withdrawal, indulgence, and transforming love appear in their relationships, then practice discerning a response that holds grace and truth together.

## Materials

- Painter's tape or soft rope to mark four floor quadrants
- Four quadrant signs
- Chairs and water
- Optional individually packaged snacks displayed as symbols, not required for consumption
- Timer

## Room map

|  | Lower grace | Higher grace |
|---|---|---|
| **Higher truth** | Performance / condemnation | Transforming love |
| **Lower truth** | Isolation / disengagement | Indulgence / sentimentality |

The original activity leaves the high-grace/high-truth quadrant empty until the debrief.

## Facilitator flow

### 1. Frame — 3 minutes

Explain that participants will explore postures rather than play roles perfectly. Anyone may observe, use a chair, change movements, or step out at any time. No food or exercise is required.

### 2. Explore three quadrants — 9 minutes

Use 90-second to two-minute rounds, with a pause between them.

- **Performance:** repeat a simple task while monitoring form and appearance. Accessible alternatives include stacking cards, writing perfectly, or a seated hand pattern.
- **Isolation:** turn slightly away and reduce interaction. Do not require participants to cover senses or repeat rejecting phrases.
- **Indulgence:** choose freely among harmless comforts or tokens while ignoring the shared task. Snacks may be selected but never must be eaten.

Participants may notice a different emotional effect over time, but the facilitator should not pressure anyone to remain uncomfortable.

### 3. Enter the open quadrant — 3 minutes

Invite everyone into the fourth quadrant. Allow rest, eye contact, and unhurried presence. Ask what feels different before naming the matrix.

### 4. Debrief — 15–25 minutes

1. What did you notice first in each quadrant? What changed with time?
2. Which posture felt familiar, attractive, or draining?
3. What might each quadrant represent in relationships with family, friends, coworkers, church, or God?
4. What do the two axes represent?
5. What makes high grace and high truth different from simply balancing niceness and honesty?
6. Where might transforming love require welcome, confession, a boundary, patience, or change?

Read John 1:14 and Ephesians 4:15. Connect the open quadrant to Jesus' fullness of grace and truth.

## Integration

Invite each participant to complete privately:

- Under pressure I tend toward…
- Grace would invite me to…
- Truth would require me to…
- One faithful next step is…

Close in prayer without requiring public disclosure.

## Safety and accessibility

The original guide used sustained burpees, sensory blocking, compulsory eating, and tossing food. This library version intentionally removes those elements because they can create physical risk, food-related harm, humiliation, or exclusion.

- Ask about allergies before bringing food; keep food optional and sealed.
- Provide seated and nonphysical equivalents.
- Never require closing eyes, covering ears, rejecting others, or continued activity without breaks.
- Avoid labeling a participant as a quadrant.

## Limits

The quadrants illustrate tendencies, not diagnoses. Grace and truth are not measurable quantities, and real pastoral situations require context, wise authority, and safeguarding.
