# The Bond

## Outcome

Participants experience two patterns of connection—a hub centered on one initiator and a distributed web—then reflect on unity, interdependence, leadership, and the church as a living community.

## Materials

- One soft rope bag, rescue throw bag, or large ball of thick yarn
- Open space without trip hazards
- Chairs around the perimeter
- A prepared list of low-risk questions

## Setup

Inspect the rope for knots and damage. Use a length appropriate to the room. Form a close circle with enough space for a gentle underhand toss. State that the web is symbolic and will not support body weight.

## Facilitator flow

### 1. Frame — 5 minutes

Explain the toss, the right to pass on any question, and the prohibition on wrapping rope around hands, wrists, neck, torso, mobility devices, or furniture. Participants may remain seated or serve as observers.

Use questions such as:

- What is something that gave you joy this week?
- What is one skill or interest you enjoy sharing?
- Where is a place you feel at home?
- What helps you feel included in a group?

### 2. Round one: the hub — 8 minutes

Choose the first question initiator. They retain one end, toss the bag to each participant, ask a question, and receive the bag back after the answer. Continue until everyone has participated or the rope ends.

Pause and observe the pattern. Do **not** ask the group to lean or squat against the rope. Instead, apply only gentle hand tension and notice where the load concentrates.

### 3. Round two: the web — 8 minutes

The person who catches and answers becomes the next question initiator, retains a section of rope, and tosses to someone new. Continue until the group forms a distributed web.

Again, keep tension light and bodies stable.

### 4. Debrief — 15–25 minutes

1. What differences do you see between the two patterns?
2. What did the first initiator experience in each round?
3. Which pattern felt efficient, relational, vulnerable, or resilient?
4. What are the strengths and risks of centralized and distributed leadership?
5. What could the bonds represent?
6. What does wise leadership hold, share, or release?
7. How can unity differ from uniformity?

Read John 13:34–35 and Ephesians 4:1–16. Connect the web to interdependence and mutual contribution, while noting that healthy communities still need accountable leadership.

## Planted transition

Invite participants to draw a tree representing abundant life. Place the trees together to reveal a forest:

- **Tree:** individual formation
- **Forest:** church, body of Christ, and life together
- **Fruit and seed:** love, witness, and disciples who help form others

## Safety and accessibility

- Never use the rope for load-bearing, leaning, lifting, restraint, or balance.
- Keep the rope below shoulder height and loosely held.
- Use a ball of thick yarn if rope could be triggering or difficult to handle.
- Provide seated participation and an observer/mapmaker role.
- Stop immediately if anyone becomes entangled or uncomfortable.

## Limits

Distributed networks are not automatically healthier than centralized structures. Churches need both mutual participation and accountable authority. The activity reveals tradeoffs; it does not settle governance questions.
