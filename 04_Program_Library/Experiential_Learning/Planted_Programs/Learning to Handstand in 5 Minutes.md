# Learning to Handstand in 5 Minutes

## Outcome

Participants experience how a changed vantage point can make the familiar appear strange, then consider whether Jesus' “upside-down kingdom” is actually the restoration of a world disordered by sin.

## Important framing

The title is retained from the original *Planted* program, but this library version does **not** teach participants to perform a handstand. Unsupported or rapidly taught inversions create avoidable risk and exclude many bodies.

## Materials

- A landscape image or seedling image that can be rotated
- Projector, screen, or printed copies
- Optional phone camera with rotation lock
- Open wall space for a trained facilitator's optional demonstration

## Facilitator flow

### 1. Safe perspective shift — 5 minutes

Choose one:

- Rotate an image 180 degrees, invite observations, then restore it.
- Ask participants to view a familiar object from a seated, standing, and low side angle.
- Use a phone camera to rotate the room image without rotating bodies.
- A facilitator with established handstand competence may briefly demonstrate against a clear wall after a safety check; participants observe only.

The facilitator asks, “Why is this upside down?” A planned response may be, “What if the world we are used to is the thing that has been turned upside down?”

### 2. Notice — 5 minutes

- What became unfamiliar from the altered view?
- What did your mind try to correct?
- How quickly did the unusual view begin to feel normal?

### 3. Kingdom interpretation — 10–15 minutes

Read Mark 1:14–15 and selected Beatitudes from Matthew 5:3–12.

Discuss:

1. Why can Jesus' kingdom sound upside down—the last first, the weak strong, the least great?
2. What if Jesus is revealing creation's true orientation rather than inventing an arbitrary reversal?
3. How does resurrection change what we call power, success, life, and death?
4. What is already present in Christ and the Spirit? What remains not yet restored?

Connect the seed image of 1 Corinthians 15:35–49: what is sown in weakness is raised in glory. “You were not buried; you were planted” functions as pastoral metaphor, not a denial of grief.

### 4. Integration — 5 minutes

Invite participants to name one situation they may be interpreting only through the “present age.” Ask what resurrection hope makes visible and what faithful action it permits now.

## Safety and accessibility

- Do not invite untrained participants to attempt a handstand, headstand, shoulder stand, or inversion.
- Do not use inversion with anyone who is pregnant or has relevant wrist, shoulder, neck, spine, balance, cardiovascular, eye-pressure, or neurological concerns.
- Keep the default activity visual and nonphysical.
- Never suggest that a perspective shift alone resolves suffering, depression, trauma, or injustice.

## Limits

The kingdom is not merely a mindset, and resurrection is not positive reframing. Christian hope rests on God's action in the bodily resurrection of Jesus and the promised renewal of creation.
