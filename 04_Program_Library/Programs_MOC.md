# Programs MOC

## Orientation

A Garden Project program is a portable facilitated experience that helps a group encounter, notice, interpret, and embody one theological or formation insight. Programs are embodied metaphors: people undergo an experience before the debrief names its meaning.

**Minimal materials. Maximum meaning.**

Programs should be simple, replicable, meaningful, memorable, and portable—often using resources already in the room.

## Governing learning movement

**Frame → Experience → Notice → Interpret → Integrate**

1. **Frame** consent, boundaries, access, and purpose without explaining away the discovery.
2. **Experience** one simple interaction or challenge.
3. **Notice** what participants sensed, did, assumed, and felt.
4. **Interpret** the experience through Scripture and a fitting theological claim.
5. **Integrate** one prayer, practice, relationship, or next step.

The activity creates experience; the debrief creates meaning. Programs need enough time for both.

## Safety and stewardship

- Participation and disclosure are invited, never coerced.
- Offer dignified accessible alternatives and observer roles.
- Do not use pain, humiliation, compulsory food, sensory deprivation, forced exertion, or trauma disclosure as teaching devices.
- Name what the illustration clarifies and what it cannot prove.
- Preserve provenance, attribution, adaptation history, and permissions.
- A facilitator should know the pastoral and safeguarding escalation path before beginning.

## Open design questions

- What one experience will people remember?
- Can it run anywhere with a backpack—or with objects already present?
- Does the experience naturally generate the theological question?
- Is the debrief longer and more important than learning complicated rules?
- What could be misunderstood, excluded, or harmed?
- Does a program belong inside a curriculum, workshop, formation pathway, or several contexts?
