# Program Library

Reusable facilitated experiences that help a group encounter, discuss, and embody a theological or formation insight. Programs are modular: they may stand alone or serve a session within a larger workshop, curriculum, or CoJourner pathway.

Interpretive map: [[Programs MOC]]

This is the internal master library. Programs prepared for public use are eventually published through [[00 The Garden Project Ministry|The Garden Project]] under the [[03 Publishing and Stewardship Pipeline]].

## Collections

- [[00 Planted. Programs|Planted]] — five experiential programs following the movement from creation and brokenness through resurrection, formation, church, and mission
- [[00 God With Us Programs|God With Us]] — portable programs that teach participants to recognize and inhabit the presence-and-homecoming storyline

## Use a program when

- an embodied or relational experience will illuminate an idea better than explanation alone;
- the activity can be facilitated safely, accessibly, and without coercion;
- there is enough time to notice, interpret, and integrate the experience;
- the facilitator can name what the illustration clarifies and what it cannot prove.

## Program design philosophy

The strongest programs are embodied metaphors rather than disguised lectures or elaborate games. Prefer:

- one memorable experience;
- minimal materials that fit in a backpack—or are already in the room;
- a natural metaphor that creates questions before explanation;
- more time for noticing and debrief than for complicated rules;
- one biblical insight stated with appropriate limits;
- a design simple enough to run anywhere and deep enough to remember years later.

## Program standard

Every program should include:

1. A clear participant outcome
2. Materials and room setup
3. Framing and opt-out language
4. A timed facilitation sequence
5. Open-ended noticing questions
6. Biblical and theological interpretation
7. Integration or a next step
8. Physical, emotional, accessibility, and pastoral safeguards
9. Limits, source, and revision history
