# Devil Not Today (part 1)

## East of Eden

> He presented another parable to them: "The kingdom of heaven may be compared to a man who sowed good seed in his field. But while people were sleeping, his enemy came, sowed weeds among the wheat, and left. When the plants sprouted and produced grain, then the weeds also appeared. The landowner's servants came to him and said, 'Master, didn't you sow good seed in your field? Then where did the weeds come from?'
>
> "'An enemy did this,' he told them.
>
> "'So, do you want us to go and pull them up?' the servants asked him.
>
> "'No,' he said. 'When you pull up the weeds, you might also uproot the wheat with them. Let both grow together until the harvest. At harvest time I'll tell the reapers: Gather the weeds first and tie them in bundles to burn them, but collect the wheat in my barn.'"
> (Matthew 13:24–30)

Before we begin our Bible study, we'll do a short experiential program: [Dartboard](../../Programs/Dartboard%20(Notion).md).

As you do it, do not rush past what you feel and notice—especially as the conditions change. We will return to the experience in discussion.

Romans 3:23 states it plainly: "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." And 1 John 1:8 is more direct still: "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us."

The diagnosis is clear: we are broken in a broken world, and our fractures take different forms. If sin is "missing the mark," then the next question presses in: *where does sin come from?* Genesis 3 is often titled "The Fall." But if we listen carefully, it gives us more than a label—it gives us clues. Let's step into the scene.

First let's make some observations. Who was there? The serpent, who "was more crafty than any other animals"; Eve, "the mother of all living" Adam named her later; her husband Adam, who showed up after Eve's encounter with the serpent; and God of course, He is omnipresent, but notice that He was at a distance at first, because later He had to look for Adam and Eve. We can tell how cunning the serpent was from the way he spoke: "Did God really say…" "You will not certainly die…" etc. His words were so twisted from the original Word of God.

How did Eve fall for the trick of the serpent? She went with the flow - "God did say 'You must not eat the fruit…'", and she added "you must not touch it", which was not mentioned by God when He was commanding in Genesis 2:17. She was one step in the trap.

What did Eve do before she made the decision of eating the fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil? The Scripture drew the scene: When the woman saw that the fruit of the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eye, and also desirable for gaining wisdom (verse 6). She set her eyes on the fruit first, and started thinking about what the fruit could do to her body and mind. Pay attention to this detail, and we will get back to this part.

What happened when Eve ate the fruit and shared it with her husband? "Their eyes were opened". The name of the tree was true, they gained knowledge and self-awareness and were enlightened. What's below the surface was that shame and guilt creeped in. They realized that they were naked (shame), tried to cover themselves and hid from God (that's guilt, they knew they did something wrong), and fear became the motives of their actions: "I was afraid…" said Adam in verse 10.

Maybe originally the awareness of humans was like the dart board with the bullseye only; once our Father and Mother ate the fruit from the tree, the dart board was no longer bullseye only, but as what we can see today, with so much area to mess around. It's not because our world view was extended, but our view on God became more distant. Maybe God's original thoughts of humans were enjoying intimate relationships with them forever in the Garden of Delight, now there are age limits on human flesh. Adam and Eve didn't die right away physically, but their relationships with God were choked by shame, guilt, and fear. They were dead spiritually.

There are more consequences of sin in Genesis 3 for later studies. Until now, we still have little clue how everything just happened. Was Eve intentionally disobedient and tried to rebel against God? No, she was deceived, she was a victim, she was not the source of sin. The case may be more complicated than we thought. Was sin from the tree or the fruit? Well, it has "good" in its name, so I don't think it's all evil. The serpent seems most likely to be the Suspect. Did an animal cause the whole human being to stumble, or is there anything behind it?

We have begun to trace the pattern, but the text invites us to widen the lens. Consider Jesus—called "the sinless, spotless Lamb of God" (1 Peter 1:19 NLT). He entered the full weight of human experience, faced real temptation, and yet did not sin. Sin could not gain a foothold. How?

Matthew 4:1–11 takes place as Jesus fasts in the wilderness. The tempter arrives with a familiar voice: "If you are the Son of God…" Crafty. Subtle. Twisting what God has said, and targeting the identity God has declared. The pattern is the same as Eden.

And here the mask slips. The enemy is not merely a serpent, and not merely "the tempter." Jesus names him: "Away from me, Satan!" (v. 10).

"Satan"—also called the devil—means "Opposer." Scripture insists there is more at work in the world than what we can see. Whether someone has language for it or not, most people sense that something is *off*: the world is fractured, and we are too. The Bible does not reduce that fracture to psychology or sociology alone; it speaks of spiritual conflict.

Ephesians 6:12 (NLT) frames it with clarity: "For we are not fighting against flesh-and-blood enemies, but against evil rulers and authorities in this dark world, and against evil spirits in the heavenly places."

The devil's strategies are often ordinary before they are overt. One is *isolation*. In Genesis, God comes "walking" in the garden and calls for Adam and Eve—suggesting distance, hiding, and relational fracture. In Matthew, Jesus is led into the wilderness, and the tempter appears after long solitude. Isolation makes a person easier to target, because it is harder to test lies against truth in community.

Then come the whispers. Lies rarely arrive as pure falsehood; they are twisted truth. The devil knows what God has said and who God has named. He takes a true statement and bends it with a question: "Did God really say…?" "If you are the Son of God…"

Jesus later describes this enemy with sobering realism: "When he lies, he speaks his native language, for he is a liar and the father of lies" (John 8:44). The devil does not create; he *corrupts*. He takes what God made for good and retools it toward ruin. Liberty, happiness, love—even religion—can be hijacked when severed from God's truth. "The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy" (John 10:10). That is the pattern.

What are some raw materials the devil uses to form weapons? There's a verse in John's messages that can be used to summarize the correlations between Eve's and Jesus's encounter with the devil:

> 1 John 2:16 — For everything in the world - the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life - comes not from the Father but from the world.

|  | The lust of the flesh | The lust of the eyes | The pride of life |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Devil to Eve** | the woman saw the fruit of the tree was good for food (Gen 3:6) | (the fruit of the tree was) pleasing to the eye (Gen 3:6) | and also desirable for gaining wisdom (Gen 3:6) |
| **Devil to Jesus** | "Tell these stones to become bread." (Matt 4:3) | (the devil) showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their splendor (Matt 4:8) | "All this I will give you…" (Matt 4:9) |

The devil is not only a liar but a manipulator. He studies disordered desire and learns how to pull it like a thread. Steal. Kill. Destroy. And when someone yields, the tempter often becomes the accuser—piling on shame, guilt, and fear, as if condemnation were the final word.

Jesus, however, is not overcome as Eve was. He meets temptation without surrender. Though the wilderness looks like isolation, Jesus is not truly alone: he remains anchored in the presence of the Father. His body grows weak, but his communion with God deepens.

Notice how he fights. He does not outsmart the devil with cleverness; he answers lies with Scripture, holding fast to identity and calling. Deuteronomy 8:3: "Man shall not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God." Deuteronomy 6:16: "Do not put the Lord your God to the test." Deuteronomy 6:13: "Worship the Lord your God, and serve Him only."

Not today, Satan. With the sword of truth, Jesus says *no* to the liar, stands firm in the presence of God, and the battle turns: "Then the devil left him, and angels came and attended him" (Matthew 4:11).

The victory of Jesus is not the denial of the devil's reality, but the beginning of his defeat. One day there will be no more death or sorrow; for now we live in the "already" and the "not yet" of God's saving work. Jesus redeems what the thief has stolen, raises what has been killed, and restores what has been destroyed. He speaks truth over lies, identity over shame, forgiveness over guilt, and home over isolation.

"It's not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick… For I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners." (Matthew 9:12–13). Saint Augustine once said, the church is not a museum of saints, but a hospital for the sinners. Christians are all forgiven sinners. We all need to be healed. We all have broken hearts that need to be restored. You don't clean yourself before you turn back to God, just like you don't wait to see the doctor till you are healed. Healing is always available in the name of Jesus. Even for the healthy, no one can promise they won't be sick in their lifetime, just like no one can say they won't sin anymore. Sin is a contagious disease, but the Good News is: Jesus died for our sins from the past, present, and in the future. It doesn't mean we can keep messing around, Jesus loves us too much to let us stay the same. Who would like to be sick and broken when they could choose to be healthy and whole?

Take a breath. The terrain is clearer now: we live amid spiritual conflict, but we are not left defenseless. We do not have to be victims of whispered lies, and we do not have to fight alone. In the presence of God, and in the strength of community, we can proclaim truth over deception and stand firm.

"Give no opportunity to the devil" (Ephesians 4:27 ESV).

You are a seed, and you are buried in the darkness. Because of sin, the world is full of brokenness. However, that's not the end of the story…
