# Planted Vol. 1 — There Is A Garden Named Delight

The creation story is the most fascinating story I've ever heard. A pastor once said, "if you don't believe the first chapter of the Bible, it might be hard for you to believe the rest of it." The statement is true. The creation story is not just "a story", but "God's story". Do you know how the word *history* is spelled? *History* is "His-story". In my different stages of life, every time I read Genesis 1 and 2, it always brought something new to me. For example, Genesis 1:6–7:

> And God said, "Let there be an expanse in the midst of the waters, and let it separate the waters from the waters." And God made the expanse and separated the waters that were under the expanse from the waters that were above the expanse. And it was so.

God didn't create the waters, He simply separated them! It blew my mind. Later I learned that waters in the Bible a lot of times are representative of chaos and out of order. Chaos is not from God, and our God is a God of order.

God created the world in six days by speaking things into existence. At the end of the first five days, when God looked at what He just created, He said, "It's good." On the sixth day when God created humans - the last component of the whole picture - it wasn't just "good" to God, but "it was *very* good" (Genesis 1:31). Have you had the moments when you finished a project and you leaned back on the chair looking at the project with a smile on your face? That kind of "it was very good" feeling. Day six was the climax of the creation story, and Genesis 2 was that highlight unfolding.

Men were mere dust (Genesis 2:7), but because God blew the breath of life - known as God's Spirit - into it, the dust became a living soul. Human beings were not created randomly. Males and females were made in God's image, in God's likeness, and to reflect God's own nature (Genesis 1:27). The first man's name was Adam, the name simply means "human". God placed him in a garden He planted - Eden, which means pleasure or delight. Later in the garden God made a woman out of the man, and her name Eve means "life". Now you know why no one else in the Old Testament named "Adam" or "Eve", because these names are abstract concepts in the original language Hebrew. Eve was taken out from Adam, and Adam called her "Bone of my bones, Flesh of my flesh" (Genesis 2:23). The same way *life* is from *humanity*, and it is traced back to the breath of life from God. To know God, we will receive the insights of what it means to be human, and what it means to live, because God is the ultimate source of life.

It was very good. Period. Hebrew is a unique language because one Hebrew word can contain a group of various contexts or ideas. It was good. It was pleasant. It was beautiful. God brings chaos back into order, and He made beautiful things out of the dust. What a fabulous Artist and Designer! Ephesians 2:10 says: for we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them. In other words, we are God's masterpiece. God created us for relationships and sharing His glory. These relationships by design are two-way relationships. We should walk in them and give God glory, as what we are created for. When people see a good artwork, they don't praise the artwork but the artist. If a Christian lives out his faith, through him people may see the Creator and give Him praise.

Psalm 139 in my opinion is the most beautiful psalm which tells how God knows us and loves us before we even exist. Let's study the whole psalm, and just highlight some verses here:

> v.6 Such knowledge is too wonderful for me;
> It is high, I cannot attain it.
>
> v.14 I will praise You, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made;
> Marvelous are Your works,
> And that my soul knows very well.
>
> v.17 How precious also are Your thoughts to me, O God!
> How great is the sum of them!
>
> v.18 If I should count them, they would be more in number than the sand;
> When I awake, I am still with You.

The meanings of the Hebrew word "thoughts" in verse 17 include "purposes", "intentions", "plans", etc. The word was also used by the prophet Isaiah (Isaiah 55:9):

> For as the heavens are higher than the earth,
> So are My ways higher than your ways,
> And My thoughts than your thoughts.

God's heart for us is way beyond our imaginations. He knows every bit of us more than more than we ever could to know ourselves. As an engineer, God sees through the mechanism behind our thoughts, words, and actions. He reads our life as a book, he knows how it starts and how it ends. The past, present, and future are all in His hands. The lyrics of the worship song *God Song* by Hillsong displays this author-books relationships:

> "Cause if You signed Your name upon my heart
> They way that authors always do
> Then You must be proud to call me Yours
> And I must be made for loving You"

Psalm 139 reveals the deepest desires as humans - to be known and to be loved - which are part of God's designs. Not being known nor being loved is the zone of isolation. It's painful and life-depriving. Nobody wants to be stuck in isolation forever. How about other situations? Quote from Tim Keller:

> "To be loved but not known is comforting but superficial. To be known and not loved is our greatest fear. But to be fully known and truly loved is, well, a lot like being loved by God. It is what we need more than anything. It liberates us from pretense, humbles us out of our self-righteousness, and fortifies us for any difficulty life can throw at us."

The desires of being known and being loved do not always feel that strong, but it's always there at the deepest corner of our hearts. People are driven by that deepest desire wanting to earn the approval of others, especially from their parents; people trying their best to fit in; some people are prompted by it and jump from relationship to relationship because they desperately want to be known and loved. Well, most of the time our effort is in vain, because nothing in this world can fulfill the deepest desire of our souls. By nature, our attempts of filling the void all lead to performance, total excess, or isolation.

What's supernatural is the intimate relationship with God. It's the relationship of both grace and truth, and it's beyond the limit of the dimension of time. Intimate relationship with God is like seeds being delightfully planted in the garden. It's time to know who you are - you are a seed; and it's time to know the Ultimate Gardener who made you. God knows who you are and what's in you. Would a farmer grieve over the seed they just sowed? No, farmers know how the seeds will end up being, so they picture the harvest and sow with joy. The same way when God planted you delightfully in the garden, He sees the potential that He put in you, and He wants to see them to be fulfilled. He has a unique plan in your life.
