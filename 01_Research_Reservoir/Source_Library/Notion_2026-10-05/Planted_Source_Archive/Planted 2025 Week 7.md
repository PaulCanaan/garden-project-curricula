# Planted. 2025 — Week 7 Program: Testimony Sharing

First 15 minutes: hanging out, sharing what's going on in life

## Homework from our last meeting

- Prepare to share your testimony.
- Since all of us will share, we need to limit each sharing time under 5 minutes. This will help – when you prepare for it, try to write down your testimony, read it and record it, see how long it's going take.
- In your testimony, think about these three questions:
  1. What was your life like before you accepted Jesus as your Lord and Savior?
  2. The moment you experienced Jesus in your life. What was it like?
  3. What is your life now? How has Jesus been working and changing your life?
- Often God reveals the details when you recall them and write them down. Pray for authenticity and courage. Be as vulnerable as you can.

## Program: Testimony Sharing

(60 to 90 minutes, depends on how many people sharing)

- Timing people's sharing time
- Reminder when people share for 4 minutes (it's okay to share a little longer than 5 minutes)
- The Grace-Truth matrix – the patterns of our testimonies
- The common denominator of our testimonies – Jesus. All of our stories point to the story of Jesus – his life, death, and resurrection.

Here comes to the question: **Who is Jesus?**

This is actually our homework for the next week: How would you tell the story of Jesus? Like the way you would introduce a friend. From one small perspective, tell one identity or one character of Jesus. It could be the perspective of something Jesus did that revealed his characters, the perspective of his own claims or the way others see him, even the perspective from the Old Testament that you can see Jesus in the story.

## Why do we share the stories of Jesus?

- "In our haste to develop detailed answers to the question 'Why Jesus die?' we sometimes forget to ask, 'How did Jesus live?' Indeed, we need to understand his life in order to comprehend his death." – Christopher Watkin, *Biblical Critical Theory* and N. T. Wright, *Jesus and the Victory of God*
- "C. S. Lewis's principle that the 'myth', the story, cannot simply be discarded when we fancy that we have extracted all its ethical and theological principles: its solution in which they are suspended and from which they draw their nourishment." – Christopher Watkin, *Biblical Critical Theory* and C. S. Lewis, *Surprised by Joy*

**Book club:** *Basic Christianity* by John Stott — A short book but the language is rich. It can really refresh the way we think of Christ, like the seeing how his claims were based on his characters. "Christianity is Christ." – John Stott
