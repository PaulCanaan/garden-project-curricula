# Planted. 2025 — Week 2: Creation

**Last week recap** – The Grace-Truth Matrix

## Opening

Every story has a beginning. "Planted" tells a story of a seed being planted, and it started in a garden which found at the beginning of the Bible.

Genesis 1 – "If we don't believe the very first chapter of the Bible, it would be hard for us to believe the rest of it."

By "believing" I don't mean "blind faith". St. Augustine said: "Believe in order to understand, understand in order to believe." Studying the Bible nourishes our understanding and faith.

Genesis 1 & 2 never failed in teaching me something new. In Genesis 1 and 2 we will see how the creation story prepare us to see that grace and truth.

## Framework 2: S.O.A.P.

**S – Scripture**

> **2 Timothy 3:16** — All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness.
> **Hebrews 4:12** — For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

**O – Observation**

> Exegesis – from the Greek term *exegeisthai*, "to lead out" – a critical explanation or interpretation of a text, especially of Scripture.
> Biblical Textual Criticism – investigates the history and origins of the text, but exegesis may include the study of the historical and cultural background of the author, text, and original audience.

**A – Application**

> Hermeneutics – to interpret the text to find its intended meaning (general principles) – is the science and the art of biblical interpretation. We need to handle it with both grace and truth.
> **2 Timothy 2:15** — Be diligent to present yourself approved to God as a worker who does not need to be ashamed, accurately handling the word of truth.

**P – Prayer** (our communication and response to God)

- P – Praise
- R – Repent
- A – Ask
- Y – Your will
- S – Show me

## Applying the S.O.A.P. framework in studying Genesis 1 & 2

1. Read the passage. (for long passages, maybe take turns at reading, like 3 verses per person)
2. Summarize/retell the story with your own words/what's the big idea of the passage?
3. Is there any word that's confusing or surprising to you? Any concepts or details you don't understand? See if anyone in the group can help define the confusing words.
4. What is the context of the passage? (in the beginning; *tohu wa-bohu*)
5. What does the passage tell us about God? About people? About God's relationship with people? (the Creator-creation distinguish)
6. What verse do you like/speaks out to you and why?
7. How does the passage change your view of God? Of yourself? And other people?
8. How may the passage change how you live?

**Other key points we may discuss:**

- The chiastic structure of the six-day creation and the seventh day. (separating and filling, and the Sabbath rest as the climax)
- How do we see Trinity in Genesis 1 – God, God's Spirit, God's Word
- Order – creativity
- "God saw it was good" "it was very good"

> "I am a reptile keeper, and I build bioactive enclosures for geckos. I would first place the substrates in the enclosure, plant plants, figure out light and heating devices, and add decomposers as clean-up crew. In each step I would make sure "it's good" before I move to the next step. And a gecko enclosure is not called gecko enclosure without a gecko moving in, right? So, I add the last component of the whole picture - the gecko! – in that mini ecosystem. Then I lean back on my chair watching her exploring her new home, and I call it "it's very good". Have you ever had that "it is very good" feeling with a smile on your face when you finish a project?"
