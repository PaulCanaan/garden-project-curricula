# Program: Dartboard (Brokenness, Distance, and Darkness)

**Session:** Devil Not Today (part 1) — East of Eden
**Estimated Time:** 10–15 min activity + 15–25 min discussion
**Group Size:** 4–20 (works best 6–14)

---

## Purpose

This program helps participants experience:

> Why "missing the mark" is not an exception but the normal human condition—and why brokenness is deeply linked with human nature.

We don't even need the Bible to tell us that we live in a broken world: by living in it, we learn that we are all broken people—and we are broken differently. This program is designed **not to condemn** participants, but to help them name what they already sense, and then prepare them to hear the gospel with honesty.

The program is designed to prepare participants to explore:

- Sin as "missing the mark" (falling short)
- The link between brokenness and human nature
- How *distance* (nearness to God) and *darkness* (impaired spiritual perception) shape our inability to reach righteousness by willpower
- Grace and truth together: God names sin truthfully, and God meets the brokenhearted with mercy

---

## Desired Discovery

Participants may begin to notice that:

> Even sincere effort cannot reliably produce "bullseye righteousness" when the standard is holy, distance increases, and vision is impaired—and that shame often follows failure unless grace interrupts.

Do not explain this before the activity unless safety or clarity requires it. Allow participants to experience the pattern before naming its meaning.

---

## Materials

- Magnetic dartboard + magnetic darts
- Blindfolds (eye masks work great)

### Optional

- Painter's tape (to mark throwing lines)
- Phone timer
- Scorekeeping sheet/whiteboard (to make it more fun)

---

## Setup

Before participants arrive:

1. Hang the magnetic dartboard securely on a wall at a good height.
2. Use tape to mark two throwing lines: **Near** (Round 1) and **Far** (Round 2). (Round 3 usually uses the Near line.)
3. Clear the throwing lane so no one can cross in front of the thrower.
4. Decide how you will keep score (simple totals; avoid making it overly competitive).

Arrange the room so that:

- participants can stand behind the thrower and still see;
- observers can participate without throwing;
- the contrast between near vs. far is obvious.

---

## Facilitator Preparation

The Program Facilitator should understand:

- this is not a performance test; it is an embodied metaphor;
- scorekeeping is to keep energy up, not to shame anyone;
- the key contrasts are *normal* → *farther* → *blindfold*;
- how to connect the debrief to both **truth** (sin/falling short) and **grace** (God does not condemn; God draws near).

Before beginning, decide:

- whether everyone throws or only volunteers (either works; keep it invitational);
- who will track score/time;
- your accessibility alternative (observe + record patterns).

---

## Introduction to Participants

Give only the information needed to participate:

> "We're going to do a quick dartboard challenge in three rounds. It's meant to be fun, but also to help us notice something about ourselves. Don't rush past what you feel as the conditions change."

Explain:

- Each person gets **3 shots per round**.
- One person throws at a time.
- No one crosses the line while darts are being thrown.
- Participation is invitational; observing counts.

Avoid explaining what the activity symbolizes.

---

## Instructions

### Preparation

1. Show the dartboard and the Near/Far lines.
2. Demonstrate a safe throw and safe retrieval.
3. Assign roles (thrower, scorekeeper, lane steward).

### Round 1 — Baseline (Normal Distance)

1. Each person takes **3 shots** from the Near line.
2. Keep a simple score if desired.

What participants experience:

> "This is doable," but the bullseye is still small.

### Round 2 — Distance (Nearness Changes Everything)

1. Move to the Far line.
2. Each person takes **3 shots** again.
3. Keep score.

What changes:

> The target is the same, but distance makes "hitting the standard" feel dramatically harder.

### Round 3 — Darkness (Blindfold)

1. Return to the Near line.
2. Invite only volunteers (no pressure).
3. Put on the eye mask and take **3 shots**.
4. A facilitator stands within arm's reach to ensure safety.

What changes:

> Without sight, intention is not enough. Direction and perception are compromised.

### Completion

End the activity when:

- everyone has had a chance to throw in rounds 1–2,
- and a few volunteers have completed the blindfold round,
- or the allotted time has passed.

Invite the group to step back and look at where darts landed.

---

## Facilitation Notes

During the activity:

- keep it playful, not competitive;
- protect participants from shame;
- avoid commenting on someone's "skill"; focus on the conditions changing;
- notice the language people use ("I can't," "I'm terrible," "this is impossible").

---

## Accessibility and Safety

### Physical Safety

- One thrower at a time.
- Keep the lane clear.
- Blindfold round: facilitator nearby; no distractions.

### Emotional and Relational Safety

- No teasing.
- No interpreting misses as "spiritual failures."
- Participation is invitational.

### Alternative Participation

Participants may:

- observe and record what they notice (feelings, patterns, group dynamics);
- keep time/score;
- help retrieve darts safely.

---

## Discussion

Really take your time in this. Encourage participants to engage in conversation.

### 1. Experience

1. What did you feel in each round?
2. What changed in you when distance increased?
3. What changed in you when your eyes were covered?

### 2. Observation

1. In archery games, what does the referee say if you "miss the mark"?
2. What patterns did you notice across the rounds?

### 3. Interpretation

1. What do you think the **bullseye** represents?
2. What do you think **distance** represents?
3. What do you think the **eye mask** represents?

### Key Points (Name the Mapping)

- **Bullseye:** the standard of God. Sin is anywhere away from the bullseye.
- **Distance:** how close our relationship with God is (an oversimplified depiction). Nearness makes it easier to follow the Way of God.
- **Eye covering:** "walking in darkness" before we know God—so we cannot hit the bullseye of righteousness by our own willpower.

### 4. Scripture Connection (Grace & Truth)

Read as needed:

- Romans 3:23
- Ecclesiastes 7:20
- 1 John 1:8 (if needed, read 1 John 1:7–10)
- John 3:17
- Psalm 34:18
- Matthew 5:3

Ask:

1. How do these passages name our condition honestly without crushing us?
2. Where do you see God's nearness to the brokenhearted?
3. What is the difference between conviction and condemnation?

#### Keller Quote (Optional)

"The gospel is this: We are more sinful and flawed in ourselves than we ever dared believe, yet at the very same time we are more loved and accepted in Jesus Christ than we ever dared hope."
― Timothy Keller

### 5. Response

1. Where do you feel most aware of brokenness in your life right now?
2. What would it look like to move toward God (nearness) rather than hiding?
3. What kind of support do you need from community to walk in the light?

---

## Transition

> "If brokenness is universal, the next question is not 'Who is the worst?' but 'Where did this fracture come from—and why are we so vulnerable to it?' Let's enter Genesis 3 and watch the pattern unfold."
