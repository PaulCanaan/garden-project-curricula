# Program: The Bond (Interdependence, Love, and Leadership)

**Session:** (Use in the "trees → forest" movement / community formation week)
**Estimated Time:** 12–18 min activity + 20–30 min discussion
**Group Size:** 6–18 (works best 8–14)

---

## Purpose

This program helps participants experience:

> How community becomes stronger or weaker depending on how relationships are structured—especially how leadership, initiative, and mutual dependence are shared.

The program is designed to prepare participants to explore:

- Unity and maturity in the body (Ephesians 4)
- Love as the visible mark of discipleship (John 13:34–35)
- Interdependence: we grow together, not alone
- Wisdom in leadership: when to hold, when to release, and how to empower others

The activity should not attempt to teach the whole session. Its purpose is to create a shared experience that the group can later interpret through Scripture.

---

## Desired Discovery

Participants may begin to notice that:

> A community can look "connected," but still be fragile if everything depends on one strong leader. A stronger bond forms when initiative and responsibility are shared—yet that sharing also requires trust, clarity, and love.

Do not explain this before the activity unless safety or clarity requires it. Allow participants to experience the pattern before naming its meaning.

---

## Materials

- Rescuing rope bag (throw bag with rope) — long enough for your group

### Optional

- Phone timer
- A few printed "question prompts" (for groups that freeze)

---

## Setup

Before participants arrive:

1. Clear an open space large enough for everyone to stand in a close circle and to lean back safely.
2. Check the rope bag: no knots that could snag fingers; rope length is sufficient.
3. Decide a clear safety boundary for the throw (gentle toss only; no force).

Arrange the room so that:

- everyone can stand shoulder-to-shoulder in a circle;
- there is no furniture behind participants that could cause a fall;
- anyone who opts out can still stand near the circle and observe.

---

## Facilitator Preparation

The Program Facilitator should understand:

- the point is not the "rope trick," but the relational pattern it reveals;
- the two rounds create two different community structures;
- the lean-back/squat portion requires care (no one should be forced);
- how the debrief will bridge to Ephesians 4 and John 13.

Before beginning, decide:

- who you will select as the *first* Question Initiator (or whether you will ask for a volunteer);
- what you will do if the rope runs out early (stop and move to the team-effort portion);
- your accessibility alternative (observe + record patterns; or hold the rope without leaning back).

---

## Introduction to Participants

Give only the information needed to participate:

> "We're going to do a simple two-round activity that forms two different 'bonds.' It's not a performance test. The goal is to notice what happens—how the bond forms, how strong it feels, and how the group experiences leadership and dependence."

Explain:

- gentle tosses only (think: underhand, safe, slow);
- no one is pressured to share anything personal;
- anyone may opt out of throwing or leaning back (observing is full participation).

Avoid explaining what the bond symbolizes.

---

## Instructions

### Preparation

1. Ask everyone to stand in the middle of the room in a close circle.
2. Explain the role:
   - The **Question Initiator** holds one end of the rope and tosses the rope bag to someone.
   - The Question Initiator asks a question to get to know that person.
   - The catcher answers, then holds a part of the rope so the bond forms.
3. Explain: "We will do **two rounds** with slightly different rules and form two different patterns."

### Round 1 — Centralized Bond (One Initiator)

1. Choose a person to be the *first* Question Initiator (often someone confident / socially skilled).
2. The first Question Initiator holds the rope end and gently tosses the rope bag to anyone in the circle.
3. The first Question Initiator asks a question (keep it simple and relational).
4. The catcher answers, then holds an end/segment of the rope and tosses the rope bag **back** to the first Question Initiator.
5. Repeat until:
   - everyone has been asked, *or*
   - the rope length runs out.
6. When complete, toss the rope bag back to the first Question Initiator.

What participants experience:

> The bond is formed through one central connector. The group is connected, but much depends on one person.

### Team Effort — Test the Bond (Round 1)

1. Everyone holds the rope firmly (one or two hands, comfortable grip).
2. As a group, try to:
   - lean back slightly together (only as far as feels safe),
   - squat down together,
   - then stand back up together.
3. If anyone feels unsafe, pause immediately and adjust.

### Round 2 — Distributed Bond (Initiator Moves)

1. Explain the new rule: "In this round, the person who catches the rope bag becomes the **new** Question Initiator."
2. The first Question Initiator starts by tossing the rope bag to someone.
3. The catcher answers the question, then becomes the new Question Initiator:
   - they hold a part of the rope,
   - toss the rope bag to anyone,
   - ask a question.
4. Repeat until:
   - everyone has been asked, *or*
   - the rope runs out.
5. When complete, toss the rope bag back to the *first* Question Initiator.

What changes:

> Leadership and initiative move through the community; connection becomes multi-directional.

### Team Effort — Test the Bond (Round 2)

Repeat the same group movement:

- lean back (if safe) → squat → stand.

### Completion

End the activity when:

- both bonds have been formed and tested,
- or the allotted time has passed.

Invite participants to look at the "shape" of each bond before moving into discussion.

---

## Facilitation Notes

During the activity:

- keep questions light and non-invasive;
- protect the vulnerable: no teasing, no forced sharing;
- keep the contrast between rounds clear;
- slow down if the group becomes overly silly or competitive.

### Watch For

- how much depends on the first Question Initiator in round 1
- who feels empowered vs. passive in each round
- whether the group feels more "alive" when initiative is shared
- whether distributed leadership creates confusion—or shared ownership

---

## Accessibility and Safety

### Physical Safety

- Gentle underhand tosses only.
- Keep the circle tight enough for safe tosses.
- The lean-back/squat portion is optional. Offer "hold steady and observe" as a full option.

### Emotional and Relational Safety

- No shame for quietness or awkwardness.
- Do not interpret someone's social skill as spiritual maturity.
- Do not pressure vulnerability.

### Alternative Participation

Participants may:

- observe and track patterns ("Who initiates? Who withdraws? What feels strong/fragile?")
- hold the rope without doing the squat/lean
- use a prepared question prompt card

---

## Discussion

Take time here. The discussion is part of the program, not an optional explanation afterward.

### 1. Experience

1. What did you feel in round 1? What did you feel in round 2?
2. If you were the *first* Question Initiator: how did each round feel from that role?
3. What emotions showed up—confidence, pressure, awkwardness, relief, joy?

### 2. Observation

1. What is the most obvious difference between the two bond patterns?
2. Which one *felt* stronger? Which one *looked* stronger?
3. What were the advantages and disadvantages of each pattern?
4. What happened to the group when leadership stayed centralized vs. when it moved?

### 3. Interpretation

1. What could the **bond** represent in real life (friendship, church community, discipleship, mission, family)?
2. What might the rope represent (unity, love, trust, shared responsibility, commitment)?
3. What might the Question Initiator role represent (leadership, initiative, spiritual gifts, maturity)?
4. What do you think this program is inviting us to consider about community and discipleship?

### 4. Scripture Connection

**Key Passages:**

- Ephesians 4 (unity, maturity, "joined and held together")
- John 13:34–35

#### John 13:34–35 (Read Aloud)

"A new command I give you: Love one another. As I have loved you, so you must love one another. By this everyone will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another."

Ask:

1. In Ephesians 4, what holds the body together—and what grows it up?
2. How does "speaking the truth in love" relate to the kind of bond we need?
3. According to John 13, what is the clearest visible sign of Jesus' disciples?
4. Where did you see (or not see) love in the way the bond formed?

### 5. Response

1. In your life right now, where do you rely too heavily on one "strong connector"?
2. Where might Jesus be inviting you to take initiative—to love, to include, to serve, to ask?
3. What is one concrete step you can take this week to strengthen the bond of your community?

---

## Key Points

- A bond is not only "connection," but **unity with a foundation** (love + truth + shared commitment).
- Interdependence is strength: we grow together through many connections, not one.
- Wisdom in leadership includes learning when to **hold** and when to **let go** so others can lead.

### Central Insight

> A community becomes resilient when love and responsibility are shared—when the bond is held together by many threads, not one.

### Important Clarification

> This program does not mean centralized leadership is always wrong. It highlights a risk: when a community depends on one person, it becomes fragile—and others do not mature into love and responsibility.

The analogy is intentionally limited. Do not force every detail of the activity to carry theological meaning.

---

## Transition

Use the shared experience to lead into the next part of the session:

> "A single tree can be strong, but a forest holds together through countless hidden connections. If we want to grow into maturity, we need bonds of love and truth that are shared—so let's explore what Scripture says about being 'joined and held together,' and how Jesus forms a people, not just individuals."
