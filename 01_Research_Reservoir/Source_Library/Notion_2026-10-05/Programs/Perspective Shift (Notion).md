# Program: Perspective Shift (Handstand)

**Session:** (Use when introducing "the kingdom feels upside-down")
**Estimated Time:** 20–30 min activity + 20–30 min discussion
**Group Size:** 4–12 (works best 6–10)

---

## Purpose

This program helps participants experience:

> What it feels like to have your perspective inverted—and why Jesus' teaching about the kingdom often feels "upside-down" compared to the world.

The program is designed to prepare participants to explore:

- The kingdom's reversal of status and power (Matthew 19:30)
- Humility before exaltation (learning to "come down safely" before trying to be lifted)
- Greatness redefined as childlike humility and servanthood (Matthew 18:3–4; Mark 10:43–45)
- The Beatitudes as a kingdom portrait that contradicts the world's instincts (Matthew 5:3–6)

The activity should not attempt to teach the whole session. Its purpose is to create a shared bodily experience that the group can later interpret through Scripture.

---

## Desired Discovery

Participants may begin to notice that:

> When your body is upside down, what once felt "normal" immediately feels strange—yet with time, you can begin to adapt. In the same way, we can become so accustomed to an upside-down world that Jesus' kingdom feels foreign. The gospel is not merely new information; it is a reorientation.

Do not explain this before the activity unless safety or clarity requires it. Allow participants to feel the inversion before naming its meaning.

---

## Materials

- Open space
- One clear wall
- Yoga mats

### Optional

- A timer
- A printed "exit safely" cue card (for anxious participants)

---

## Setup

Before participants arrive:

1. Choose an open area with a clear wall.
2. Place yoga mats in the corner where wall meets floor (extra stability + safety).
3. Ensure the floor is dry and non-slip.

Arrange the room so that:

- there's a clear "practice lane" near the wall;
- observers can see without crowding;
- participants can opt out without embarrassment.

---

## Facilitator Preparation

The Program Facilitator should understand:

- safety is the priority; no one should be pressured to invert;
- this program includes multiple levels; participants should stop at the safest level for them;
- the debrief should connect: *inversion → disorientation → adaptation → reorientation*.

Before beginning, decide:

- who will demonstrate each step (ideally someone with basic experience);
- who will spot (stand nearby, ready to assist);
- what modifications you will offer (e.g., "stay at Step 1 only" is valid participation).

---

## Introduction to Participants

Give only the information needed to participate:

> "We're going to practice a perspective shift using a simple handstand progression. You can stop at any step. The goal isn't athletic achievement—it's to notice what it feels like when your 'normal' view of the world flips."

Explain:

- participation is invitational;
- safety comes first;
- no one will be judged by how far they go;
- observing carefully is full participation.

Avoid explaining the full symbolism upfront.

---

## Instructions

### Warm-up (5 minutes)

Mobilize joints that bear load in inversion:

1. Shoulders
2. Elbows
3. Wrists

### Step 1 — Wall Walk (Inverted Feeling + Muscle Awareness)

Goal: experience and adapt to inversion safely.

1. Start in a plank with feet near the wall.
2. Walk your feet up the wall while walking hands slightly closer.
3. Hold for a few breaths.
4. Walk back down slowly.

What participants experience:

> Inversion feels disorienting; unfamiliar muscles engage.

### Step 2 — Side Exit (Learning to Come Down Humbly)

Goal: learn how to exit safely before attempting "being lifted."

1. From a partial wall-walk or supported position, practice stepping one leg down to the side.
2. Turn the body and come down gently.

Facilitator note:

> Emphasize: "Before we rise, we learn how to come down."

### Step 3 — Headstand (Stable Triangle + Core Importance)

Goal: feel the stability of a triangular base and the role of the core.

1. Form a stable triangle with forearms/hands and head (use careful instruction).
2. Lift knees slightly (or keep toes on the ground for a modified version).
3. Engage core; hold briefly.
4. Come down slowly.

Safety note:

> Offer an alternative: remain in the setup position without lifting.

### Step 4 — Wall Handstand (Kick + Hip Lift from Core)

Goal: practice a supported handstand with technique.

1. Face away from the wall.
2. Place hands on the mat, shoulder-width.
3. Kick one leg up and let heels meet the wall.
4. Practice engaging core to bring hips over shoulders.
5. Come down using the side exit.

### Step 5 — Variations + Daily Practice Moves

Offer a few options:

- short holds (5–10 seconds)
- wall taps or controlled leg lifts
- returning to Step 1 as a "daily practice"

### Completion

End the activity when:

- everyone has had time to attempt at least Steps 1–2,
- and optional participants have tried Steps 3–5,
- or the allotted time has passed.

Invite everyone to sit, breathe, and notice what feels different in body and mind.

---

## Facilitation Notes

During the activity:

- affirm every level of participation;
- keep the room calm (anxiety rises quickly with inversion);
- ensure adequate spotting;
- do not rush transitions.

### Watch For

- fear vs. curiosity
- disorientation (dizziness, panic)
- how quickly people want to "skip steps"
- how "coming down" can feel like failure unless reframed as wisdom

---

## Accessibility and Safety

### Physical Safety

- Use mats.
- Keep the wall area clear.
- Spot participants.
- Encourage stopping immediately if pain, dizziness, or wrist strain appears.

### Emotional and Relational Safety

- No teasing about fear or strength.
- Normalize opting out.
- Never force touch; ask consent before spotting.

### Alternative Participation

Participants may:

- observe and record what they notice (feelings, questions, metaphors)
- do only warm-up + Step 1
- do seated "perspective shift" reflection while others practice

---

## Discussion

Take time here. The discussion is part of the program, not an optional explanation afterward.

### 1. Experience

1. What did you observe while you were upside down?
2. What did you feel—physically and emotionally?
3. What surprised you?

### 2. Observation

1. When you were inverted, everything looked upside down, didn't it?
2. So which is upside down—us, or the world?
3. Did you start adapting the longer you stayed in it?

### 3. Interpretation

1. Why does Jesus' teaching about the kingdom often feel "reversed" compared to the world?
2. Is it possible we've been "upside down" for so long that we've grown used to it?
3. What might it look like for Jesus to re-train our sense of what is normal?

### 4. Scripture Connection

**Key Passages:**

- Matthew 5:3–6
- Matthew 19:30
- Matthew 18:3–4
- Mark 10:43–45

Ask:

1. In the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3–6), what kinds of people are called "blessed," and why does that feel upside-down?
2. What does Matthew 19:30 reverse—and how does that confront our instincts?
3. Why does Jesus connect greatness with childlike humility (Matthew 18:3–4)?
4. How does Jesus redefine leadership in Mark 10:43–45?

### 5. Response

1. Where do you most feel pressure to "be on top" in the world's sense?
2. What is one area where Jesus might be inviting you to a kingdom reorientation (humility, meekness, hunger for righteousness, servanthood)?
3. What would it mean this week to practice "coming down safely" (humble repentance, receiving help, letting go)?

#### C. S. Lewis Quote (Optional)

"I believe in Christianity as I believe that the sun has risen: not only because I see it, but because by it I see everything else."
― C. S. Lewis

---

## Key Points

- Inversion reveals how much we assume our "normal" is trustworthy.
- The kingdom often feels upside-down because our world's values are already inverted.
- Jesus forms disciples through reorientation: humility, meekness, hunger for righteousness, and servanthood.

### Central Insight

> The gospel is a perspective shift: not just seeing different things, but seeing everything differently.

### Important Clarification

> This program does not mean the world is meaningless or that physical inversion is spiritual maturity. It is a limited analogy meant to help us feel the strangeness—and necessity—of kingdom reorientation.

---

## Transition

> "If the kingdom feels upside-down, the next question is: what story has been shaping our 'normal'? Let's open Scripture and let Jesus re-train our imagination—so we can learn what is truly right-side up."
