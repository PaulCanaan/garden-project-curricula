## Slide 3 — Ecclesiology

Ecclesiology is the theological study of the Christian Church itself. Originating from the Greek word ekklesia (meaning "assembly" or "congregation") and logos (meaning "study"), it examines the church's origins, nature, purpose, structure, leadership, and its relationship to Jesus Christ.

## Slide 6 — The Theological Pyramid

*[Import note: reassembled from interleaved PDF text.]*

- **Tertiary Doctrines** (Distinguishes individual discipleship): Culture, Worship style, End-times, Ministry methods
- **Secondary Doctrines** (Distinguishes traditions): Baptism, Spiritual gifts, Church government
- **Primary Doctrines** (Distinguishes Christianity from cults): Trinity, Christology, Gospel, Scripture’s authority
- Base of the pyramid: **Truth**

Criteria:

1. Amount of Scripture support & canonical centrality
2. Necessity for salvation
3. Clarity of teaching

## Slide 8 — Inter-denominational Communication

Diagram labels: Local community · Historical community · Global community

Each tradition has its spiritual treasures and blind spots. How do members of the one Church faithfully communicate across traditions?

Inter-denominational communication is the practice of listening, learning, and laboring together as members of the one Body of Christ while remaining rooted in Scripture, growing in one’s local church, and contributing faithfully to the global and historical faith communities.
