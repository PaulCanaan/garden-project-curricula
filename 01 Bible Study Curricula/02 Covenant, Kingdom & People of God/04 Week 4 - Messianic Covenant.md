# Week 4 — Messianic Covenant

## Big idea

The Davidic covenant gives royal and messianic form to the promises made to Abraham. Israel's failed kings and exile intensify hope for an everlasting King who will restore God's people and internalize God's covenant reign.

## Narrative — Rise and fall of Israel's kingdom

- **Saul:** the people's choice, rejected through covenant unfaithfulness (1 Samuel 8–15).
- **David:** God's anointed king, through whom the covenant kingdom is established (1 Samuel 16–2 Samuel 24).
- **Solomon:** wise temple-builder with a divided heart, whose legacy leads toward divided kingdoms and exile (1 Kings 1–11).

## Discussion

1. How were the promises to Abraham—offspring, land, and blessing—expressed in the Davidic kingdom?
2. How do later biblical writers identify 2 Samuel 7 and 1 Chronicles 17 as covenantal even though the originating narratives do not foreground the word *covenant*?
3. How does failed kingship sharpen the need for a faithful Messiah?

## Discovery — Davidic covenant

Study 1 Chronicles 16–17, 2 Samuel 7, and Psalm 89.

- **Context:** David restores the ark and desires to build a house for God.
- **Mediator:** David
- **Parties:** God, David, and his royal line
- **Scope:** Particular and dynastic, with implications for the nations
- **Responsibility:** Faithfulness of individual kings
- **Sign:** The messianic King and his everlasting kingdom
- **God's pledge:** An enduring house, throne, and dynasty
- **Consequences:** Fatherly discipline and temporary loss through exile, but not cancellation of God's pledge
- **Fulfillment in Christ:** The faithful, suffering, risen King reigns forever
- **Unique contribution:** The covenant promise becomes explicitly royal and messianic

## Discovery — Jeremiah 31 and typology

Typology recognizes earlier people, events, and institutions as historical patterns that God brings to fuller realization later in the story.

- **Noahic echoes:** Jeremiah 31:35–36
- **Abrahamic echoes:** Jeremiah 31:1–2, 5–7
- **Mosaic contrast and renewal:** Jeremiah 31:32–34
- **Davidic and kingdom echoes:** Jeremiah 31:12–14, 37

Jeremiah announces forgiveness, personal knowledge of God, and Torah written on the heart. The covenant story is not discarded; it is renewed, internalized, and carried toward universal fulfillment.

## Theological synthesis

The Davidic covenant narrows Abraham's offspring promise to a royal line, secures land through rest and dwelling, and places worldwide blessing under the rule of God's anointed king. Jesus embodies and fulfills this royal trajectory.

Jeremiah's new-covenant hope shows God's kingdom becoming inwardly formative and ultimately universal: forgiven people know God, bear his instruction within them, and await renewed creation under his reign.

## Formation and application

1. What does it mean to live a life of dwelling in God's house?
2. How does the repeated movement from chaos to order and back to chaos illuminate Israel's monarchy and exile?
3. What can failed kingship teach about divided desire, power, and the need for a faithful King?
