# Covenant Comparison

The covenants display both continuity and diversity. Together they reveal God's pledge, form God's people, and carry creation toward fulfillment in Christ.

## At a glance

| Covenant | Primary texts | Mediator | Scope | Sign | Central pledge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Creation/Adamic | Genesis 1–3 | Adam | Universal | Tree of Life | Shared vocation and promised redemption |
| Noahic | Genesis 6–9 | Noah | Universal | Rainbow | Preservation of creation |
| Abrahamic | Genesis 12, 15, 17 | Abraham | Lineal for universal blessing | Circumcision | Offspring, land, blessing |
| Mosaic/Sinaitic | Exodus 19–24; Deuteronomy 5–11 | Moses | National with priestly mission | Sabbath and Torah | Treasured people, priestly kingdom, holy nation |
| Davidic | 2 Samuel 7; 1 Chronicles 17; Psalm 89 | David | Dynastic with worldwide horizon | Messianic King | Everlasting house, throne, and kingdom |
| New covenant | Luke 22; 1 Corinthians 11; Hebrews 8–10 | Jesus Christ | Universal and global | Baptism and Lord's Supper | Forgiveness, new heart, Spirit, eternal life |

## Creation or Adamic covenant

- **Context:** Creator–creation relationship.
- **Parties:** God, humanity, and the created world.
- **Orientation:** Obedience and vocation within God's good order; God's redemptive promise after rebellion.
- **Curse:** Death and exile from Eden.
- **Christological fulfillment:** The last Adam obeys, bears death, and restores access to life.

## Noahic covenant

- **Context:** Earth corrupted by human violence and wickedness.
- **Parties:** God, Noah, descendants, and living creatures.
- **Orientation:** Unilateral divine preservation.
- **Curse:** The covenant itself is not revoked; God sustains creation toward renewal.
- **Christological fulfillment:** Salvation through judgment and preservation toward Revelation 21–22.

## Abrahamic covenant

- **Context:** God calls Abraham out of the world represented by Babel.
- **Parties:** God, Abraham, and his offspring.
- **Orientation:** God's unilateral pledge elicits faith and covenant response.
- **Curse:** Exclusion from covenant blessing for despising the covenant sign.
- **Christological fulfillment:** Jesus is the promised seed and heir through whom the nations are blessed.

## Mosaic covenant

- **Context:** God liberates Israel from slavery and brings them near at Sinai.
- **Parties:** God and Israel.
- **Orientation:** Covenant obedience enables Israel to inhabit its priestly vocation and enjoy blessing in the land.
- **Curse:** Disease, famine, defeat, and exile.
- **Christological fulfillment:** Jesus fulfills the law, bears its curse, and gives the Spirit.

## Davidic covenant

- **Context:** The ark returns, David desires to build God's house, and God promises to build David's house.
- **Parties:** God, David, and his royal line.
- **Orientation:** Unilateral dynastic pledge with discipline for unfaithful kings.
- **Curse:** Temporary loss and exile, but not the cancellation of God's messianic promise.
- **Christological fulfillment:** Jesus, the faithful Davidic King, suffers, rises, and reigns forever.

## New covenant

- **Context:** Passover and the passion of Jesus.
- **Parties:** God and a redeemed people from Israel and the nations.
- **Orientation:** Grace-based and Spirit-empowered because Christ fulfills covenant obedience.
- **Curse:** Christ bears the curse of the law on behalf of his people.
- **Christological fulfillment:** The covenant is embodied and mediated by Christ; its fullness arrives with his return.

## The pattern across the covenants

- God's promise creates the covenant reality.
- Human faithfulness is responsive participation, never the source of divine grace.
- Covenant forms a people for communion, holiness, and mission.
- Covenant signs make promise and belonging tangible.
- Human failure narrows hope toward a faithful representative.
- Jesus gathers the promises, responsibilities, signs, and hopes of the covenants into himself.
- New covenant fulfillment renews rather than discards God's purpose for creation.
