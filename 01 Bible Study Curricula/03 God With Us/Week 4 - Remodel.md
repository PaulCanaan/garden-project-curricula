# Week 4 — Remodel

## Jesus the True Temple → The Church as the Spirit's Temple

**Governing question:** How does temple reality move from place to person to people?

## Draft word-study focus

- *enoikeō* — dwell in; examine divine indwelling in its personal and communal contexts
- *epoikodomeō* — build upon; relate growth and construction imagery without collapsing their distinct passages

## Key texts

- John 1:14; 2:13–22; 7:37–39
- Matthew 27:50–51
- Colossians 1:19; 2:6–7
- 1 Corinthians 3:9–17; 6:19–20
- 2 Corinthians 6:16
- Ephesians 2:19–22
- 1 Peter 2:4–10

## Discovery

- Why does Jesus identify his body as the temple?
- How do incarnation, cross, resurrection, priesthood, sacrifice, and Spirit converge in him?
- What changes when the veil is torn?
- When Paul calls believers God's temple, when is his emphasis corporate and when individual?

## Trifold investigation

| Dwelling | Divine presence | Priestly sphere | Mission field |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jesus | Fullness of God dwelling bodily | High Priest and sacrificial Lamb | Israel and the nations addressed by his Kingdom mission |
| Church | Indwelling Holy Spirit | Body of Christ, royal priesthood, living sacrifices | World to which the Church is sent |

## Teaching movement

In Jesus the temple becomes personal without becoming private: the incarnate Son is God's climactic dwelling, priest, and sacrifice. By union with Christ and the gift of the Spirit, the Church becomes a people built together into God's dwelling and sent as a priestly witness.

## Formation

- **Reflect:** How does Jesus preserve holiness while removing exclusionary barriers?
- **Practice:** Treat bodies and congregational relationships as belonging to God's holy dwelling.
- **Prayer:** Resurrender personal and communal life to the Spirit's inhabiting presence.

## Guardrail

“Place → person → people” names canonical development, not the irrelevance of place, creation, or gathered worship.
