# Week 2 — Construction

## The Jerusalem Temple

**Governing question:** What makes sacred space the house of God's glory?

## Draft word-study focus

- *bayit* — house; examine when context refers to household, palace, or temple
- *kavod* — weight/glory; trace how divine glory fills sacred space

## Key texts

- 2 Samuel 7
- 1 Kings 5–8, especially 8:10–30
- Psalm 26:8; 27:4
- Exodus 40:34–38 as the tabernacle comparison

## Discovery

- What happens when glory fills tabernacle and temple?
- Why does David long for God's house, and why is Solomon the builder?
- What responsibilities fall upon king, priests, and covenant people?
- How does Solomon acknowledge that even heaven cannot contain God?

## Trifold investigation

| Divine presence | Priestly sphere | Mission field |
|---|---|---|
| Most Holy Place and ark symbolism | Holy Place, priestly ministry, and the ordered life of Israel | Courts, covenant people, resident foreigners, and nations addressed in Solomon's prayer |

Preserve the difference between sanctuary rooms and temple courts; “Holy Place” and “inner court” should not be treated as simple synonyms.

## Teaching movement

David longs and prepares; Solomon constructs and dedicates; God's glory fills the house as it filled the tabernacle. Yet the temple cannot contain the Creator, and the presence of glory bestows covenant responsibility rather than guaranteeing immunity from judgment.

## Formation

- **Reflect:** Do I seek sacred atmosphere, or the God whose glory makes space sacred?
- **Practice:** Let worship join reverence, justice, obedience, and welcome.
- **Prayer:** Ask that communal structures serve God's presence rather than substitute for it.
