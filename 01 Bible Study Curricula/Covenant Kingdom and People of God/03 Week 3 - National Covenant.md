# Week 3 — National Covenant

## Big idea

God brings Israel out of bondage, forms them through the wilderness, and brings them into promised life. Liberation is not an end in itself; God rescues a people for worship, holiness, communion, and priestly mission.

## Narrative — The Exodus Road

Retell the greater exodus in three movements:

1. **The way out:** deliverance from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 1–15).
2. **The way through:** formation in the wilderness and covenant at Sinai (Exodus 16–Deuteronomy 34, especially Exodus 19–24).
3. **The way in:** entry into and inhabitation of the promised land (Joshua 1–24).

See [[Exodus Road]].

## Discussion

1. If exile is fundamentally estrangement from God's presence, how is exodus its reversal?
2. How are the knowledge, presence, and promises of Yahweh restored across the way out, through, and in?
3. Why does Exodus 3:12 describe worship at the mountain as the purpose of deliverance?

## Discovery — Mosaic covenant

Study Exodus 19–24 and 34.

- **Context:** God remembers the ancestral covenant and liberates Israel from slavery.
- **Mediator:** Moses
- **Parties:** God and Israel
- **Scope:** Particular and national, with a priestly vocation for the nations
- **Responsibility:** Covenant obedience expressed through Torah
- **Sign:** Sabbath and the Torah as instructions for life
- **God's pledge:** Israel as treasured possession, kingdom of priests, and holy nation
- **Consequences:** Disease, famine, loss of land, and exile
- **Fulfillment in Christ:** Jesus fulfills and surpasses the law and bears its condemnation
- **Unique contribution:** A redeemed people are formed for holy life and mediating presence

## Theological synthesis

The exodus becomes a recurring scriptural pattern rather than an isolated event. God makes his name known, restores his promised presence in the tabernacle, and brings his people toward the inheritance pledged to Abraham.

The promised land is not merely a destination. It is the lived reality of God's covenant faithfulness. Likewise, wilderness is not wasted delay: it is the formative space in which rescued people learn trust, obedience, worship, and dependence.

## Formation and application

1. What does the identity “kingdom of priests and holy nation” require of God's people?
2. Where are you experiencing wilderness as testing and formation?
3. How can God's instructions become a way of life rather than a technique for earning acceptance?
