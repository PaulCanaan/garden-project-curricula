# Week 1 — Universal Covenant

## Big idea

Creation and fall provide the context for God's covenantal work of preservation, redemption, and restoration. God's faithfulness is visible from the beginning, even where the biblical text does not yet use the word *covenant*.

## Narrative — From creation to re-creation

Retell Genesis 1–11 through the movement between chaos and cosmos:

- **Creation:** unformed creation is ordered into a good and harmonious world.
- **Fall:** human rebellion turns ordered life toward moral chaos, death, and exile.
- **Flood:** de-creation is followed by re-creation and God's covenant with Noah.
- **Babel:** human grasping disorders the world again and prepares for God's call of Abraham.

## Discussion

1. Was there a covenant before Noah, even though *covenant* first appears explicitly in Genesis 6:18?
2. What is the Noahic covenant “established” upon: a new arrangement, or God's prior relationship and purpose for creation?
3. How should Hosea 6:7 and the creation context inform the question of an Adamic or creation covenant?

## Discovery — Creation covenant

Revisit Genesis 1–3 using the same lens.

- **Context:** Creator–creation relationship
- **Mediator:** Adam
- **Parties:** God, humanity, and creation
- **Scope:** Universal
- **Human responsibility:** Obedience to God's command and faithful image-bearing
- **Sign:** Tree of Life as embodied promise and condition
- **God's pledge:** Shared vocation and the promise of a redeemer
- **Consequences:** Death and exile from Eden
- **Fulfillment in Christ:** The obedient last Adam bears death and opens the way to life

## Theological synthesis

The language of “covenant of works” and “covenant of grace” highlights important dimensions of the story but can narrow attention to only one covenant element. “Covenant of creation” emphasizes the Creator's bond with humanity and the world; “covenant of redemption” emphasizes God's restorative pledge after the fall.

The Noahic covenant makes explicit God's enduring commitment to the created order. Creation can therefore be reread from the standpoint of its preservation and restoration: the God who orders the world does not abandon it to chaos.

## Formation and application

1. How does God's original design change the way you see God, other people, creation, and the meaning of life?
2. What does God's unilateral pledge reveal about his character?
3. How might trust in God's covenant faithfulness reshape your relationship with him?
