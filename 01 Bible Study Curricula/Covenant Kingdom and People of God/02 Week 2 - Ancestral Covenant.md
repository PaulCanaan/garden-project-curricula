# Week 2 — Ancestral Covenant

## Big idea

God calls Abraham out of the world represented by Babel and pledges offspring, land, and worldwide blessing. The promise rests finally on God's faithfulness, while faith-filled obedience is the fitting response of God's covenant partner.

## Narrative — Sojourners in the promised land

Genesis 12 opens a new movement in the story:

- **Abraham:** the father of faith leaves homeland and security in response to God's call.
- **Isaac:** the promised son participates in the sacrifice of Genesis 22.
- **Jacob:** the God-wrestler receives and carries the promise despite his instability.

The ancestors possess the promise while living as sojourners. Their identity rests more in God's pledge than in present circumstances.

## Discussion

1. What is the significance of God's presence passing alone between the divided animals in Genesis 15?
2. When the covenant is renewed with Isaac and Jacob, what makes it one covenant rather than several unrelated covenants?
3. How do offspring, land, and blessing develop across Genesis?

## Discovery — Abrahamic covenant

Study Genesis 12, 15, and 17.

- **Context:** God calls Abraham from the world of Babel and idolatry.
- **Mediator:** Abraham
- **Parties:** God, Abraham, and his offspring
- **Scope:** Particular and lineal, with a universal purpose
- **Response:** Faith and covenant faithfulness
- **Sign:** Circumcision (Genesis 17:10–14)
- **God's pledge:** Offspring, land, and blessing to all nations
- **Consequences:** Exclusion from covenant blessing for despising the sign
- **Fulfillment in Christ:** Jesus is the true seed and heir through whom the nations are blessed

## Theological synthesis

In ancient covenant ceremonies, both parties could pass between divided animals as a self-maledictory oath. In Genesis 15, God's presence passes through alone. The fulfillment of the promise rests on God's covenant faithfulness and grace rather than Abraham's achievement.

Obedience still matters—not as the foundation of the promise, but as faithful participation in covenant blessing. The outward sign of circumcision also anticipates inward renewal: Scripture moves toward the circumcision of the heart, accomplished by God's Spirit in Christ.

## Formation and application

1. What does it mean for Abraham's family—and the church—to become a blessing to the nations?
2. What does the posture of a sojourner reveal about faith, security, and hope?
3. Where might God be calling you to leave the familiar, trust his promise, and become a channel of blessing?
