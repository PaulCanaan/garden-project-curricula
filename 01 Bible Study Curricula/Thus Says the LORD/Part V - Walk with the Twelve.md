# Part V — Walk with the Twelve

## God Forms — The New People

**Big question:** What kind of people is God forming through judgment and restoration?

The historical sequence supplies the survey's skeleton; formation supplies its theological heartbeat. The Twelve also has a received literary order, so leaders should distinguish historical placement from canonical arrangement and acknowledge disputed dates rather than force certainty.

## Chapter 1 — Before the Exile: A People Called to Return

- **Representative prophets:** Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum
- **World:** Israel and Judah amid Assyrian domination and approaching exile
- **Motifs:** covenant infidelity, idolatry, justice, mercy, repentance, Day of the LORD, YHWH's rule over nations
- **Formation:** God warns and forms a repentant people.

Joel's date is disputed; use it here for thematic and pedagogical placement while naming the uncertainty.

## Chapter 2 — During the Exile: A People Called to Trust

- **Representative prophet:** Habakkuk; Daniel and Ezekiel receive separate parts
- **World:** Babylonian crisis and the moral scandal of violent empire
- **Motifs:** lament, watchful waiting, divine sovereignty, judgment of the oppressor, the righteous living by faithfulness
- **Formation:** God preserves and forms a faithful people.

## Chapter 3 — After the Exile: A People Called to Hope

- **Prophets:** Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi
- **World:** Persian-period restoration and the unfinished work of rebuilding Jerusalem
- **Motifs:** temple, priesthood, purified worship, covenant renewal, coming King and messenger
- **Formation:** God restores and forms a hopeful people.

## Formation movement

Return to God, trust him amid unresolved judgment, rebuild shared worship and life, and wait hopefully when restoration remains incomplete.
