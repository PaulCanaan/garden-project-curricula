# There Is a Garden Named Delight

## Purpose

Help participants receive creation as gift, identity, communion, vocation, and delight. The Story begins not with human failure but with the Creator who orders the world, draws near, and entrusts humanity with priestly cultivation.

## Central question

Who are we, and what were we created for?

## Key texts

- Genesis 1:27 — humanity in God's image
- Genesis 2:8, 15 — the garden in Eden and humanity's cultivating vocation
- Psalm 8:4–5 — small within creation yet crowned with glory and honor
- Psalm 139:14 — fearfully and wonderfully made
- Proverbs 8:30–31 — wisdom, delight, presence, and humanity
- Zephaniah 3:17 — the God who saves and delights in his people
- John 15:1 — the Father as gardener
- Ephesians 2:10 — created in Christ for good works prepared beforehand

## Teaching movements

### Creation as ordered gift

Genesis 1–2 emphasizes an ordered world of relationships, purpose, and divine presence. Questions about material origins are legitimate, but the session's primary focus is theological and vocational rather than resolving scientific chronology.

### Humanity as image and seed

Humans bear God's image rather than being described only “according to their kinds.” The seed metaphor holds received identity, hidden potential, direction toward an end, and fruitfulness for others.

### Sabbath as climax

The seventh day is holy and blessed. God's rest signifies creation brought into ordered purpose and divine presence, not divine exhaustion.

### Work and worship

Genesis 2:15 joins cultivation and keeping. Human work is stewardship within God's world and carries a priestly dimension: serving God through faithful care of what God loves.

### The God who delights

Eden means delight. The biblical Story begins with divine goodness and delight, so Christian identity begins in gift before achievement.

## Literary observation — forming and filling

The source highlights a two-triad pattern in Genesis 1:

| Forming | Realm | Filling | Inhabitants or rulers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Light distinguished from darkness | Day 4 | Sun, moon, and stars |
| Day 2 | Sky distinguished from waters | Day 5 | Birds and sea creatures |
| Day 3 | Land distinguished from seas; vegetation appears | Day 6 | Land creatures and humanity |

The pattern can be summarized as **A–B–C → A′–B′–C′ → Sabbath**. Use it as a literary observation that displays order and purpose, not as a shortcut around the text's interpretive questions.

## Storyline

The Creator is infinitely transcendent and intimately near. Humanity is small within the cosmos yet significant to God, planted in a garden of delight for communion, cultivation, and fruitful vocation.

## Formation response

- Receive identity as gift rather than performance.
- Name one part of creation or vocation entrusted to your care.
- Practice Sabbath as purposeful presence, gratitude, and trust.
