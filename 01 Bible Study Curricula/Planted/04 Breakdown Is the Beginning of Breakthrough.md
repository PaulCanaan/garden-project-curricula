# Breakdown Is the Beginning of Breakthrough

## Purpose

Bring personal testimony into the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus without making suffering itself redemptive. Participants learn that Christ enters death and opens the way to new creation.

## Central question

What has Jesus done in death and resurrection, and how does his Story reframe ours?

## Key texts

- Matthew 26:36–46 — Jesus' obedient struggle in Gethsemane
- John 12:24 — the grain of wheat dies and bears much fruit
- Romans 5:12–21 — Adam and Christ, death and reigning life
- Galatians 3:13 — Christ bears the curse of the tree
- 1 Corinthians 15:20–28, 42–49 — Christ as firstfruits and resurrection as sowing
- 1 Peter 1:23 — new birth through imperishable seed
- Revelation 12:11 — the Lamb and faithful testimony

## Teaching movements

### Our stories within the ultimate Story

Testimony is not self-display. It names God's action and locates personal identity within Jesus' life, death, resurrection, and ongoing grace.

### First Adam and last Adam

The source traces a biblical pattern: a human in a garden, before a tree, facing a decision. Adam grasps autonomy in Eden; Jesus submits to the Father's will in Gethsemane and bears the curse on the cross. Jesus is not merely a better private example but the representative head of new humanity.

### The seed enters the ground

John 12:24 interprets Jesus' death through seed logic. Burial is not the final word; through Jesus' death comes resurrection and abundant fruit.

### Creation finished; new creation inaugurated

The source pairs God's completed creation and Sabbath with Jesus' “finished” work on the cross. Use this as theological poetry rather than a claim that resurrection or Pentecost are unnecessary: the cross accomplishes salvation, the resurrection inaugurates new creation, and the Spirit forms its people.

## Storyline

“You are not buried; you are planted” is resurrection language. It must never minimize grief, trauma, or ongoing harm. In Christ, suffering does not receive authority to define the whole person or the end of the Story; God can bring life without calling evil good.

## Formation response

- Tell the truth about both wound and grace.
- Locate identity first in God's action and belonging in Christ.
- Name one sign of resurrection hope without forcing resolution.
