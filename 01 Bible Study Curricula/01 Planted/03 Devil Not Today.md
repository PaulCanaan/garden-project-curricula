# Devil Not Today

## Purpose

Name sin, temptation, accusation, distorted desire, and spiritual conflict truthfully while keeping Christ's saving victory central.

## Central question

What has disordered human desire and relationship, and how does Jesus meet us within that brokenness?

## Key texts

- Genesis 3 — mistrust, autonomous judgment, alienation, shame, and exile
- Matthew 4:1–11 — Jesus resists the tempter in the wilderness
- Matthew 9:12–13 — Jesus comes for the sick and calls sinners
- John 10:10 — destruction contrasted with abundant life
- Romans 3:23 — all fall short of God's glory
- 1 John 1:8–9 — self-deception, confession, and cleansing
- 1 John 2:16 — disordered desire, sight, and pride
- James 1:14–15 — desire, sin, and death
- Ephesians 6:11–12 — conflict beyond flesh and blood

## Program

[[Dartboard]] embodies “missing the mark” while explicitly refusing spiritual ranking or shame.

## Teaching movements

### Sin as more than failure

“Missing the mark” is one biblical image for sin, but the session should also name rebellion, bondage, guilt, distorted desire, alienation, and corrupt spiritual powers.

### Autonomy leads to alienation

Genesis 3 portrays humanity grasping the authority to define good and evil independently of God's wisdom. The result is not neutral intellectual advancement but fractured relationship with God, one another, the self, and creation.

### Guilt, shame, and fear

- **Guilt:** what I did was wrong.
- **Shame:** I am irredeemably wrong and must hide.
- **Fear:** I cannot safely approach God.

Keep these distinct so the gospel can answer each without denying responsibility or pain.

### Temptation and accusation

The enemy both entices people away from trust and accuses them after failure. Christian resistance therefore involves truthful discernment, confession, assurance, Scripture, community, and the victory of Christ.

## Comparative observation — Eve and Jesus

The manuscript places Genesis 3 and Matthew 4 beside 1 John 2:16:

| Pattern | Eve in Genesis 3 | Jesus' wilderness testing |
|---|---|---|
| Desire of the flesh | Fruit seen as good for food | Stones invited to become bread |
| Desire of the eyes | Fruit pleasing to the eye | Kingdoms and splendor displayed |
| Pride of life | Fruit desired for gaining wisdom | Rule offered apart from obedient suffering |

This is a canonical comparison, not a claim that every temptation fits three rigid categories.

## Storyline

The field contains wheat and weeds (Matthew 13:24–30, 37–39). Evil is real, but it does not own the field or determine the ending. The Son of Man sows good seed, and judgment and harvest belong to God.

## Formation response

- Name temptation without confusing it with identity.
- Confess sin without turning confession into self-condemnation.
- Receive assurance and identify one concrete practice of resistance in community.
