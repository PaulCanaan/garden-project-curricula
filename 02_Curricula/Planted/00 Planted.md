# Planted

## Transformation promise

Participants will learn to see their lives within the full biblical story—from creation and alienation through the death and resurrection of Jesus toward kingdom life, communal formation, fruitful vocation, and new creation.

## Governing image

A seed is small, hidden, embodied, future-oriented, and capable of multiplication. *Planted* uses that image to tell the story of Jesus, the kingdom, faith, formation, and the church:

> You were not merely buried; in Christ, you are planted.

This is resurrection hope, not a denial of grief. The seed enters darkness and death; life comes through God's action, in God's time, toward fruit for others.

## Governing biblical story

**Creation → fall → redemption → new creation**

The curriculum resists a “half story” that begins only with human sin and ends with escape. Creation reveals gift and vocation; sin produces alienation; Jesus enters death and inaugurates new creation; the Spirit forms a people whose shared life bears fruit and plants new seeds. See [[B Full Story of the Bible]].

## Three seeds

*Planted.* tells one Story through three interwoven expressions of the seed:

- **The seed of Jesus:** the promised seed who enters death and rises as the beginning of new creation.
- **The seed of the Kingdom:** God's reign begins in hiddenness, grows patiently, and bears disproportionate fruit.
- **The seed of faith:** a person receives life in Christ, grows through grace and truth, and becomes fruitful for others.

## Storyline

1. You are a seed planted in a good creation for communion and vocation.
2. Sin buries human life in alienation, distorted desire, accusation, shame, and death.
3. Jesus enters the broken world, obeys in Gethsemane, dies on the tree, and rises as the beginning of new creation.
4. Resurrection and the kingdom reorient perception: what appears upside down may be the right-side-up life of God's future breaking into the present.
5. Nurtured by grace and truth over time, people become fruitful trees; together they become a forest—the church as a life-giving community that plants again.

## Formation design

Each session should integrate:

- **Scripture:** close and canonical reading
- **Story:** the full biblical arc and testimony
- **Experience:** a safe, accessible facilitated program
- **Reflection:** attention to desire, identity, relationships, and vocation
- **Practice:** one concrete response sustained beyond the gathering
- **Prayer:** receiving and answering God's presence
- **Community:** being known, loved, challenged, and sent with others

## Featured workshops

Two larger formation experiences accompany and complete the five-movement curriculum without becoming additional chapters:

- [[Eternity Planted]] — enlarges participants' vision toward Kingdom, God's presence, eternal life, and new creation: **Where is the Story going?**
- [[From Planted to Plant]] — commissions participants toward interdependent church life, vocation, disciple-making, and mission: **What does a planted person become?**

The first looks upward and forward; the second looks outward and sends. Their programs and frameworks may appear within the core curriculum, but the workshops integrate those assets into larger summit experiences.

## Ministry relationship
