# Week 3 — Reconstruction

## Exile, Return, and the Unfinished Temple

**Governing question:** What does faithfulness look like when restoration remains incomplete?

## Draft word-study focus

- *qodesh* — holiness/sanctuary; distinguish holiness, holy things, and sanctuary by context
- *avad* — work/serve/worship; explore how service joins vocation and worship without relying on a single English gloss

## Key texts

- 2 Kings 24–25
- Ezekiel 8–11; 40–48
- Ezra 3; 6; 9
- Nehemiah 9
- Haggai 2; Zechariah 2; 6; 14
- Psalm 127:1

## Discovery

- What was lost when Jerusalem and its temple were destroyed?
- Why do return and reconstruction not feel like final homecoming?
- How do the prophets imagine a greater return of glory and an end-time dwelling?
- What must be rebuilt besides physical structures?

## Teaching movement

Covenant rupture leads to exile and temple destruction. Return permits rebuilding, but reconstructed space does not by itself restore faithful worship or fulfill prophetic hope. The people live between promise and fulfillment, waiting for God to build the true house.

## Formation

- **Reflect:** What does it feel like to inhabit an unfinished restoration?
- **Practice:** Offer daily work as covenant service while refusing to measure God's future by present impressiveness.
- **Prayer:** Lament what remains broken and ask for faithful hope rather than nostalgia.

## Development questions

- Qualify precisely what is meant by the second temple lacking “former glory.”
- Distinguish Ezra–Nehemiah's historical reconstruction from Ezekiel's visionary temple.
- Decide how much Second Temple history belongs in the core versus deep-study resolution.
