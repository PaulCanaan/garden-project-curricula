# Bible Word Study Practice

The active *God With Us* draft uses one or two Hebrew or Greek terms each week. This practice should train careful reading, not promise access to secret meanings unavailable in translation.

## A responsible workflow

1. **Choose a consequential word.** Select a repeated or theologically important term from the week's primary passage.
2. **Identify the actual form.** Record language, lemma, inflected form, and transliteration using a trusted tool.
3. **Read the immediate context.** Determine how syntax, genre, parallelism, and argument constrain meaning here.
4. **Survey usage.** Compare occurrences by the same author, corpus, and testament before moving to distant texts.
5. **Map the semantic range.** List plausible senses; do not combine every possible gloss into one occurrence.
6. **Compare translations.** Ask why responsible translations differ and what interpretive decision each reflects.
7. **Trace theology canonically.** Only after contextual work, explore how the term or concept develops across Scripture.
8. **State a modest conclusion.** Explain what the study clarifies, what remains uncertain, and why it matters for the passage.

## Weekly draft terms

| Week | Source-document terms | Development caution |
|---|---|---|
| Blueprint | *shavat*, *mishkan* | Relate rest and dwelling without claiming identical meanings |
| Construction | *bayit*, *kavod* | *Bayit* means house in a broad range; context determines temple reference |
| Reconstruction | *qodesh*, *avad* | Distinguish holy/sanctuary senses and work/serve/worship senses |
| Remodel | *enoikeō*, *epoikodomeō* | Study each within its own Pauline argument before combining them |
| Expansion | *skēnē*, *naos* | *Skēnē* is a noun; verify any “tabernacling” claim against the relevant verb and context |

## Common fallacies to avoid

- Treating etymology as present meaning
- Importing every lexical sense into one verse
- Building doctrine on a Strong's number or English concordance alone
- Assuming two passages share a concept merely because an English translation uses the same word
- Ignoring grammar, discourse, genre, and historical setting
- Claiming that an original-language gloss overturns all major translations without strong evidence
