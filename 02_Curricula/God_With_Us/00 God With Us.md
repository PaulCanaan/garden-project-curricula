# God With Us

## The Homecoming of the Sojourners

> **The ultimate goal is not a building, but God dwelling with humanity.**

*God With Us* traces God's desire to dwell with humanity from creation to new creation. It is *Sojourners; Part II*: the covenant curriculum follows the journey of God's covenant partners, while this curriculum follows their homecoming in the presence of God.

The historical temple is neither incidental nor ultimate. It participates in a biblical trajectory that moves from cosmic and garden temple, through tabernacle and Jerusalem temple, to Jesus, the Spirit-indwelt Church, and the garden-city of new creation.

## Curriculum identity

- **Governing biblical theme:** the presence and dwelling place of God
- **Governing narrative metaphor:** building a house that becomes a home
- **Controlling question:** How does the Creator make his home with redeemed humanity?
- **Emotional center:** longing, exile, homecoming, glory, and rest
- **Theological foundation:** temple theology within the Immanuel Principle
- **Signature assets:** [[Building A House]], the trifold temple structure, [[T2T]], word-study practice, and [[The Church Workshop]]

## Formation promise

Participants will learn to recognize the temple trajectory across Scripture, see Jesus as the true and greater Temple, understand the Church as a people built together by the Spirit, and practice worship, witness, and hospitality as a tabernacling presence in the world.

## The Immanuel Principle

The covenant study introduces the God who binds himself to and walks with his people. *God With Us* highlights the destination and fulfillment of that promise: God makes his home with his people.

- **Covenant:** Who are God's people, and how do they walk with him?
- **Temple:** Where does God dwell, and how are the sojourners brought home?
- **Christ:** the Mediator of the new covenant and true Temple
- **Church:** the new-covenant people built into a dwelling by the Spirit
- **New creation:** God and the Lamb dwelling with humanity without boundary

## Foundation → Frame → Dwelling

### Foundation — God establishes

God's appearing and saving action ground every dwelling. The Gospel lays the foundation, finally revealed in Jesus Christ.

### Frame — God forms

God gives a pattern of ordered life to the people among whom he dwells. The frame makes a dwelling recognizable before the story is complete.

### Dwelling — God fills

A house becomes a home when it is inhabited. God's presence fills tabernacle, temple, Christ, Church, and finally all creation according to their distinct covenantal places in the Story.

[[Building A House]] teaches this lens through embodied pattern recognition rather than an opening lecture.

## Recurring trifold temple structure

Each major dwelling is examined through three concentric spheres:

1. **Divine presence** — the holy center where God's presence is especially revealed
2. **Priestly sphere** — people and practices ordered toward service to God on behalf of others and service to others on behalf of God
3. **Mission field** — the wider sphere into which God's presence and blessing extend

These are typological correspondences to investigate, not identical architectural equations. Historical differences among Eden, tabernacle, temple, Jesus, Church, and new creation must remain visible.

## Recurring learning rhythm

Each week currently contains:

1. A focused [[Bible Word Study Practice|word study]]
2. Key biblical passages
3. Discovery and discussion questions
4. Completion of the trifold temple structure
5. A concise theological conclusion
6. Prayer and worship
7. Reflection and journaling

This rhythm is provisional until pilot testing. Word studies must illuminate usage in context rather than treat roots or dictionary glosses as hidden meanings.

## Development guardrails

- Do not collapse heaven, creation, Eden, tabernacle, temple, Jesus, Church, and new creation into interchangeable realities.
- Do not infer theology from architectural similarity alone; Scripture establishes the correspondences.
- Distinguish tabernacle rooms, temple courts, people, and functions carefully; the current trifold chart remains a development hypothesis.
- Preserve both individual indwelling and the New Testament's strong corporate temple identity.
- Do not imply that human technique manufactures God's presence.
- Treat the second temple's “lack of former glory” as a claim requiring careful textual and historical qualification.
- Verify every Hebrew and Greek term, Strong's number, gloss, and semantic claim before publication.
- Worship-song selections and quotations require pastoral, theological, and rights review.
