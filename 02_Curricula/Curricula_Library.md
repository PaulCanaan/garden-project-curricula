# Curricula Library

The formation-resource system of [[00 The Garden Project Ministry|The Garden Project]]. This directory is the internal source of truth for resources the ministry develops, pilots, improves, and eventually publishes.

Interpretive map: [[Curricula MOC]]

The library turns the Christian Worldview OS into teachable pathways. Theology supplies the substance; frameworks organize insight; programs make ideas experiential; curricula form people over time; workshops create focused entry points; CoJourners help the resources become faithful shared life.

## Library map

- [[00 Bible Study Curricula|Bible Study Curricula]] — session-based journeys through Scripture and biblical theology
- [[00 Program Library|Program Library]] — reusable, facilitated experiential-learning activities
- [[00 Framework Library|Framework Library]] — models for interpretation, discernment, formation, and action
- [[00 Workshop Library|Workshop Library]] — focused learning experiences derived from curricula, programs, and frameworks

## Relationship to The Garden Project

The Garden Project is the ministry and eventual publisher. `07 Curricula` is its editorial and formation workshop.

- **The ministry** guards mission, audience, voice, pastoral accountability, partnerships, and distribution.
- **The library** preserves the master content, sources, frameworks, facilitator logic, safety guidance, and revision history.
- **CoJourners**, formed directly through The Garden Project ministry, help people inhabit the material in real communities.
- **Pilots and feedback** return lived learning to the library before publication.

See [[02 Ministry Ecosystem]] and [[03 Publishing and Stewardship Pipeline]].

## Formation architecture

| Layer | Primary question | Typical artifact |
|---|---|---|
| Biblical and theological foundation | What is true in the story of God? | Bible study curriculum |
| Framework | What pattern helps people understand and act? | Reusable model or diagnostic |
| Program | What shared experience makes the insight tangible? | Facilitated activity or pathway |
| Workshop | How can people enter and practice this material in a focused setting? | Retreat, seminar, or intensive |

## Stewardship rules

1. Do not call an internal note “published” merely because it is complete enough to teach once.
2. Preserve provenance, biblical grounding, permissions, and revision history.
3. Separate master content from participant and leader editions.
4. Pilot before public release, and record what participants and facilitators actually experience.
5. Require pastoral, theological, safety, accessibility, and editorial review appropriate to the resource.
6. Publish through The Garden Project with clear versioning and attribution.
7. Keep the resource revisable after release; publication is stewardship, not finality.

## Two-axis status language

Publishable artifacts use two fields because content maturity and publication readiness are not the same thing.

### `development_status`

- **seed:** concept, outline, or recovered source not yet coherent as a whole
- **developing:** coherent draft under active construction
- **developed:** complete internal master that still needs integrated field use
- **pilot-ready:** prepared for bounded, supervised use with feedback collection
- **validated:** revised through repeated use and evidence
- **retired:** preserved for history but no longer recommended

### `publication_status`

- **internal-master:** working source of truth; not a public release
- **ministry-approved:** approved for accountable Garden Project ministry use
- **publication-ready:** edited, reviewed, accessible, attributed, and packaged for reuse
- **published:** released with a version and distribution record
- **retired:** withdrawn from recommended distribution

Indexes, MOCs, roadmaps, and pipelines may use `status: active`; they do not inherit publication status merely because they describe publishable resources.
