# Curricula MOC

## Orientation

The Garden Project curricula are modular biblical-formation journeys. Each retells the one biblical metanarrative through a distinct governing lens, converges on Jesus Christ, and invites participants to inhabit the Story in worship, community, vocation, and mission.

The curricula share DNA without becoming interchangeable. The Story is constant; the camera angle, theological foundation, symbolic language, challenge level, and signature assets change.

## Three design principles

1. **Biblically outlined** — the curriculum's movement arises from Scripture's own storyline, patterns, and themes.
2. **Christ-centered** — every lens finds its center and fulfillment in Jesus without erasing historical or covenantal development.
3. **Formation-oriented** — knowledge becomes a way of seeing, loving, practicing, belonging, and participating in God's mission.

## Shared theological method

Read Scripture as one unified Story in which God progressively reveals himself through creation, covenant, Kingdom, temple, prophetic promise, Christ, the Spirit-formed Church, and new creation.

- **Biblical metanarrative:** the Story's coherent movement
- **Christology:** the Story's center
- **New creation:** the Story's eschatological horizon
- **Biblical theology and typology:** the method of tracing development and fulfillment
- **Formation:** learning to inhabit rather than merely explain the Story

## The Immanuel Principle

Covenant introduces the God who binds himself to and walks with his people. Temple theology highlights where that covenant journey is going: God dwelling with his people.

- **Covenant:** Who are God's people, and how do they journey by promise?
- **Temple:** Where does God dwell, and how are the sojourners brought home?
- **Christ:** the Mediator of the new covenant and the true Temple
- **Church:** God's new-covenant people and living dwelling
- **New creation:** the fulfilled reality of God with humanity

## Modular architecture

Each curriculum can unfold at different resolutions while preserving its governing Story and destination.

| Resolution | Typical setting | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Intensive | Conference, retreat, or one-day introduction | Preserve the whole arc; reduce depth and practice load |
| Core | Five-session class or group | One major movement per gathering |
| Extended | Ten-session group | Separate biblical discovery from formation and practice |
| Deep study | Fifteen or more sessions | Add word study, sources, typology, reading, practice, and leader development |

Shorter does not mean simplified beyond coherence; deeper does not mean content-heavy without formation.

## Reusable and signature assets

- [[Frameworks MOC|Frameworks]] are curriculum-independent thinking and formation tools.
- [[Programs MOC|Programs]] create memorable embodied experiences.
- [[Workshops MOC|Workshops]] are focused formation experiences that may accompany a curriculum.
- [[00 CoJourner Program|CoJourner]] supplies the common relational practice through which any curriculum can be inhabited.

Each curriculum retains signature assets. *Planted.* uses the most programs and foundational frameworks because it is seeker-friendly and formative rather than content-heavy. Other curricula may be more theologically focused and carry one featured workshop or entrance program.

## Development sequence

1. Reconcile and pilot the complete *Planted.* master.
2. Prepare Covenant as a Garden Project leader and participant edition.
3. Develop and pilot *God With Us* from its integrated active draft: verify the trifold correspondences and word studies, then produce leader and participant editions.
4. Develop and pilot *Thus Says the LORD* in its seven-part Entrance → Journey → Exit architecture; verify every chapter's contextual and Christological claims before publication.
5. Preserve the curricular sequence while allowing contextual entry and modular formats.
