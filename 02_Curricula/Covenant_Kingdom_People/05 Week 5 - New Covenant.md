# Week 5 — New Covenant

## Big idea

Jesus is the new King who inaugurates new creation through a new exodus. His death, burial, and resurrection fulfill the covenant story and open a way that his covenant people now learn to walk.

## Narrative — New King, new exodus, new creation

Luke 9:30–31 names Jesus' coming death in Jerusalem as his *exodus*. Retell the gospel through the [[Exodus Road]]:

1. **The way out — death:** Jesus, the Passover Lamb, gives his life to deliver humanity from sin, death, and exile.
2. **The way through — burial:** Jesus passes through the wilderness of death and rests in the tomb after completing redemption.
3. **The way in — resurrection:** Jesus conquers death and opens the way into God's new-creation life.

## Discussion

1. How does Jesus' baptism anticipate the greater “baptism” of his crucifixion?
2. How are Passover, the Lord's Supper, and the future kingdom banquet connected?
3. What does it mean to say Jesus fulfills rather than merely replaces the prior covenants?

## Discovery — The blood-sealed new covenant

Study Luke 22 and 1 Corinthians 11.

- **Context:** Passover and Jesus' approaching passion
- **Mediator:** Jesus Christ
- **Parties:** God and a redeemed people from Israel and the nations
- **Scope:** Universal and global
- **Responsibility:** Spirit-empowered covenant life flowing from Christ's completed obedience
- **Signs:** Baptism and the Lord's Supper
- **God's pledge:** Forgiveness, a new heart, indwelling Spirit, eternal life, and final communion
- **Curse:** Christ bears the covenant curse on behalf of his people
- **Fulfillment:** Already accomplished in Christ and awaiting consummation at his return
- **Unique contribution:** Jesus is both covenant mediator and covenant embodiment

## Fulfillment of the covenants

- **Davidic:** Jesus is David's enthroned and everlasting heir (Luke 1; Acts 2).
- **Mosaic:** Jesus fulfills the law, bears its curse, and gives the Spirit (Hebrews 8–10; 2 Corinthians 3).
- **Abrahamic:** Jesus is the promised seed through whom the nations receive blessing (Romans 4; Galatians 3–4).
- **Noahic:** Jesus carries creation through judgment toward renewed creation (1 Peter 3; 2 Peter 3; Romans 8).
- **Adamic:** Jesus is the last Adam whose obedience and resurrection bring life (Romans 5; 1 Corinthians 15).

## Theological synthesis

Baptism recalls rescue through water and signifies union with Christ in death and resurrection. The Lord's Supper holds together past remembrance, present participation, and future anticipation of the messianic banquet.

The already/not-yet pattern clarifies both sides of salvation: people are saved **from** sin and death and saved **for** communion and abundant life in God's kingdom. Between Christ's completed work and final glorification lies the formative pilgrimage of sanctification.

Jesus walks the Exodus Road first and becomes the way for his followers. Discipleship is therefore covenant living: walking with Christ, becoming like Christ, and moving toward the promised renewal of all things.

## Formation and application

1. How has this covenant study clarified the unity of Scripture and the centrality of Christ?
2. How do God's people inhabit God's story rather than merely understand it?
3. If early Christians were followers of “the Way,” how should covenant identity reshape daily life, community, purpose, and practice?
