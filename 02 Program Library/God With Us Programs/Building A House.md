# Building A House

## Outcome

Participants create a recognizable house from ordinary objects, discover how a frame makes an unfinished structure recognizable, and begin using **Foundation → Frame → Dwelling** as an interpretive lens for God's dwelling places throughout Scripture.

The program does not summarize *God With Us*. It gives participants a way to see. Its success is measured when people begin asking, **“Where is the frame?”** during the rest of the curriculum.

## Program DNA

- **Simple:** one challenge and one discovery.
- **Replicable:** it can run in a church meeting room, living room, classroom, retreat space, or outdoors.
- **Meaningful:** participants experience pattern recognition before it is explained.
- **Memorable:** the improvised frame becomes the image they carry into the study.
- **Portable:** use what is already present; no building kit is required.

## Materials

Required: ordinary, safe objects already available in the room or setting.

Examples include:

- chairs, cushions, empty boxes, paper, books, bags, jackets, water bottles, sticks, or rope;
- optional timer;
- optional camera, with participant permission, to record each creation.

Do not use people as structural pieces. Do not use fragile, sharp, valuable, private, unstable, or heavy objects.

## Preparation and setup

1. Inspect the room and identify objects that may and may not be moved.
2. Mark any off-limits furniture, equipment, personal belongings, exits, or accessibility routes.
3. Choose a clear building area for each group of three to six participants.
4. Preserve an unobstructed route through the room.
5. Prepare an equivalent tabletop, drawing, or observation option for participants who should not lift or move objects.
6. Plan where groups will gather for the gallery walk and debrief.

## Facilitator flow

### 1. Frame the experience — 3 minutes

Say:

> “In your group, use safe objects already available in this room to create something that everyone will immediately recognize as a house. You may not speak while building. You have five minutes. It does not need walls, decoration, or a finished interior; it only needs to be recognizable.”

Add:

> “Participate only in ways that are safe and comfortable for you. You may gather objects, arrange lightweight items, observe for hazards, keep time, or use the tabletop or drawing alternative. Ask before touching anyone's belongings.”

Do not mention Foundation → Frame → Dwelling yet.

### 2. Build without words — 5–7 minutes

- Divide participants into groups of three to six.
- Begin the timer.
- Let groups improvise without coaching the shape.
- Intervene only for safety, blocked access, prohibited objects, or misunderstood rules.
- Give a one-minute warning.

### 3. Gallery walk — 4–5 minutes

Invite participants to walk or move through the display area and silently observe each creation. Provide a seated viewing option or bring images/objects into view when needed.

Ask participants to notice:

- When did you first recognize a house?
- What feature communicated “house” most quickly?
- What was present even though the house was unfinished?

### 4. Notice together — 7–10 minutes

Begin with experience before explanation:

1. At what point did your collection of objects become recognizable as a house?
2. Which elements were necessary? Which were decoration?
3. Which house communicated its identity most quickly, and why?
4. Could you recognize an unfinished house? What allowed you to do that?
5. What made each house different? What pattern did they share?
6. What makes a house more than a random pile of objects?
7. What is a house ultimately for?

Let participants name **structure**, **pattern**, or **frame** before supplying the language when possible.

### 5. Reveal the interpretive lens — 8–10 minutes

Introduce the three movements.

#### Foundation — God establishes

- A foundation gives the house its ground and stability.
- In Scripture, God's dwelling begins with God's initiative, promise, grace, and ultimately Christ.
- **Recurring question:** What has God established here?

#### Frame — God forms

- A frame makes the identity and arrangement of a house recognizable before it is complete.
- The frame is the emphasis of this program because *God With Us* trains participants to recognize recurring sacred-space patterns.
- **Recurring question:** What pattern is God building here?

Within each major dwelling place, look for a threefold movement:

1. **God's presence** — the holy center or source.
2. **Priestly sphere** — people formed to serve God on behalf of others and serve others on behalf of God.
3. **Mission field** — the wider world into which God's presence and blessing are meant to extend.

#### Dwelling — God fills

- A house exists to be inhabited; a frame is not the destination.
- The biblical storyline moves toward communion: God making his home with humanity.
- **Recurring question:** How is God's presence revealed, and what life flows from it?

Summarize:

> **God establishes the foundation, forms the frame, and fills the dwelling.**

### 6. Biblical transition — 5 minutes

Read Ephesians 2:19–22 or 1 Peter 2:4–5.

Say:

> “Every group built a different house, yet we recognized the shared frame. Throughout *God With Us*, we will encounter dwelling places that look different—the garden, tabernacle, temple, Jesus, the Church, and the new creation. We will learn to recognize the same Story: God comes near, forms a priestly people, and extends his presence and blessing into the world.”

Preview the recurring structures:

- **Garden temple:** garden / land of Eden / world outside Eden
- **Tabernacle and temple:** Most Holy Place / Holy Place / courtyard
- **Jesus:** fullness of God's presence / great High Priest / Israel and the nations
- **Church:** indwelling Spirit / Body of Christ and living sacrifices / world to which the Church is sent

These are theological correspondences to investigate during the curriculum, not equations the activity itself proves.

### 7. Integrate — 3–5 minutes

Invite participants to reflect privately or in pairs:

- What “frame” currently gives structure to my life with God?
- Is there structure without communion, or inspiration without durable form?
- Where might God be forming us together into a dwelling and a people sent in love?

Close with a prayer that God would give participants eyes to recognize his presence and make them a faithful dwelling in Christ by the Spirit.

## Facilitator key points

1. **The frame is the pedagogical center.** Do not turn the activity into a lecture about construction technique.
2. **Pattern recognition is the skill.** Different structures can share a recognizable identity.
3. **Foundation, Frame, and Dwelling belong together.** Foundation without frame has no durable form; frame without dwelling misses its purpose; dwelling is gift, not something people manufacture.
4. **God acts first.** God establishes, forms, and fills; participants learn to recognize and inhabit God's work.
5. **Temple theology is relational and missional.** God's presence forms a priestly people whose vocation extends blessing outward.
6. **The house is an entrance, not proof.** Scripture establishes the theology; the activity trains attention.

## Safety and accessibility

- Keep exits, walkways, fire equipment, and accessible routes open.
- Do not allow climbing, standing on furniture, throwing objects, unstable stacking, or moving heavy furniture.
- Ask permission before touching personal belongings or photographing creations.
- Provide tabletop construction, drawing, object-selection, timekeeping, safety observation, and verbal debrief roles.
- Allow speaking when silence creates an access barrier; the nonverbal rule serves attention, not exclusion.
- Do not rank houses by beauty, height, strength, or artistic ability.
- Participation and public explanation are optional.

## Limits

- A physical house cannot prove the Bible's temple patterns; it only trains participants to notice structure.
- Foundation → Frame → Dwelling is an interpretive aid, not an exhaustive doctrine of temple or formation.
- Sacred-space correspondences must respect the historical and covenantal differences among Eden, tabernacle, temple, Jesus, Church, and new creation.
- God's presence is not produced by correct structure, institutional design, emotional atmosphere, or human technique.
- “House” language must not reduce the Church to a building; the New Testament emphasizes a people built together in Christ.

## Variations

- **Small group:** Build one house together, then ask each participant which feature made it recognizable.
- **Large group:** Use multiple building zones and appoint a safety observer for each group.
- **Low-mobility / tabletop:** Use paper, cards, cups, pens, or a quick drawing.
- **Outdoors:** Use safe found objects without damaging plants, habitats, or property; leave the site as found.
- **Short version:** Three minutes to build, three observation questions, and the three-part reveal.
- **Nonverbal-access alternative:** Permit limited communication or assign participants to design, select, arrange, observe, or interpret according to access needs.
