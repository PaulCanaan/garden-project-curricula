# Storytelling

## Outcome

Participants prepare and share a concise testimony that is honest about their life, centered on Jesus rather than self-dramatization, and attentive to God's ongoing work.

## Preparation

Send prompts several days in advance:

1. What was life like before you began following Jesus, or before a significant encounter with him?
2. How did you encounter Jesus or become aware of his work?
3. What is life like now? Where is Jesus changing you, and what remains unfinished?

Invite participants to write, read aloud, and time a draft. Aim for three to five minutes. Tell them they may share a smaller story, pass, or keep details general.

## Facilitator flow

### 1. Establish the covenant — 10 minutes

- Listen without fixing, comparing, interrogating, or giving unsolicited advice.
- Do not repeat personal stories outside the group.
- Confidentiality has limits when someone may be in danger, abuse is disclosed, or church policy requires escalation.
- No one must disclose trauma, illegal behavior, medical details, sexuality, family secrets, or another person's private information.
- A testimony may include doubt, slow change, and unanswered prayer.

Read John 21:25 and Revelation 12:11. Explain that testimonies point toward the story of Jesus; they do not make the speaker the hero.

### 2. Share — 35–60 minutes

- Invite rather than assign the first speaker.
- Keep a visible timer; give a gentle signal near four minutes.
- After each story, allow a moment of silence and one simple response: “Thank you for trusting us.”
- Use pairs or triads if the group is larger than twelve.

### 3. Discern patterns — 10–15 minutes

Without analyzing individuals, ask:

1. What did these stories reveal about who God is?
2. What actions of God appeared?
3. What identities were received or restored?
4. What responses of faith became possible?

Connect the answers to [[Gospel Flow]]: **who God is → what God does → who we are → what we do**.

### 4. Close — 5 minutes

Thank God for common grace and particular stories. If appropriate, share communion as remembrance of the story that gathers every testimony—the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus.

## Follow-on program

For a second gathering, invite each person to tell one story about Jesus—his character, claim, action, Old Testament anticipation, death, or resurrection. This moves from “my story” to “the Ultimate Story.”

## Safety and pastoral care

- Never reward the most dramatic story.
- Do not pressure participants to reconcile, forgive, or reinterpret trauma during the session.
- Have a named pastoral follow-up path before beginning.
- If a disclosure indicates imminent danger or abuse, follow church policy rather than processing it publicly.
- Secure consent before recording, publishing, or reusing any testimony.

## Limits

The three-part structure is a scaffold, not a demand for a clean conversion arc. Some people cannot identify one moment of encounter; some lives grow through covenant nurture; some stories are presently unresolved.
