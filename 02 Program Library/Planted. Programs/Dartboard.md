# Dartboard

## Outcome

Participants experience the difference between clear sight, increasing distance, and impaired perception, then connect the image of “missing the mark” to universal brokenness, grace, and God's saving action in Christ.

## Materials

- Magnetic or hook-and-loop dartboard only
- Soft darts
- Floor markers for throwing distances
- Optional score sheet
- Optional vision-obscuring glasses for a facilitator demonstration

## Setup

Secure the board on a clear wall. Establish a throwing line and a no-entry zone between the line and target. Test every dart. Do not use sharp steel-tip darts.

## Facilitator flow

### 1. Frame — 3 minutes

State the purpose clearly: this is not a test of worth or righteousness. Skill, disability, and prior experience will affect results. The activity is an illustration of limits, not a measurement of anyone's spiritual state.

### 2. Three rounds — 10 minutes

Each person may take up to three throws per round or choose an observer/scorekeeper role.

1. **Ordinary distance:** throw with clear sight.
2. **Greater distance:** move the line back within the equipment's safe range.
3. **Impaired sight:** safest default is a facilitator-only demonstration using blurred glasses. Do not blindfold participants who are throwing.

### 3. Notice — 5 minutes

- What changed across the rounds?
- How did distance or impaired sight affect confidence and control?
- What did scoring make you feel or do?

### 4. Interpret — 10–15 minutes

1. In archery, what does it mean to miss the mark?
2. What might the bullseye represent—and where does that analogy break down?
3. How might distance picture alienation from God?
4. How might impaired sight picture darkness, deception, or distorted desire?
5. Why can diagnosis without grace become condemnation?

Read Romans 3:23, 1 John 1:8–9, John 3:17, and Psalm 34:18. Emphasize that sin is universal and serious, yet Jesus comes to save rather than to create a spiritual ranking system.

## Integration

Invite participants to name privately one area where they need truthful confession and one way they need to receive grace. Close with confession, assurance, and prayer.

## Safety and accessibility

- Use only soft magnetic or hook-and-loop equipment.
- Keep all people behind the throwing line.
- Retrieve darts only after a round is complete.
- Do not blindfold a person who is walking or throwing.
- Offer seated throwing, a closer line, scorekeeping, or observation.
- Avoid public comparison of scores during the theological debrief.

## Limits

Sin is richer than inaccurate aim: it includes rebellion, distorted desire, guilt, bondage, corrupt powers, and broken relationship. Relationship with God does not mechanically make moral success easy, and people close to God still sin. Name these limits explicitly.
