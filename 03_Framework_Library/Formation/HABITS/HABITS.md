# HABITS

## Purpose

HABITS is a framework for the character of a faithful Christian knower. It asks not only whether something is true or how we know it, but who we are becoming as we seek, receive, test, embody, and share truth.

**HABITS** stands for:

- **H — Humility**
- **A — Attention**
- **B — Bias Awareness**
- **I — Integrity**
- **T — Teachability**
- **S — Stewardship of Truth**

## Core insight

Knowing is never merely cognitive. Pride, distraction, self-deception, disobedience, defensiveness, and irresponsible speech can distort how truth is perceived and used. Christian knowing therefore requires intellectual virtues formed before God, Scripture, other people, and the world.

HABITS is a formation loop:

1. **Humility** opens us to reality.
2. **Attention** directs what we perceive and love.
3. **Bias Awareness** exposes distortion and self-deception.
4. **Integrity** aligns knowing, being, speaking, and doing.
5. **Teachability** keeps understanding open to correction and growth.
6. **Stewardship of Truth** directs knowledge toward love, courage, and responsibility.

## H — Humility

**Question:** Am I approaching truth as a creature accountable to God and capable of being wrong?

Humility resists the illusion of self-sufficiency. It does not reject knowledge; it makes truthful knowing possible by acknowledging dependence, limitation, and the need for grace and community.

### Biblical roots

- **Proverbs 11:2** — wisdom accompanies humility.
- **1 Corinthians 8:1–2** — imagined mastery may reveal that we do not yet know as we ought.
- **James 4:6** — God opposes the proud and gives grace to the humble.

### Practices

- Name what is known, inferred, assumed, and still uncertain.
- Represent another view fairly before evaluating it.
- Pray for freedom from the need to appear correct.

## A — Attention

**Question:** What am I attending to, and how is that attention shaping what I see and love?

Attention is a moral and spiritual act. What people repeatedly notice, ignore, contemplate, and fix their eyes upon shapes perception, desire, and action.

### Biblical roots

- **Proverbs 4:25–27** — direct the eyes and consider the path.
- **Philippians 4:8** — dwell upon what is true, honorable, just, pure, lovely, and commendable.
- **Hebrews 12:1–2** — run faithfully by fixing the eyes on Jesus.

### Practices

- Slow down before interpreting or reacting.
- Notice what the text, person, or situation actually presents.
- Examine what algorithms, habits, fears, and desires are directing attention.

## B — Bias Awareness

**Question:** What interests, wounds, loyalties, assumptions, or social pressures may be distorting my judgment?

Scripture does not portray people as neutral observers. We are interested interpreters capable of self-deception, premature judgment, selective attention, and self-justification.

### Biblical roots

- **Jeremiah 17:9** — the human heart is deeply deceptive.
- **Proverbs 18:17** — the first case sounds right until it is examined.
- **Matthew 7:3–5** — self-examination precedes clear judgment of another.

### Practices

- Ask what evidence would change the conclusion.
- Seek witnesses and sources that challenge the preferred account.
- Invite trusted people to name blind spots and conflicts of interest.

## I — Integrity

**Question:** Are my claims, character, speech, and actions aligned?

Biblical truth is embodied. Integrity refuses the separation of knowing from obedience or public claims from private practice. What we do with truth affects how clearly we receive it.

### Biblical roots

- **Psalm 15:1–2** — the one who dwells with God walks blamelessly and speaks truth from the heart.
- **John 7:17** — willingness to do God's will is joined to discerning Jesus' teaching.
- **James 1:22** — hearing without doing becomes self-deception.

### Practices

- Check whether conduct contradicts the truth being taught.
- Correct errors openly and proportionately.
- Refuse selective evidence, misleading rhetoric, and hidden manipulation.

## T — Teachability

**Question:** Can I receive correction, test what I hear, and continue growing?

Teachability receives truth without becoming gullible. It joins openness, discernment, discipline, and learning in community.

### Biblical roots

- **Proverbs 9:9** — the wise become wiser through instruction.
- **Proverbs 12:1** — love of discipline is love of knowledge.
- **Acts 17:11** — the Bereans receive the message eagerly and examine Scripture carefully.

### Practices

- Ask for critique before defending a conclusion.
- Test teaching through Scripture, wise community, evidence, and fruit.
- Treat correction as an opportunity for faithfulness rather than humiliation.

## S — Stewardship of Truth

**Question:** How should this truth be handled, communicated, and entrusted for the good of others?

Truth is not merely possessed; it is entrusted. Faithful stewardship joins accuracy with love, courage with restraint, and clarity with responsibility.

### Biblical roots

- **Luke 12:48** — greater entrustment brings greater responsibility.
- **Ephesians 4:15** — truth is to be spoken in love toward maturity in Christ.
- **2 Timothy 2:15** — the worker seeks approval by rightly handling the word of truth.

### Practices

- Verify before repeating and distinguish fact from interpretation.
- Credit sources and preserve provenance.
- Consider audience, timing, confidentiality, power, and likely consequences.
- Speak difficult truth lovingly; do not conceal necessary truth through cowardice.

## Using HABITS

Use the six questions when reading Scripture, researching, teaching, making decisions, engaging disagreement, evaluating media, or using artificial intelligence:

| Movement | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| Humility | What are my limits, and where might I be wrong? |
| Attention | What am I noticing, neglecting, or being trained to see? |
| Bias Awareness | What may be distorting my judgment? |
| Integrity | Are knowing, speaking, being, and doing aligned? |
| Teachability | How will I receive, test, and respond to correction? |
| Stewardship of Truth | What faithful responsibility comes with this knowledge? |

HABITS can guide personal reflection, group discussion, source evaluation, facilitator preparation, or a final review before teaching and publication.

## Limits and failure modes

- HABITS forms the knower; it does not replace exegesis, evidence, logic, expertise, or the guidance of the Holy Spirit.
- Bias awareness can become endless suspicion or false equivalence; the goal remains truthful judgment.
- Humility must not become timidity about what is clear.
- Stewardship must not become control, secrecy, or using “truth” as a weapon.
- The framework should lead toward wisdom and love, not a performance of intellectual virtue.
