# Gospel Flow

## Purpose

Gospel Flow keeps Christian teaching and practice ordered by grace. It begins with God rather than the self and grounds action in gift and identity rather than anxiety or performance.

## Core insight

**Who God is → what God has done → who we are → what we do**

Imperatives become Christian when they flow from God's character, saving action, and the identity received in Christ.

## Components

### Who God is

Name the character, purposes, and presence of the Triune God disclosed in the text or situation.

### What God has done

Name God's acts in creation, covenant, Christ's life, death and resurrection, the gift of the Spirit, and the promised renewal of all things.

### Who we are

Name the identity, belonging, freedom, vocation, and hope given to God's people because of God's action.

### What we do

Name a concrete response of faith, repentance, love, obedience, justice, worship, service, or witness.

## Sequence or logic

The flow is asymmetric: human response depends on divine initiative. Response matters, but it does not become the ground of acceptance. In repeated use, obedient action also trains perception and helps believers recognize God's character more deeply.

## Biblical and theological grounding

- Exodus 20:1–3 — deliverance before command
- Romans 1–11 and 12–16 — mercy and identity before embodied response
- Ephesians 1–3 and 4–6 — God's work and the church's identity before its walk
- Colossians 3:1–17 — raised identity leading to renewed practices
- 1 John 4:7–19 — love because God first loved us

## Worked example

Teaching generosity:

1. **Who God is:** generous Father and faithful provider.
2. **What God has done:** given the Son and every spiritual blessing in Christ.
3. **Who we are:** recipients and stewards rather than owners defined by scarcity.
4. **What we do:** give freely, wisely, and joyfully for the good of others.

## Use cases

- Sermon, lesson, and curriculum design
- Gospel-centered counseling and reflection
- Testing whether an application has become moralistic
- Structuring testimony and evangelism

## Limits and failure modes

- Do not force every biblical passage into four equal sections.
- “Who we are” should remain communal as well as personal.
- The model does not replace close exegesis or the full biblical story.
- Application should be concrete without implying that obedience earns the preceding gifts.
