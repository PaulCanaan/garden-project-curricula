# SOAP

## Purpose

SOAP is a framework for reading, studying, and living out the Bible. It slows reading, locates a passage within its literary and redemptive context, and turns understanding toward embodied response and prayer.

## Components

### S — Scripture

- Read slowly and in context.
- Identify the literary form: narrative, poetry, wisdom, prophecy, Gospel, apocalyptic, or epistle.
- Mark key words, grammar, repetition, contrasts, structure, and connections.
- Read enough surrounding material to avoid isolating a verse.

### O — Observation

- What is happening—who, what, where, when, how, and why?
- What does the passage reveal about God, humanity, creation, sin, covenant, and God's relationship with his people?
- Where does this passage stand within the story from creation to new creation?
- What questions should remain open before interpretation or application?

### A — Application

- How is God shaping attention, desire, character, relationships, or vocation through this passage?
- What promise is to be trusted, truth received, sin confessed, practice begun, relationship repaired, or hope sustained?
- How can this truth be embedded in a realistic daily or communal rhythm?

### P — Prayer

Prayer is the faithful response of people addressed by God's Word.

- Praise God for who he is and what he has done.
- Confess and receive grace where the text exposes sin or distortion.
- Ask the Spirit for faith, obedience, wisdom, and hope in Christ.
- Intercede for others and the situations illuminated by the passage.

## Sequence or logic

**Attend to the text → understand what is there → respond in life → answer God in prayer**

Return to the text after prayer; the movements can deepen one another.

## Worked example

Reading John 15:1–17:

- **Scripture:** note repeated words such as abide, love, command, fruit, and joy.
- **Observation:** Jesus is the vine; disciples depend on him; fruit and love emerge from abiding.
- **Application:** replace a hurried devotional goal with ten quiet minutes of abiding and one concrete act of love.
- **Prayer:** thank Jesus for initiating friendship and ask the Spirit to make love fruitful.

## Limits and failure modes

- SOAP is not a substitute for historical context, language tools, commentaries, or communal interpretation.
- Observation and interpretation should not be confused.
- Application must flow from the passage rather than private association.
- Prayer is communion, not a technique for securing a preferred result.
